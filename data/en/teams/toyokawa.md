## intro

Toyokawa High School (豊川高校) is a private school in Toyokawa, a city in the east of Aichi Prefecture, in the region known as Higashi-Mikawa. Its football club was founded in 1958 and spent most of its history in the lower divisions of the Aichi prefectural league. That changed after a new head coach arrived in April 2023, when the team was in the third division. It won promotion that year and again in 2025, and in 2026 it plays in Aichi's top prefectural league. In June 2026 it won the Aichi qualifying tournament for the summer Inter-High for the first time, and on 14 November 2026 it beat Tokai Gakuen 2-0 in the final of the Aichi winter qualifying tournament. Toyokawa won both of Aichi's high-school titles in the same year and will play at the winter All Japan High School Soccer Tournament for the first time in 2026–27. Aichi's titles have usually gone to private schools from Nagoya, the prefectural capital, so a team from the east of the prefecture winning both is a change.

## style

The head coach holds the JFA S licence, the highest coaching qualification in Japan. He previously led Yamanashi Gakuin High School to the national winter title in 2020–21, in his second season there, and before that coached Kanagawa University, where he worked with the future Japan international ITO Junya. At Toyokawa he brought an attitude as much as a system: the team's motto is to be "the first penguin", the first to jump into the water, and mistakes are treated as data on the way to success. His assistant, who played in Brazil and runs the local junior club A.S. Laranja Toyokawa, is in charge of individual technique.

The team's play is built on speed and accuracy of perception, seeing the game and deciding quickly. The coach uses video analysis to share tactics with the players. Toyokawa defends in a compact block and counter-attacks, with dribblers from A.S. Laranja Toyokawa adding individual skill. In the 2026 winter qualifiers it kept clean sheets in four of its five matches.

## history

- **1958** — The football club is founded. For decades it moves between the second and fourth divisions of the Aichi prefectural league; its best result in the Inter-High qualifiers is a place in the last four.
- **2023** — The new head coach arrives on 1 April, with the team in Division 3. In his first season Toyokawa wins promotion to Division 2 and reaches the last eight of the Inter-High qualifiers.
- **2024** — Runners-up in the Aichi new-year tournament for first- and second-year players, and third in the Inter-High qualifiers. The first-year team wins its league.
- **2025** — Toyokawa wins Division 2 and is promoted to Division 1.
- **2026** — In the Inter-High qualifiers Toyokawa beats Nagoya High School 2-1, Shigakukan 3-0 in the semi-final and Toho 2-0 in the final on 6 June, the school's first prefectural title. At the national finals it loses 2-3 to Seiwa Gakuen High School in the first round. In August midfielder OSHITA Aoi signs for Shonan Bellmare from 2027, the first player from the boys' team to join a J.League club. In the winter qualifiers Toyokawa beats Meito 3-0, Aichi Sandai Mikawa 6-0 and Chukyodai Chukyo 2-0, draws 3-3 with Toho in the semi-final and wins the shoot-out 4-3, then beats Tokai Gakuen, Aichi's representatives the year before, 2-0 in the final on 14 November. At the national finals, which start on 28 December 2026, it plays Nagasaki Institute of Applied Science High School in the first round. The winner meets the winner of Yamanashi Gakuin High School, the head coach's former team, and Yanagigaura High School.

## model

For readers new to Japanese youth football: high-school teams and J.League academies play in the same national league pyramid. At the top is the U-18 Premier League (EAST and WEST), then nine regional Prince Leagues, then the prefectural leagues. Two high-school tournaments carry even more prestige than the league: the summer Inter-High and the winter championship, which is televised nationally and ends at the National Stadium in Tokyo in January. Each prefecture sends one or two teams to each tournament through its own qualifying competition.

- **A partnership with a local junior club.** Toyokawa works closely with A.S. Laranja Toyokawa, the junior club where SUGAWARA Yukinari, a member of Japan's squad at the 2026 World Cup, played from under-12 to under-15 level before joining Nagoya Grampus's academy. The junior club's president is also Toyokawa High School's assistant coach, so players coached in the same way from junior-high age can carry on at the school. The aim is to keep talented players from the east of Aichi playing at home, instead of leaving for clubs and schools in Nagoya.
- **A large squad.** The club has more than 170 players, the largest football squad in Aichi. It trains on an artificial pitch opened in April 2022 and has support from sports science and medical specialists.
- **Three years from the third division.** The head coach arrived with the team in Division 3 of the Aichi league in 2023. Three years later Toyokawa is in Division 1 and has won both of Aichi's high-school titles.

## alumni

The club's best-known product so far is still at school: **OSHITA Aoi** (midfielder) will join Shonan Bellmare in January 2027. He came through A.S. Laranja Toyokawa and has played for the Aichi selection since his first year. Toyokawa's girls' team has also produced a player for the WE League, Japan's professional women's league.

SUGAWARA Yukinari, who grew up in Toyokawa and played for A.S. Laranja Toyokawa, did not attend Toyokawa High School. No Toyokawa graduate was in Japan's squad for the 2026 World Cup.
