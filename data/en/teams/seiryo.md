## intro

Seiryo High School (星稜) is a private school in Kanazawa, the capital of Ishikawa Prefecture on the Sea of Japan coast, with an attached junior high school. The boys' football club was founded in 1967. For two decades Seiryo was the dominant team in Ishikawa and one of the best-known school sides in Japan. It qualified for the winter All Japan High School Soccer Tournament 17 years in a row, from 1999–2000 to 2015–16. It reached the final in 2013–14 and won the title in 2014–15, the first national championship for any school from Ishikawa. Its most famous former player is HONDA Keisuke, who scored at three World Cups. In 2026 the team plays in Prince League Hokushinetsu Division 1, where it was fourth after 16 matches with 6 wins, 7 draws and 3 defeats (25 points).

## style

Seiryo is a school team in a region where winters are long and snowy, and it has built its success on producing players over three years rather than on one-off star generations. Its sides are usually technically tidy and patient in possession, and they have a reputation for strong results in knockout football. The two winter finals of 2014 and 2015 showed both sides of this. In January 2014 Seiryo led Toyama Daiichi 2-0 in the final at the old National Stadium in Tokyo, but conceded twice late on and lost 2-3 after extra time. Twelve months later it went back to the final, fell behind 1-2 to Maebashi Ikuei, equalised, and won 4-2 after extra time. The current head coach is not named here, because no official English spelling of his name has been published.

## history

- **1967** — The boys' football club is founded.
- **1999–2000** — Seiryo begins a run of 17 consecutive appearances at the winter championship, which lasts until the 2015–16 tournament.
- **2004–05** — With HONDA Keisuke in the side, Seiryo becomes the first team from Ishikawa to reach the semi-finals of the winter championship. It beats Takigawa Daini High School 4-3, Naha Nishi 1-0 and Maebashi Shogyo 1-0, then draws 2-2 with Ichiritsu Funabashi in the semi-final and loses 4-5 on penalties.
- **2007** — Runners-up at the summer Inter-High, losing the final to Ichiritsu Funabashi.
- **2012–13** — A second winter semi-final, again lost on penalties.
- **2013–14** — Seiryo reaches the winter final for the first time. In the last final played at the old National Stadium, it leads Toyama Daiichi 2-0 but loses 2-3 after extra time.
- **2014–15** — Champions. In the final at Saitama Stadium, Seiryo leads Maebashi Ikuei 1-0 at half-time, falls 1-2 behind, equalises at 2-2 and wins 4-2 with two goals in extra time.
- **2015–16** — A third semi-final in four years at the winter championship.
- **2016** — The run of winter appearances ends when Hogakuen (鵬学園) beat Seiryo 1-0 in the Ishikawa final. Since then, Seiryo, Hogakuen and Kanazawa Gakuin University Affiliated High School have shared the prefecture's places at national tournaments.
- **2021** — Semi-finals of the Inter-High, held in Fukui. Seiryo loses 2-3 to Yonago Kita.
- **2024** — The winter championship comes immediately after the Noto Peninsula earthquake of 1 January 2024, which struck Ishikawa. On 2 January Seiryo play Ichiritsu Funabashi in the third round and lose after conceding four goals. The "friendship cheering" for the Ishikawa team in the stands, and the sportsmanship of their opponents, was widely reported.
- **2026** — In the Ishikawa Inter-High qualifiers in June, Seiryo reaches the semi-finals but is knocked out by Hogakuen: 1-1 after extra time, 4-5 on penalties. Seiryo does not play at the national Inter-High. In the league it is fourth in Prince League Hokushinetsu Division 1 after 16 rounds.

## model

For readers new to Japanese youth football: high-school teams and J.League academies play in the same national league pyramid. At the top are the U-18 Premier League (EAST and WEST), then nine regional Prince Leagues, then the prefectural leagues. Two high-school tournaments carry even more prestige than the league: the summer Inter-High and the winter championship, which is televised nationally and ends in January. Japanese school years run from April to March, so a winter championship is named after the year it starts (the "2014" tournament ended in January 2015). This page writes it as 2014–15.

- **A regional league regular.** Seiryo plays in Prince League Hokushinetsu Division 1, which covers Niigata, Toyama, Ishikawa, Fukui and Nagano. Its rivals there include Toyama Daiichi High School, Matsumoto Kokusai, Nihon Bunri and the youth teams of J.League clubs such as Albirex Niigata, Kataller Toyama and Matsumoto Yamaga.
- **One of the strongest school sides in the snow belt.** Ishikawa has long, snowy winters, which makes year-round outdoor training harder. Seiryo's success, and that of nearby Toyama Daiichi, showed that Hokuriku schools could beat the established powers from Shizuoka, Chiba and Kyushu.
- **Junior-high links.** The attached Seiryo Junior High School has its own football team, and some of its players move up to the high-school squad. The club is large: the school reported more than 110 boys across the three year groups in 2023.
- **Players from beyond the prefecture.** Seiryo also recruits from outside Ishikawa. HARADA Wataru, for example, came from the Vissel Kobe under-15 team.
- **Producing players by different routes.** Seiryo players have gone straight to J.League clubs (HONDA Keisuke, TOYODA Yohei and SUZUKI Daisuke) and via university (HARADA Wataru).

## alumni

- **HONDA Keisuke** (midfielder) — the leading figure in Seiryo's run to the 2004–05 winter semi-finals. He joined Nagoya Grampus straight from school in 2005, then played for VVV-Venlo, CSKA Moscow and AC Milan, among others. He scored at the 2010, 2014 and 2018 World Cups, one of very few Asian players to score at three World Cups.
- **TOYODA Yohei** (forward, retired) — a year older than Honda at Seiryo, he became one of the J.League's best-known strikers and a Japan international, above all with Sagan Tosu. After retiring as a player he was appointed president of Sagan Dreams, the company that runs Sagan Tosu, in July 2026.
- **SUZUKI Daisuke** (defender, JEF United Chiba) — joined Albirex Niigata from the school, where he had already been registered as a special designated player, then played for Kashiwa Reysol, Gimnàstic de Tarragona in Spain and Urawa Reds. He was called up to the Japan squad in 2014. He has captained JEF United Chiba every season since joining in 2021, including the 2025 promotion that returned the club to J1 after 17 years.
- **HARADA Wataru** (defender, Kashiwa Reysol) — joined Seiryo from the Vissel Kobe under-15 team and went on to Nippon Sport Science University. He turned professional with FC Imabari, moved to Sagan Tosu in 2022 and joined Kashiwa Reysol on a permanent transfer for the 2025 season. He is still in Kashiwa's squad for 2026–27.
