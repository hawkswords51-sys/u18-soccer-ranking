## intro

Rissho Shonan High School (立正大学淞南高校, usually shortened to "Rissho Shonan" or simply "Shonan") is a private school in Matsue, the capital of Shimane Prefecture, on the Sea of Japan coast of western Honshu. Shimane is one of Japan's least populous prefectures and has no J.League club of its own, yet Rissho Shonan has become one of the strongest football schools in western Japan. The football club was started in the late 1980s by a first-year pupil, OKANO Masayuki, who later scored the goal that sent Japan to its first World Cup. Since its first national appearance in 1996, the team has played 20 times at the winter All Japan High School Soccer Tournament, reaching the semi-finals in 2010–11. It has played 19 times at the summer Inter-High and reached the semi-finals three times. In 2026 it plays in the Prince League Chugoku and lies second after 14 matches.

## style

The club describes its football as fast, direct attacking through the centre of the pitch: a "high-speed counter-attack that goes forward quickly through the middle", combined with well-rehearsed set pieces. That makes Rissho Shonan unusual in modern Japanese youth football, where most strong teams prefer to build patiently and attack down the flanks. Defending matters just as much. When the team won its 20th Shimane title in the winter qualifiers of 2023, it scored 12 goals and conceded none across the prefectural rounds. The 2026 squad is a typical example. It has scored freely in the Prince League Chugoku, with 10 wins from 14 matches, but at the Inter-High it was held 1-1 by Oita in the first round and went out 6-7 on penalties.

## history

- **Late 1980s** — The school, then called Matsue Nihon University High School, has no football club. OKANO Masayuki, a first-year pupil who arrived in 1988, starts one himself and acts as both captain and coach. By the time he leaves, the new team has finished as high as third in Shimane.
- **1990s** — A young head coach takes charge and turns the club into a serious force. The school was renamed Shonan Gakuen High School in 1992 and took its present name in 2001.
- **1996–97** — The club makes its first appearance at the winter championship, the 75th edition, and soon becomes a regular at the national finals.
- **2010–11** — The best winter run so far. At the 89th championship the team beats Niigata Nishi 5-1 in the third round and goes on to the semi-finals, where it finishes joint third (Japan does not play a third-place match). Midfielder KATO Taiki, later a J.League player, is a third-year in this team. In the same year the team reaches the last 16 of the Takamado Cup All Japan Youth Championship.
- **2011** — Rissho Shonan is one of the ten teams in the first season of the U-18 Premier League WEST, alongside J.League academies such as Sanfrecce Hiroshima and Nagoya Grampus. The step up is too large: the team wins one match out of 18, finishes last with five points and is relegated to the Prince League Chugoku. In the summer, though, it reaches the Inter-High semi-finals.
- **2012** — A second consecutive Inter-High semi-final.
- **2014** — Champions of the Prince League Chugoku. The team goes to the year-end play-off for a Premier League place but does not win promotion.
- **2015** — A third Inter-High semi-final.
- **2023** — The long-serving head coach, who had led the club for about 30 years, becomes general manager ("sō-kantoku"). His assistant of more than two decades takes over as head coach and, in his first season, wins both Shimane qualifying tournaments. The winter title is the club's 20th.
- **2024–25** — Other Shimane schools win the winter qualifiers. In June 2025 the school's operating body signs a partnership with Vissel Kobe and launches an under-15 team, Vissel Kobe's first junior-youth side outside Hyogo Prefecture.
- **2026** — The club qualifies for its 19th Inter-High but is knocked out in the first round by Oita on penalties after a 1-1 draw. In the Prince League Chugoku it is second after 14 matches, with 10 wins, one draw and three defeats (31 points).

## model

For readers new to Japanese youth football: high-school teams and J.League academies play in the same national league pyramid. At the top are the U-18 Premier League (EAST and WEST), then nine regional Prince Leagues, then the prefectural leagues. The Prince League Chugoku covers the five prefectures of western Honshu: Tottori, Shimane, Okayama, Hiroshima and Yamaguchi. Two high-school tournaments carry even more prestige than the league. The summer Inter-High is a short, intense tournament played in the heat. The winter championship is televised nationally, and its later rounds are played in the Tokyo area around New Year. Japanese school years run from April to March, so a "2010" team plays its winter tournament in January 2011.

- **A national team from a small prefecture.** Shimane has a small population and no J.League club, so Rissho Shonan recruits widely. Many of its players come from junior club teams outside the prefecture, especially in the Kansai region around Osaka and Kobe. Among the alumni below, HAYASHI Naoki, MATSUDA Riki, KATO Taiki and SAKAI Yuhi all came from junior clubs in Osaka, Nara or Hyogo.
- **A home for late developers.** Few of the club's professionals were famous at 15. Most went from the school to university football, at Biwako Seikei Sport College, Fukuoka University or Osaka University of Health and Sport Sciences, and joined J.League clubs after graduating.
- **A link with Vissel Kobe.** Since 2025 the school's operating body has worked with Vissel Kobe. The partnership created an under-15 team in Matsue and gives the club access to the J.League club's coaching programmes, a rare arrangement for a school side.

## alumni

- **OKANO Masayuki** (forward, retired) — founded the school's football club as a pupil in the late 1980s. After Nihon University he joined Urawa Red Diamonds in 1994 and also played for Vissel Kobe and Gainare Tottori. In November 1997 he scored the extra-time golden goal against Iran in Johor Bahru, Malaysia, that took Japan to its first World Cup. After retiring he worked as general manager of Gainare Tottori.
- **MATSUDA Riki** (forward, Kataller Toyama) — went from the school to Biwako Seikei Sport College and then to Nagoya Grampus. He has since played for JEF United Chiba, Avispa Fukuoka, Ventforet Kofu, Cerezo Osaka and Ehime FC, and wears number 10 for Toyama in 2026. His twin brother, the full-back MATSUDA Riku, also came through Rissho Shonan and Biwako Seikei, and played for FC Tokyo, Cerezo Osaka and Gamba Osaka.
- **HAYASHI Naoki** (defender, Tokyo Verdy) — joined Kashima Antlers from Osaka University of Health and Sport Sciences. He has moved between Kashima and Tokyo Verdy, and he is a Verdy player in 2026.
- **INOUE Kenta** (midfielder, Shimizu S-Pulse) — a quick wide attacker who went to Fukuoka University before turning professional with Oita Trinita. He then moved to Yokohama F. Marinos and now plays for Shimizu.
- **KATO Taiki** (midfielder, Zweigen Kanazawa) — a member of the 2010–11 team that reached the winter semi-finals. After Biwako Seikei Sport College he played for SP Kyoto FC and Renofa Yamaguchi, then for Kanazawa and Montedio Yamagata, and he is now back at Kanazawa.
- **INABA Shuto** (midfielder, Kagoshima United FC) — a year below KATO at the school. After Fukuoka University he began his career abroad with Albirex Niigata Singapore. He then played for Kataller Toyama, Blaublitz Akita and FC Machida Zelvia, and is now in his second spell at Kagoshima.
- **SAKAI Yuhi** (defender, Fukuoka University / Avispa Fukuoka) — who left the school in 2023 and now plays for Fukuoka University. In 2026 Avispa Fukuoka announced that he will join the club for the 2027 season, and he is already registered on Avispa's squad while still a student.

Current players aren't named on this page because none of them has an English spelling published by the JFA, the J.League or a club. The same applies to the coaching staff.
