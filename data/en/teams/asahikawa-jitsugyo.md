## intro

Asahikawa Jitsugyo High School — 旭川実業, often shortened to 旭実 — is a private school in Asahikawa, the main city of northern-central Hokkaido. Its football club was founded in 1966. Since 2011 it has been one of the strongest teams in Japan's northernmost prefecture. It has won Prince League Hokkaido five times and twice earned a place in the national U-18 Premier League, the top tier of Japanese youth football, in 2012 and 2023. Its best results in the two great high-school tournaments are the quarter-finals of the summer Inter-High, in 2017 and again in 2026, and the last 16 of the winter championship in 2012–13. In 2026 the team plays in Prince League Hokkaido. In the summer it reached the Inter-High quarter-finals, where it led the eventual champions Shizuoka Gakuen High School 2-0 before losing 3-2.

## style

Asahikawa is one of the coldest cities in Japan — its weather station recorded −41.0°C in January 1902 — and from late autumn to spring the city's pitches are usually under snow. Teams from this part of Hokkaido lose months of outdoor training every year that schools in Tokyo or Kyushu take for granted. Asahikawa Jitsugyo's results show how far a northern school can get anyway. Its 2026 Inter-High run was full of goals: ten scored and seven conceded in four matches. It beat Osaka Toin 5-2 in the first round, got past Matsumoto Kokusai 8-7 on penalties after a 1-1 draw, and came from behind to beat Yaita Chuo 2-1. In the quarter-final against Shizuoka Gakuen High School it scored twice in the first half; Shizuoka Gakuen came back with three goals after the break, the winner in stoppage time, and went on to win the title.

## history

- **1966** — The football club is founded.
- **2006** — Runners-up in Prince League Hokkaido.
- **2011** — The breakthrough year. The team wins Prince League Hokkaido and both Hokkaido qualifying tournaments, for the summer Inter-High and the winter championship. It then wins both of its matches in the Premier League promotion play-off.
- **2012** — A first season in the Premier League EAST, against teams such as Aomori Yamada, Shizuoka Gakuen High School and Hokkaido Consadole Sapporo U-18. It is a hard year: one point from 18 matches, last place and relegation. In the winter championship that season (2012–13) the team gets through two rounds on penalties after goalless draws, the first against Ohzu High School, and goes out in the third round on penalties after a 1-1 draw — its best run in the competition.
- **2015, 2018, 2020** — Prince League Hokkaido champions three more times. The 2020 season was shortened by the COVID-19 pandemic.
- **2017** — Qualifies for the Inter-High as Hokkaido's second team and reaches the quarter-finals, the school's best national result until then.
- **2021–2024** — Qualifies for the Inter-High four years in a row. In 2023 it reaches the last 16 and loses 2-0 to Ichiritsu Funabashi.
- **2022** — Wins Prince League Hokkaido again. In the promotion play-off final in December it draws 0-0 with Kyoto Sanga F.C. U-18 over 110 minutes and wins the shoot-out 5-4, returning to the Premier League after 11 years.
- **2023** — A second Premier League EAST season. The team finishes 12th of 12 and is relegated after one year.
- **2026** — After missing the 2025 Inter-High, the team wins the Hokkaido qualifier — 3-2 against Sapporo Otani High School in the semi-final and 1-0 in the final — for its tenth Inter-High appearance, and then equals the school's best result by reaching the quarter-finals. In Prince League Hokkaido it goes into round 13 one point behind the leaders; a 1-0 defeat by Hokkaido Consadole Sapporo U-18 on 26 September gives Sapporo the title.

## model

For readers new to Japanese youth football: high-school teams and J.League academies play in the same national league pyramid. At the top are the U-18 Premier League (EAST and WEST), then nine regional Prince Leagues, then the prefectural leagues. Two high-school tournaments carry even more prestige than the league: the summer Inter-High and the winter championship, which is televised nationally and ends at the National Stadium in January.

- **Football in the far north.** Hokkaido is large and sparsely populated, its season is short and its winters are long. Teams travel great distances within the prefecture, and the national tournaments are usually held far to the south. A team from Asahikawa playing in the Premier League against clubs from Tokyo and Shizuoka is unusual, and the school has done it twice.
- **Hokkaido's school-team standard-bearer.** Prince League Hokkaido is shared with Hokkaido Consadole Sapporo U-18, the academy of the region's J.League club, and with strong Sapporo schools such as Sapporo Otani High School. Since 2011 the title has gone to Asahikawa Jitsugyo five times.
- **Two places at the Inter-High.** Hokkaido normally sends two teams to the Inter-High, so the team can qualify by finishing first or second in the Hokkaido tournament, as it did in 2017. In the winter championship Hokkaido has only one place, which is why the school has seven winter appearances against ten summer ones.
- **A big squad.** The club has well over 100 players and runs several teams, so the second and third squads play in the lower levels of the league pyramid as well.

## alumni

- **TAKAHASHI Kensuke** (futsal; head coach) — went to the school from Asahikawa and became one of Japan's leading futsal players. He played in Spain for Caja Segovia and Guadalajara, and represented Japan at the 2004 and 2012 FIFA Futsal World Cups. After coaching Bardral Urayasu and the Indonesia national futsal teams, he was appointed head coach of the Japan national futsal team in July 2024 and led Japan at the AFC Futsal Asian Cup in early 2026.
- **YAMAUCHI Riku** (midfielder, Tegevajaro Miyazaki) — went on to Sapporo University, then played for Vanraure Hachinohe and Thespa Gunma before joining Tegevajaro Miyazaki for 2026. Miyazaki play in J2 in the 2026/27 season.
- **KAWAI Toshiki** (midfielder, Tegevajaro Miyazaki) — came through Hokkaido Consadole Sapporo's U-15 team, then went to the school and to Sapporo University. He joined Miyazaki in 2025.
- **ENDO Masayoshi** (midfielder, Kagoshima United FC) — from eastern Hokkaido. He went to Toin University of Yokohama after the school and has played for Kagoshima United since 2022.

Two earlier players went straight from the school into the J.League: a full-back who joined Bellmare Hiratsuka in 1999, and a goalkeeper who joined Yokohama F. Marinos in 2009. They are not named here because no English spelling of their names has been published by the JFA, the J.League or a club.
