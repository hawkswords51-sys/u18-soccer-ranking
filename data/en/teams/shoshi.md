## intro

Shoshi High School (尚志高校, usually just "Shoshi") is a private school in Koriyama, in the middle of Fukushima Prefecture. Its football club was founded in 1997 and has had the same head coach, NAKAMURA Koji, since 1998. In less than thirty years it has become the dominant team in Fukushima and one of the strongest school sides in the Tohoku region of north-eastern Japan. It has reached the semi-finals of the winter All Japan High School Soccer Tournament three times: in 2011–12, the season of the Great East Japan Earthquake, in 2018–19 and in 2025–26. In 2025 it was also a semi-finalist at the summer Inter-High and champion of Prince League Tohoku. It has played four seasons in the national U-18 Premier League. In 2026 the first team plays in Prince League Tohoku, where it is top of the table, and a second team plays in the Fukushima prefectural league. At the 2026 Inter-High, held in Fukushima, Shoshi went out in its first match, losing 1-2 to Yaita Chuo High School in the second round.

## style

NAKAMURA, who played at Narashino High School and Juntendo University before coaching, has built Shoshi around technique and passing rather than physical power. The team likes to keep the ball, play short combinations and break defences with quick movement in the final third. Before the 2025–26 winter semi-final he described the club's direction plainly: the basics of receiving and passing have to be done thoroughly, and the players had been working on them in early-morning sessions. He has also said he took training ideas from Fukushima United, the prefecture's professional club, to help the team play its attacking football against the strongest opponents.

The 2025 season showed both sides of the style. In Prince League Tohoku the team finished first. At the Inter-High it led Kamimura Gakuen 1-0 at half-time in the semi-final, then lost control of the ball after the break and was beaten 1-2 by a direct free kick in stoppage time. NAKAMURA called the performance a beating, whatever the score said. Five months later, in the winter semi-final against the same opponents at the National Stadium in Tokyo, Shoshi drew 1-1 and lost only in the tenth round of the penalty shoot-out, 8-9.

In 2026 the team has been the most consistent side in Prince League Tohoku. After 15 of 18 rounds it had won 13, drawn one and lost one, with 45 goals scored and eight conceded. Its Inter-High ended early: against Yaita Chuo it had many more shots, but conceded a winner in stoppage time.

## history

- **1997** — The football club is founded at Shoshi High School in Koriyama.
- **1998** — NAKAMURA Koji becomes head coach. He is still in charge almost three decades later, and is also listed by the club as an adviser to its wider youth organisation.
- **2011** — Shoshi is one of the founding members of the national U-18 Premier League, but is relegated to Prince League Tohoku after one season.
- **2011–12** — Months after the earthquake, tsunami and nuclear accident of March 2011, Shoshi carries the hopes of a damaged prefecture at the 90th winter championship. It beats Kiryu Daiichi 3-1 in the quarter-final to become the first team from Fukushima to reach the last four, then loses the semi-final to Yokkaichi Chuo Kogyo.
- **2018–19** — A second winter semi-final, played at Saitama Stadium 2002. Forward SOMENO Itsuki scores a hat-trick, but Aomori Yamada draws 3-3 and wins the penalty shoot-out 4-2 on its way to the title.
- **2019** — Back in the Premier League EAST, Shoshi finishes tenth and is relegated. In the summer it reaches the Inter-High semi-finals for the first time, held in Okinawa, beating Hatsushiba Hashimoto 4-2 in the quarter-final before losing 0-1 to Toyama Daiichi High School.
- **2023** — Promoted to the Premier League again, Shoshi finishes second in the EAST division, its best league season.
- **2024** — Relegated from the Premier League. At the Fukushima Inter-High qualifiers it loses the final on penalties to Teikyo Asaka, but still qualifies as runner-up, since Fukushima has hosted the tournament and had two places since 2024.
- **2025** — The club's most successful year. Shoshi wins Prince League Tohoku and reaches the Inter-High semi-finals, losing 1-2 to Kamimura Gakuen. In the Premier League promotion play-off in Hiroshima in December it beats Okayama Gakugeikan 4-0 but loses the deciding match 1-2 to Higashiyama. In the winter championship it beats Teikyo Nagaoka 1-0 in the quarter-final to reach the semi-finals for the third time, then loses to Kamimura Gakuen on penalties at the National Stadium.
- **2026** — Shoshi wins the Fukushima Inter-High qualifiers, beating Teikyo Asaka 9-8 on penalties after a 2-2 draw in the final, for its 16th consecutive Inter-High and 18th in all. At the tournament it loses its first match 1-2 to Yaita Chuo High School. In the league, it leads Prince League Tohoku after 15 rounds, two points ahead of Aomori Yamada's second team, chasing a return to the Premier League.

## model

For readers new to Japanese youth football: high-school teams and J.League academies play in the same national league pyramid. At the top is the U-18 Premier League (EAST and WEST), then nine regional Prince Leagues, then the prefectural leagues. Promotion to the Premier League is decided by a play-off in December between the leading Prince League teams. Two high-school tournaments carry even more prestige than the league: the summer Inter-High and the winter championship, which is televised nationally and ends at the National Stadium in January. Because the Japanese school year begins in April, a winter tournament is dated by the season in which it starts (2025–26 is the "2025" edition).

- **One coach, one idea.** Shoshi is unusual even in Japan for having had the same head coach for almost its entire history. The technical, passing style has stayed the same through four Premier League seasons and three relegations.
- **A club, not just a team.** The school runs SHOSHI FC as a family of teams: the high-school boys' and girls' teams, an under-15 junior-youth team, an under-12 team, a football school and a goalkeeper school. Players can come through the club's own teams before reaching the high-school squad.
- **Depth across the pyramid.** With a first team in the Prince League and a second team in the Fukushima league, Shoshi gives a large squad regular competitive football. In 2026 the second team was also top of its division.
- **A national recruitment base.** The squad mixes players from Fukushima and the rest of Tohoku with others from junior-youth teams across eastern Japan, including J.League academies in the Kanto region.
- **Producing players by several routes.** Alumni have gone straight to J.League clubs (SOMENO Itsuki) or turned professional after university (YAMAGISHI Yuya, KASE Naoki, MATSUMOTO Yuma).

## alumni

- **YAMAGISHI Yuya** (forward, Nagoya Grampus) — went from Shoshi to Ryutsu Keizai University, then turned professional and played for Thespa Gunma, FC Gifu, Montedio Yamagata and Avispa Fukuoka before moving to Nagoya, where he plays in J1 and wears number 11.
- **SOMENO Itsuki** (forward, Tokyo Verdy) — joined Shoshi from Kashima Antlers' junior-youth team in Tsukuba. His hat-trick against Aomori Yamada in the 2018–19 winter semi-final is the most famous individual performance in the club's history. He joined Kashima Antlers straight from the school and has since had several spells at Tokyo Verdy, where he now wears number 9 in J1.
- **KASE Naoki** (midfielder, Shonan Bellmare) — went from Shoshi to Ryutsu Keizai University, turned professional with Iwaki FC, and joined Shonan Bellmare for 2026.
- **MATSUMOTO Yuma** (midfielder, Tegevajaro Miyazaki) — went from Shoshi to Niigata University of Health and Welfare, then played for Kataller Toyama and FC Imabari. He now plays for Tegevajaro Miyazaki in J2.

## squad

- **HOSHI Sosuke** (midfielder, third year) — a Japan U-17 international. He was one of four high-school players in the squad that won the 2026 AFC U17 Asian Cup in Saudi Arabia, beating China 3-2 in the final in May, and was called up again for Japan's U-17 tour of France in September 2026. As a second-year he played in defence and midfield in the 2025 promotion play-off and the winter championship.
