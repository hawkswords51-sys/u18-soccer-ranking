## intro

JEF United Chiba U-18 is the under-18 team of JEF United Ichihara Chiba, one of the ten founding clubs of the J.League and, since December 2025, back in the top flight after seventeen years away. The academy has a longer national record than its recent league placings suggest: it reached the final of the Japan Club Youth (U-18) Championship in 1998 and the final of the J Youth Cup in 1998 and 2003, and it has produced a World Cup midfielder in ABE Yuki and one of the most prolific strikers in J1 history in SATO Hisato. The last few seasons have been a rebuild. In 2023 the team was playing in the Chiba prefectural league; two promotions in two years took it back to the Prince League Kanto Division 1, where it finished fourth in 2025 and, after twelve matches of the 2026 season, sits second.

It has also shown it can run up a score — an 8-0 win over Ventforet Kofu U-18 in May — and its best players are now being trusted by the first team: two current third-years hold first-team squad numbers for the 2026-27 season. Two national competitions run alongside the league for club academies in Japan. The Japan Club Youth (U-18) Championship is a summer tournament for club teams only; the J Youth Cup is played in the autumn and winter between the academies of J.League clubs. Neither has a third-place match, so both beaten semi-finalists are listed as "third". JEF lost the 1998 Club Youth final to Gamba Osaka Youth, the 1998 J Youth Cup final to Kashima Antlers Youth, and the 2003 J Youth Cup final to Sanfrecce Hiroshima Youth. Club academies such as JEF's do not enter the high-school competitions — the summer Inter-High and the winter All Japan High School Championship — which are for school teams only.

## history

- **1946** — Furukawa Electric's works team is founded. It becomes JEF United Ichihara and joins the J.League as one of its original ten clubs in 1993; the club now plays as JEF United Ichihara Chiba, at Fukuda Denshi Arena in Chiba City.
- **1994–1996** — The youth team (then "JEF United Ichihara Youth") reaches the semi-finals of the J Youth Cup in 1994 and 1996, and of the Club Youth Championship in 1996.
- **1998** — Two national finals in one year: runners-up in the Club Youth Championship and in the J Youth Cup. ABE Yuki makes his J.League debut for the first team the same year, while still of youth-team age.
- **2001–2003** — Three more deep J Youth Cup runs: semi-finals in 2001 and 2002, and the final in 2003.
- **2007, 2015** — Semi-finals of the Club Youth Championship.
- **2011** — The national Premier League and the regional Prince Leagues are created. JEF finish fourth in the first Prince League Kanto Division 1 season, but later slip down the tiers: by 2023 the team is playing in the Chiba prefectural league.
- **2023** — Champions of the Chiba prefectural Division 1, then winners of the December play-off for a place in the Prince League Kanto Division 2.
- **2024** — Champions of the Prince League Kanto Division 2 at the first attempt, five points clear of Yamanashi Gakuin High School.
- **2025** — Fourth in the Prince League Kanto Division 1. In October HIMENO Makoto signs a professional contract while still of youth-team age, and in December he scores in the J1 promotion play-off semi-final as the first team wins promotion.
- **2026** — After twelve rounds the team is second in the Prince League Kanto Division 1 with 7 wins, 1 draw and 4 losses (22 points), behind an unbeaten Ichiritsu Funabashi.

## model

- **Where JEF sits in the pyramid.** Japanese youth football is organised in tiers: the national Premier League (EAST and WEST) at the top, then nine regional Prince Leagues, then the prefectural leagues. Kanto's Prince League has two divisions, which makes JEF's route — prefectural league in 2023, Division 2 in 2024, Division 1 since 2025 — two promotions in two seasons. The leading teams in each Prince League go into a play-off in December for promotion to the Premier League; JEF's remaining 2026 fixtures include Ventforet Kofu U-18, the league leaders Ichiritsu Funabashi and Omiya Ardija U18.
- **Rivals on the doorstep.** Chiba is one of the strongest football prefectures in Japan for school teams — RKU Kashiwa and Ichiritsu Funabashi among them — and JEF compete with them for the same players. Both appear in JEF's 2026 league programme (RKU Kashiwa through its B team).
- **From the club's own junior teams, and from outside.** Most of the academy's professionals came up through the club's under-15 team. Others join at sixteen from local junior-youth clubs: SAITO Shunji, now with a first-team number, came to the under-18s from a Chiba junior-youth club.
- **University as a second route.** Several graduates who were not offered a contract at eighteen went to university and came back: KUBONIWA Ryota (Rikkyo University) and TORIUMI Koji (Meiji University) are both in the current first-team squad.
- **School year.** Japanese school years run from April to March, so a "third-year" in 2026 was born between April 2008 and March 2009. The league season follows the school year rather than the European calendar.

## alumni

- **ABE Yuki** (midfielder, retired) — the academy's most decorated graduate. He came through JEF Ichihara's youth team, made his first-team debut in 1998, and later played for Urawa Red Diamonds and Leicester City. He played in all four of Japan's matches at the 2010 World Cup in South Africa. Since retiring in 2021 he has coached in Urawa's academy and is head coach of Urawa Red Diamonds Youth.
- **SATO Hisato** (forward, retired) — in JEF Ichihara's youth system from 1994 to 1999, then a professional at JEF. He spent twelve seasons at Sanfrecce Hiroshima, equalled the J1 all-time scoring record in 2015, won 31 caps for Japan, and finished his career back at JEF in 2020.
- **IDE Haruya** (midfielder, Vissel Kobe) — JEF under-15 and under-18, then the first team, Gamba Osaka, Montedio Yamagata and Tokyo Verdy before joining Kobe.
- **SAKURAGAWA Solomon** (forward, Cerezo Osaka) — JEF under-15 and under-18, then JEF's first team, a loan at Fagiano Okayama and a spell at Yokohama FC; he joined Cerezo Osaka in January 2026.
- **OKANO Jun** (defender, Blaublitz Akita) — JEF under-15 and under-18; a centre-back who has also played for Oita Trinita, FC Machida Zelvia and V-Varen Nagasaki.
- **TORIUMI Koji** (defender, JEF United Chiba) — JEF under-15 and under-18, then Meiji University; back at JEF after a spell at Cerezo Osaka.
- **KUBONIWA Ryota** (defender, JEF United Chiba) — JEF under-15 and under-18, then Rikkyo University, before returning to the club as a professional.
- **HIMENO Makoto** (midfielder, JEF United Chiba) — the most recent graduate. A member of the club's academy from under-12 level, he was registered with the first team while still in the under-18s in 2025, signed a professional contract that October, played for Japan at the 2025 FIFA U-17 World Cup, and scored in the J1 promotion play-off semi-final against Omiya Ardija.
