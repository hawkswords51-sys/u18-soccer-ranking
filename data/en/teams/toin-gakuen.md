## intro

Toin Gakuen High School — 桐蔭学園, usually just "Toin" — is a large private school in Aoba Ward, Yokohama, in Kanagawa Prefecture. It is best known in Japan as an academic school, and its football club has a long record of combining that with national-level results. Its biggest title is the 2011 Inter-High, the summer national high-school championship, which it won by beating Shizuoka Gakuen High School 2-1 in the final. In the winter All Japan High School Soccer Tournament it has 10 appearances and reached the semi-finals in the 2002–03 season. Former pupils include two members of Japan's 2002 World Cup squad, MORIOKA Ryuzo and TODA Kazuyuki, and HAYAKAWA Tomoki, the Kashima Antlers goalkeeper who was named the 2025 J.LEAGUE MVP. In 2026 the team plays in Prince League Kanto Division 1 and reached the quarter-finals of the Inter-High. Do not confuse it with Toko Gakuen High School, its neighbour and rival in Kanagawa, or with Osaka Toin High School.

## style

The 2026 summer showed a team that can win close games. In the knockout stage of the Kanagawa Inter-High qualifiers it won its first three matches by a single goal each, then lost the final 1-2 to Toko Gakuen High School but still qualified, because Kanagawa sends two schools. At the national finals it scored seven and conceded four in four matches: 3-2 against Okayama Gakugeikan, 2-1 against Omi and 2-0 against Kyushu Kokusai University High School, before losing 0-1 in the quarter-final to Ohzu High School, who went on to the semi-finals. In December 2025 the team also won the GO FOR WORLD CUP in Saitama, a winter invitational tournament, beating Shizuoka Gakuen High School on penalties in the final.

## history

- **1964** — Toin Gakuen opens in Yokohama. The football club becomes one of Kanagawa's leading teams; the founding year of the club itself is not published by an official source we could check.
- **1993** — A first Inter-High semi-final.
- **2002–03** — The club's best run in the winter championship: a semi-final at the 81st edition, the year Ichiritsu Funabashi won the title.
- **2004** — Quarter-finalists at the All Japan Youth (U-18) Championship, then the competition in which school teams and J.League academies played for one national U-18 title.
- **2005** — A second Inter-High semi-final.
- **2011** — National champions at the Inter-High in Akita. It was the first national final for both Toin and Shizuoka Gakuen High School, and Toin came from behind to win 2-1.
- **2017–18** — Back in the winter championship for the first time in 14 years, beaten on penalties in the second round.
- **2020–21** — A 10th winter-championship appearance.
- **2023** — Second in Prince League Kanto Division 2 with 10 wins, 6 draws and 2 losses, which earned promotion to Division 1. The club said it was the only Kanagawa high school to reach Division 1 that year.
- **2025** — Winners of the GO FOR WORLD CUP in Saitama invitational in December, beating Shizuoka Gakuen High School on penalties in the final.
- **2026** — A third straight season in Prince League Kanto Division 1, and a 12th Inter-High appearance, ending in the quarter-finals.

## model

For readers new to Japanese youth football: high-school teams and J.League academies play in the same national league pyramid. At the top are the U-18 Premier League (EAST and WEST), then nine regional Prince Leagues, then the prefectural leagues. Two high-school tournaments carry even more prestige than the league: the summer Inter-High and the winter championship, which is televised nationally and ends at the National Stadium in January.

- **Kanagawa is one of the hardest prefectures to qualify from.** Toin shares it with Toko Gakuen High School and several other strong schools, and the best local players are also recruited by the academies of Yokohama F. Marinos, Kawasaki Frontale, Shonan Bellmare and Yokohama FC. Kanagawa sends one school to the winter championship and, in 2026, two to the Inter-High.
- **An academic school.** Toin is known first as a university-preparation school, and the club has produced a long line of players who went on to university football before turning professional — HAYAKAWA Tomoki went via Meiji University, SHIRAWACHI Keita via Toin University of Yokohama.
- **Links to Toin University of Yokohama.** The university belongs to the same school group and has a strong football club. According to the high-school club's own staff page, the current head coach is also general director of the university's football club, so a player can stay in one coaching system from high school through university.
- **A record of producing coaches.** Two former pupils were managing J1 clubs at the same time in 2026: HASEBE Shigetoshi at Kawasaki Frontale and KOBAYASHI Yoshiyuki at JEF United Chiba.

## alumni

- **HAYAKAWA Tomoki** (goalkeeper, Kashima Antlers) — at Toin from 2014 to 2016, then Meiji University, and joined Kashima in 2021. He was in goal when Kashima won the J1 League in 2025 and was named the 2025 J.LEAGUE MVP, only the second goalkeeper to win the award. He was named in Japan's squad for the 2026 FIFA World Cup.
- **MORIOKA Ryuzo** (defender, retired) — at Toin from 1991 to 1994, then Kashima Antlers, Shimizu S-Pulse and Kyoto Sanga. He won 38 caps for Japan and captained the team at the start of the 2002 World Cup on home soil, before an injury in the opening match against Belgium. As a coach he has managed Gainare Tottori and worked in the Kyoto Sanga and Shimizu S-Pulse academies.
- **TODA Kazuyuki** (midfielder, retired) — joined Shimizu S-Pulse from the school, played on loan for Tottenham Hotspur and ADO Den Haag, and started all four of Japan's matches at the 2002 World Cup. He later coached university teams and managed SC Sagamihara.
- **HASEBE Shigetoshi** (manager, Kawasaki Frontale) — went on to Chuo University and played for Verdy Kawasaki, Kawasaki Frontale, Vissel Kobe and JEF United Ichihara. He managed Mito HollyHock and then Avispa Fukuoka, who won the J.LEAGUE YBC Levain Cup in 2023 under him, the club's first major title. He has managed Kawasaki Frontale since 2025.
- **KOBAYASHI Yoshiyuki** (manager, JEF United Chiba) — went on to Komazawa University and played for Tokyo Verdy, Omiya Ardija, Kashiwa Reysol and Albirex Niigata. Appointed JEF manager in 2023, he led the club back to J1 in 2025 for the first time in 17 years, and his contract has been extended into the 2026–27 season.
- **SHIRAWACHI Keita** (forward, Zweigen Kanazawa) — went on to Toin University of Yokohama and turned professional with Iwaki FC. After a loan at Azul Claro Numazu he has played on loan at Zweigen Kanazawa since 2026.

The current squad is not listed here. None of the 2026 players has an English spelling published by the JFA, the J.LEAGUE or a club, so their names are not romanised on this page.
