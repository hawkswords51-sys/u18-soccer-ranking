## intro

Ryutsu Keizai University Kashiwa High School — RKU Kashiwa, 流通経済大学付属柏 in Japanese — is one of the strongest school football programmes in Japan and a regular in the U-18 Premier League EAST. The school opened in 1985 and the football club started with it; today it has around 131 players and has sent more than 110 players into professional football. It is best known abroad for what Japanese coaches call the "Ryukei model": a single pathway that runs from a junior club through the high school and on to Ryutsu Keizai University, all under the same organisation.

## style

The club's identity was built on hard work, ferocity in duels and an enormous volume of running. Under head coach ENOMOTO Masahiro the team has been adding a more measured build-up and more decision-making by the players themselves — what Japanese reporters call "the new Ryukei". The usual shape is 4-4-2, often with a diamond midfield.

## history

- **1985** — The school opens; the football club is founded. For fifteen years it lives in the shadow of Ichiritsu Funabashi and Narashino, the two clubs that dominated Chiba.
- **2000** — HONDA Yuichiro, already a celebrated coach at Narashino, takes over. He spent his teacher's retirement money on an apartment next to the school and lived alongside his players. The hard-running, siege-mentality identity of the club dates from these years.
- **2001-2002** — The club dormitory opens and a second, dedicated pitch is built.
- **2005** — RKU Kashiwa beat Ichiritsu Funabashi in a competitive match for the first time, and reach the winter national tournament.
- **2007** — National champions in both the winter tournament and the All Japan Youth Championship.
- **2013** — Premier League EAST champions, then national champions in the final against Vissel Kobe U-18.
- **Now** — ENOMOTO Masahiro describes his role as "a good guide" rather than a commander, and the club has moved from top-down instruction towards player-led decision making.

## model

Three things make the pathway unusual by Japanese standards, and almost unique by European ones.

- **A junior club of its own.** Club Dragons Kashiwa covers U-10 to U-15 and feeds the high school. It describes itself as offering "a value different from a J.League academy", and in practice it is a safety net for players not selected by professional academies, and for late developers.
- **A university at the other end.** Ryutsu Keizai University plays in the top division of the Kanto university league and has won the national university competitions five times. A player who does not break through at 18 can spend four years there and still turn professional — a strong argument for families choosing a school at 15.
- **Professional-level facilities and standards.** Main and second pitches, a clubhouse, meeting and shower rooms; junior players train where high-school and university players are working at full intensity. In 2026 four graduates joined J.League clubs directly, and two of the current third-years have already signed for Kataller Toyama.

## alumni

More than 110 former players have turned professional. Among them:

- **HAYASHI Akihiro** (goalkeeper, FC Tokyo and others)
- **OMAE Genki** (forward, Shimizu S-Pulse and others) and **NAKAZATO Takahiro** — part of the 2007 title-winning year, which alone sent eight players into the J.League
- **TAGUCHI Taishi** (midfielder), **GOYA Hiroto** (forward), **YOSHIDA Makito** (forward)
- **KOIZUMI Kei**, **AOKI Ryota**, **GERMAIN Ryo** and **AKIYAMA Yosuke** — the 2013 Premier League-winning year
- **OGAWA Ryoya** and **SEKIGAWA Ikuma**, both now at Kashima Antlers, are the most recent graduates to reach the top of the domestic game

Straight from school to professional clubs: KAMEDA Ayumu (Kataller Toyama, 2025); OFUJI Sota (Tokyo Verdy), SHIMATANI Kishin and ANDO Koki (both Mito HollyHock — Ando moved on to Royal Antwerp in Belgium in June 2026) and MASUDA Sora (Jubilo Iwata), all in 2026.
