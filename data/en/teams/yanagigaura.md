## intro

Yanagigaura High School (柳ヶ浦高校) is a private school in Usa, a city in the north of Oita Prefecture on the island of Kyushu. The school is widely known for its sports clubs. Its football team has qualified three times for the winter All Japan High School Soccer Tournament, in 2005–06, 2023–24 and 2026–27, and three times for the summer Inter-High, most recently in 2024. In 2025 it played in the Prince League Kyushu Division 2, the regional league for the whole of Kyushu, and in 2026 it plays in Oita's top prefectural league. On 15 November 2026 it drew 1-1 with Oita Tsurusaki in the final of the Oita winter qualifying tournament and won the shoot-out 4-1, stopping Oita Tsurusaki from winning a third title in a row. It will play at the national finals for the first time in three years. The school's girls' team won the All Japan High School Women's Soccer Championship for the first time in January 2026, beating Kamimura Gakuen 1-0 in the final.

## style

The club describes its game model as "keeping control of the game in both attack and defence", and it is clear that this means control of the match, not simply of the ball. Its four principles are winning the individual duels, work rate, a quick switch between attack and defence, and communication. In 2026 the team changed its approach before the winter qualifiers. According to the captain, it had played a passing game until the summer, then decided that the defenders should take no risks, play the ball forward early and attack quickly.

The head coach, who has led the team since 2019, describes his theme for 2026 with a Zen phrase about a hen and a chick pecking at the eggshell from both sides at the same moment: the coach and the players should grow together. In the 2026 semi-final, players asked him during the match whether they could change positions, and he agreed. The club's motto is "passion, sincerity, dedication".

## history

- **2005–06** — Yanagigaura reaches the winter championship for the first time and loses in the first round.
- **2019** — The current head coach is appointed.
- **2023–24** — Back at the winter championship after 18 years. In the Oita qualifiers the team beats Oita Tsurusaki 3-2 in the semi-final and Oita High School 2-1 in the final. At the national finals it loses 1-2 to Teikyo University Kani High School in the first round.
- **2024** — Yanagigaura plays at the Inter-High for the third time and loses 0-5 to Teikyo Nagaoka High School in the first round. At the end of the year it wins promotion to the Prince League Kyushu Division 2.
- **2025** — In the Prince League Kyushu Division 2 the team wins four, draws three and loses eleven, finishing eighth of ten and returning to the Oita league. In the winter qualifiers it loses 0-3 to Oita High School in the semi-final.
- **2026** — In the Inter-High qualifiers Yanagigaura loses 0-4 to Oita High School in the semi-final. In the winter qualifiers it beats Oita Nishi 3-0, Oita Tomei 1-0 and Hita Rinko 5-1, then meets Oita High School again in the semi-final on 7 November. In a strong wind the match is goalless until the last moments of extra time, when a substitute forward wins the ball, plays a one-two and crosses for a header that settles the game 1-0. The captain said the team had lost to Oita High School four times before beating them "at the fifth attempt". In the final on 15 November a defender heads in a corner after eight minutes, Oita Tsurusaki equalise before half-time, and after extra time Yanagigaura's goalkeeper saves two penalties in a 4-1 shoot-out win. The national finals start on 28 December 2026.

## model

For readers new to Japanese youth football: high-school teams and J.League academies play in the same national league pyramid. At the top is the U-18 Premier League (EAST and WEST), then nine regional Prince Leagues, then the prefectural leagues. Two high-school tournaments carry even more prestige than the league: the summer Inter-High and the winter championship, which is televised nationally and ends at the National Stadium in Tokyo in January. Each prefecture sends one or two teams to each tournament through its own qualifying competition. Japanese seasons follow the school year, so a winter championship such as "2023–24" starts in late December and finishes in January.

- **A boarding team.** Many of the players come from outside Oita Prefecture and live in the football club's own dormitory, about ten minutes by bicycle from the school, with three meals a day provided, including at weekends. Rooms are reshuffled every three to four months so that players mix with team-mates from different years and backgrounds.
- **Four teams.** The club runs a first team, a second team, a third team and a first-year team. It trains on the school ground on Wednesdays and Thursdays and at two other grounds in Usa on Tuesdays and Fridays, with Mondays for meetings and rest and weekends for matches.
- **A school outside the big cities.** Usa is a small city in the north of Oita, away from the prefectural capital where most of its rivals are based. In the last few years Yanagigaura has reached the national finals twice and played a season in the Prince League Kyushu.

## alumni

The best-known former player is a futsal international: he played for the school at the 2005–06 winter championship, joined Vasagey Oita of the F.League in 2006, and won the AFC Futsal Championship with Japan in 2012 and 2014. Two former players have played in the J.League: a midfielder who joined Tokushima Vortis from the school, and **ONODA Masahito** (defender), who moved to Yanagigaura from another high school, then played for FC Imabari, Shonan Bellmare, Montedio Yamagata and Iwate Grulla Morioka.
