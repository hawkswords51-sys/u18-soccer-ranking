## intro

Saibi High School (済美高校) is a private school in Matsuyama, the capital of Ehime Prefecture on the island of Shikoku. It was founded in 1901 as a girls' school and became co-educational in the 2002 school year, when the boys' football club was also founded. The team reached the summer Inter-High in its second year and the winter All Japan High School Soccer Tournament in its third. On 8 November 2026 it beat Teikyo Daigo 2-1 in the final of the Ehime qualifying tournament and will play at the winter championship for the fifth time, and for the first time in 15 editions. Its best national results are the round of 16 at the winter championship in 2011–12 and at the Inter-High in 2004. In 2026 it plays in Division 1 of Ehime's prefectural league (E1).

## style

The club describes its football as "watching the opponent and playing accordingly": both in attack and in defence, players are expected to read the game and make decisions rather than follow a fixed pattern. The head coach since 2026 is a former Saibi player who studied at Osaka University of Health and Sport Sciences and then spent eight years as a coach at Aomori Yamada High School, one of Japan's strongest football schools, before returning to Saibi as a coach in 2024. In that year the football website Gekisaka wrote that the previous head coach had laid the foundations and the new coach was "building on them". Although Saibi is a private school, Gekisaka reported in 2024 that almost all of its players come from Ehime.

The 2026 team has been consistent in knockout football. In June it won the Shikoku regional championship, beating Kochi Chuo 5-0, Jinsei Gakuen 1-0 and Shikoku Gakuin University Kagawa Nishi 3-1. In the Ehime winter qualifiers it entered in the fourth round as the second seed, scored 19 goals and conceded three in four matches, beat the holders, Nitta, 2-1 in extra time in the semi-finals, and came from behind to beat Teikyo Daigo 2-1 in the final at Ningineer Stadium after the score had been 1-1 at half-time.

## history

- **2002** — The school becomes co-educational and the boys' football club is founded under its first head coach.
- **2003** — With a squad of first- and second-year pupils only, Saibi reaches the Inter-High at the first attempt and loses 1-3 to Toho in the first round.
- **2004** — At the Inter-High the team beats Toyama Daiichi 3-1 and Muroran Otani 3-1 before losing 1-2 to Kunimi, the eventual champions, in the third round. In the 2004–05 school year it also plays at the winter championship for the first time, beating Jissen Gakuen 3-1 in the first round, with the tournament's first hat-trick, and losing 1-2 to Maebashi Shogyo in the second.
- **2006–2007** — Two more winter-championship appearances and a third Inter-High, each ending in the team's first match.
- **2011** — Saibi wins the Prince League Shikoku with 43 points. Its striker scores 32 goals in 18 league matches and goes on to play in the J.League. At the winter championship, its fourth appearance, the team reaches the third round and loses 2-3 to Chukyo University Chukyo, its best result so far.
- **2017** — A late winner in the Ehime final against Matsuyama Kita sends Saibi to the Inter-High for the first time in ten years.
- **2020** — The club's first head coach, who had led it for many years, dies suddenly in March. That winter the team reaches the Ehime qualifying final and loses 1-4 to Nitta.
- **2021** — A member of the club's first intake, who had played more than 300 J2 matches, returns to the school as a teacher and coach. He later becomes head coach and leads the team until the 2025 school year.
- **2023–2025** — Ehime winter runners-up in 2023 (0-2 to Imabari Higashi). In 2024 the team plays at the Inter-High for the fifth time, losing 1-2 to Komazawa University High School in the first round, and finishes runner-up at the Shikoku championship. It reaches the Ehime winter semi-finals in 2025.
- **2026** — A new head coach takes over. Saibi loses the Ehime Inter-High final 0-1 to Imabari Higashi, then wins the Shikoku championship in June and the Ehime winter qualifiers in November.

## model

For readers new to Japanese youth football: high-school teams and J.League academies play in the same national league pyramid. At the top are the U-18 Premier League (EAST and WEST), then nine regional Prince Leagues, then the prefectural leagues. Saibi plays in Division 1 of Ehime's prefectural U-18 league (E1), one level below the Prince League Shikoku. Two high-school tournaments carry even more prestige than the league: the summer Inter-High and the winter championship, which is televised nationally and ends at the National Stadium in January.

- **A large squad with several teams.** The club had 92 players in the 2026 school year. Besides the first team in E1, it enters two teams in Division 2 (E2), and its first-year players play in the Shikoku Rookie League.
- **Modest facilities.** The team trains on the school's own clay pitch, which has floodlights, and at public grounds in Matsuyama; it has no artificial-turf pitch of its own. Players can live in the school's boarding house, which has single rooms and resident staff.
- **Football and study.** Former players have gone on to universities such as Nagoya University, Tokyo Gakugei University, Ehime University, Keio, Waseda and Ritsumeikan.

## alumni

The school says five of its former players have played in the J.League.

- **FUJIMOTO Yoshiki** (forward, Ehime FC) — the top scorer in the Prince League Shikoku in 2011. He went on to Meiji University and has played for Fagiano Okayama, Ehime FC and Montedio Yamagata. He returned to Ehime FC during the 2025 season and is one of the team's vice-captains.

The other four, all now retired, include a member of the first intake who made more than 300 appearances in J2 and now coaches in the Fagiano Okayama academy. Their names are not romanised here because no English spelling has been published by the JFA, the J.League or a club. No Saibi graduate was in Japan's squad for the 2026 World Cup.
