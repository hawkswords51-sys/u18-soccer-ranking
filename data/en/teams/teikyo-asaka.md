## intro

Teikyo Asaka High School (帝京安積, Teikyo Asaka) is a private school in Asaka-machi, Koriyama, in the middle of Fukushima Prefecture. It opened in 1961 as Asaka Shogyo, a commercial high school, and took its present name in 1988 after a partnership with the Teikyo University group. It has no connection with the football powers Teikyo High School in Tokyo or Teikyo Nagaoka in Niigata, apart from the group name. As Asaka Shogyo, the football club played twice at the winter All Japan High School Soccer Tournament and reached the quarter-finals in 1983–84. The modern team dates from 2009, when the school began to invest seriously in football. Since then it has climbed from the prefectural leagues to the regional Prince League Tohoku. In 2024 it ended Shoshi's 13-year run as Fukushima's Inter-High champions. In 2026 the team plays in Prince League Tohoku again, was runner-up at the Tohoku regional championship, and went out of the Inter-High in the second round, 1-2 to Kindai University High School.

## style

The current head coach, a former Shimizu Shogyo player with professional experience, has led the team since 2009. The school says the team plays attacking, possession-based football, built on players who keep improving week by week through league matches. Its matches with Shoshi show what it can do. At the 2024 Fukushima Inter-High qualifiers it held Shoshi to a 2-2 draw after extra time and won 5-3 on penalties. Two years later the final between the same two teams again finished 2-2, and this time Shoshi won 9-8 on penalties. The 2026 Inter-High showed the team's limits as well. Against Kindai University High School, Teikyo Asaka took the lead after 30 minutes, when a cross from the left was worked across the box and finished with a right-footed shot. Then it conceded twice in 13 minutes early in the second half, to a side that pushed forward with more physical power. Kindai went on to reach the final. In the league, 2026 has been a year of survival rather than progress: after 15 of 18 rounds Teikyo Asaka is eighth of ten with four wins, two draws and nine defeats, six points clear of the relegation places.

## history

- **1961** — The school opens in Koriyama as Asaka Shogyo, a commercial high school.
- **1981–82** — Asaka Shogyo plays at the winter championship for the first time, in the 60th edition.
- **1983–84** — A second appearance, in the 62nd edition. The team goes through two penalty shoot-outs, beating Kumamoto Nogyo 2-0 and Tatara Gakuen 8-7 after 0-0 draws, and reaches the quarter-finals. There it loses 0-3 to Teikyo, who go on to win the tournament.
- **1988** — After a business partnership with the Teikyo University group the year before, the school is renamed Teikyo Asaka High School. The football club spends the next two decades away from the national stage.
- **2009** — The school appoints a head coach with professional experience and begins to strengthen the club. He is still in charge in 2026.
- **2013** — The team enters the Fukushima prefectural league in Division 2, and goes up to Division 1 in 2016.
- **2019** — It wins promotion through the regional play-off and joins Prince League Tohoku for the 2020 season.
- **2020** — The school opens IVY FIELD, an artificial-turf football ground with a clubhouse, in January.
- **2021, 2023** — Sixth in Prince League Tohoku.
- **2024** — The breakthrough year. In January the team finishes runner-up at the Tohoku new-season tournament for the first time. In June it beats Higashi Nippon Kokusai Daigaku Shohei 1-0 in the semi-final of the Fukushima Inter-High qualifiers, which is enough for a first national place, then beats Shoshi on penalties in the final. At the Inter-High it loses 0-4 to Shohei in its opening match. In the league it finishes ninth of ten and is relegated.
- **2025** — Back in the Fukushima prefectural league, the team finishes high enough to earn a place in the Prince League Tohoku play-off. In December it beats Meioh High School without conceding and then Yamagata Meisei High School 3-1 to return to the regional league at the first attempt.
- **2026** — Runners-up at the Tohoku championship in June, beaten 0-2 by Aomori Yamada after beating Hachinohe Gakuin Noheji Nishi 4-2 in the semi-final. Runners-up in the Fukushima Inter-High qualifiers, which still means a second national appearance. At the Inter-High the team has a first-round bye and loses 1-2 to Kindai University High School on 26 July.

## model

For readers new to Japanese youth football: high-school teams and J.League academies play in the same national league pyramid. At the top are the U-18 Premier League (EAST and WEST), then nine regional Prince Leagues, then the prefectural leagues. Two high-school tournaments carry even more prestige than the league: the summer Inter-High and the winter championship, which is televised nationally and ends at the National Stadium in January. The Japanese school year starts in April, so a winter championship is labelled with two years (1983–84) and is usually named after the first.

- **Living next to a giant.** Fukushima football has been dominated by Shoshi, a Premier League side that won every Fukushima Inter-High qualifying tournament from 2011 to 2023. Since 2024 the men's football part of the Inter-High has been held in Fukushima every year, so the prefecture now sends two teams. That second place is what turned Teikyo Asaka into a regular national qualifier. The coach said openly in 2024 that "a second place right in front of us is a motivation".
- **Building from 2009.** The club has had a clear plan: prefectural league in 2013, Division 1 in 2016, Prince League in 2020, national tournament in 2024. The relegation in 2024 and the immediate return in 2025 show both how hard the Prince League is and how strong the club now is in Fukushima.
- **Facilities.** IVY FIELD has a full-size pitch, a half-size pitch and a clubhouse. The team plays its Prince League home matches there. A second team plays in Fukushima Division 1.
- **Recruiting from Kanto.** Koriyama is on the Shinkansen line from Tokyo, and the squad shows it. Of the 28 players registered for the 2026 Inter-High, 17 came from junior clubs in Ibaraki, Tochigi, Saitama, Chiba and Tokyo. Most of the rest came from Fukushima and Miyagi, including two from t.a.football club, a junior-youth club whose name it shares with the school.
