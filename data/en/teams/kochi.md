## intro

Kochi High School (高知高校) is a private school in the city of Kochi, on the Pacific side of Shikoku, with an attached junior high school. It is best known across Japan for baseball, but its football club, founded in 1964, has been one of the leading teams in Kochi Prefecture for decades. According to Gekisaka's records, the club has played in the winter All Japan High School Soccer Tournament 20 times and in the summer Inter-High 17 times, reaching the quarter-finals of each once: the winter tournament in 1986–87 and the Inter-High in 2023. It should not be confused with Kochi Chuo (高知中央), a different school in the same city and its main local rival, which has its own page on this site. In 2026 Kochi plays in the Prince League Shikoku, the regional top flight for the four prefectures of the island, and after 14 matches it was third with seven wins, two draws and five defeats (23 points).

## style

The club describes its football with the phrase "全員攻撃・全員守備" – everyone attacks, everyone defends – and aims to be active and aggressive both with and without the ball. Its stated coaching philosophy is to draw out players' own thinking and decisions and to build a team run by the players rather than by instructions from the bench. The current head coach, a former Kochi player, took over in 2020 as only the third head coach in the club's history.

The 2023 Inter-High showed what that looks like at national level: a compact, hard-working side that was difficult to break down. Kochi beat Haguro of Yamagata 4-1, then held Okayama Gakugeikan – the reigning winter champions – to 0-0 and won a marathon penalty shoot-out 14-13. In the quarter-final it lost 0-1 to Meishu Hitachi, who went on to win the tournament.

Results in 2026 have been closer at home. In the prefectural Inter-High qualifier Kochi drew 0-0 with Kochi Chuo in the semi-final and went out 3-5 on penalties, so it did not reach the national finals; Kochi Chuo won the qualifier.

## history

- **1964** — The football club is founded. Only three men have been head coach since; the current one, a former player, was appointed in 2020.
- **1986–87** — The club's best winter run. At the 65th championship Kochi wins two matches, including a 2-0 win over Niigata Kogyo, before losing to the eventual champions from Shizuoka in the quarter-finals.
- **2001** — Kochi reaches the last 16 of both the summer Inter-High and the winter championship.
- **2009–10** — Another double run to the last 16. In the winter tournament Kochi beats Maruoka on penalties after a 2-2 draw and then Seiryo 1-0 at Fukuda Denshi Arena in Chiba.
- **2019** — Last 16 of the Inter-High, and a second Shikoku High School Championship title.
- **2021** — The club enters a national futsal competition for the first time, representing Shikoku at the All Japan U-18 Futsal Championship.
- **2022** — Kochi finishes near the bottom of the Prince League Shikoku and is relegated to the Kochi prefectural league.
- **2023** — The Inter-High, held in Hokkaido, brings the club's best summer result: wins over Haguro and Okayama Gakugeikan, then a narrow quarter-final defeat by Meishu Hitachi.
- **2024** — Kochi wins back its regional place in December through the Prince League Shikoku play-off, beating Tokushima Shiritsu's second team 5-2.
- **2025** — Back in the Prince League, Kochi finishes fourth of ten. In the autumn it wins the prefectural winter qualifier with a 1-0 final against Kochi Chuo. At the national tournament it is beaten 0-4 by Shohei in its first match on 31 December.
- **2026** — A semi-final penalty defeat by Kochi Chuo ends the Inter-High campaign at prefectural level. In the league Kochi is third after 14 rounds, and in September the club's most recent professional, MATSUI Takumi, makes his J.League debut on loan at FC Gifu.

## model

For readers new to Japanese youth football: high-school teams and J.League academies play in the same national league pyramid. At the top are the U-18 Premier League (EAST and WEST), then nine regional Prince Leagues, then the prefectural leagues. Two high-school tournaments carry even more prestige than the league: the summer Inter-High and the winter championship, which is televised nationally and ends in Tokyo in January. Japanese school years run from April to March, so a "2025" winter tournament is played over the New Year into 2026.

- **A six-year pathway.** The club's defining feature is its link with Kochi Junior High School, on the same campus. Many players spend six years in the same football programme.
- **Local core, outside recruits.** The rest of the squad comes from local junior clubs in Kochi, such as the junior-youth team of Kochi United SC, and from clubs in other prefectures. The school has a dormitory, where around 20 players, mostly from outside the prefecture, live.
- **A big squad without its own pitch.** The club lists 84 players, across three year groups. It has no dedicated ground of its own and trains on rented facilities, typically for two to two and a half hours on weekdays, with matches at weekends.
- **University first.** The club says that about nine in ten graduates go on to university. Its professionals have usually turned pro after university rather than straight from school: MATSUI Takumi went via Hannan University, and an earlier graduate of the 2000s went via Meiji University to a long J.League career with FC Machida Zelvia, Ehime FC, V-Varen Nagasaki and Tochigi SC, where he joined the club staff after retiring in 2021.
- **A small prefecture with a strong rivalry.** Kochi is one of Japan's least populous prefectures. The prefecture's places at national tournaments are usually decided between Kochi and Kochi Chuo, with Meitoku Gijuku also a regular contender; the two Kochi city schools met in both the 2025 winter final and the 2026 Inter-High semi-final.

## alumni

- **MATSUI Takumi** (midfielder, FC Gifu, on loan from RB Omiya Ardija) — born in Kochi Prefecture in 2003, he came through Kochi Junior High School and Kochi High School before going to Hannan University in Osaka. In July 2025 RB Omiya Ardija announced that he would join them for 2026, and he was registered as a JFA/J.League special designated player while still at university. He did not play in the J2 League for Omiya, and on 16 September 2026 he moved to J3 club FC Gifu on a development loan until 30 June 2027. He made his J.League debut for Gifu four days later.
