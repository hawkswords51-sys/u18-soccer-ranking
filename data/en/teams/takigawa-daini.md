## intro

Takigawa Daini High School — 滝川第二, usually shortened to "Taki-ni" — is a private school in Nishi Ward, Kobe, in Hyogo Prefecture. The school and its football club were founded together in 1984, and within a few years the team was playing on the national stage. It has since won the two biggest prizes open to a Japanese high-school team: the All Japan U-18 Youth Championship in 2006, when school teams and J.League academies still met in one knockout competition, and the winter All Japan High School Soccer Tournament in the 2010–11 season. Its best-known former player is OKAZAKI Shinji, who scored 50 goals for Japan and won the Premier League with Leicester City. In 2026 the team plays in Prince League Kansai Division 2 and reached the semi-finals of the summer Inter-High.

## style

The club's motto is 怯まず 驕らず 溌剌と — roughly, "never intimidated, never arrogant, always full of energy" — and the coaching staff present it as a statement about character as much as football. The 2026 Inter-High run showed a team that is hard to beat: four matches, five goals scored and two conceded, and two of its four matches went to penalties. It beat Maebashi Ikuei 3-1 and Yamanashi Gakuin 1-0, got past Toko Gakuen on penalties after a 1-1 draw in the quarter-final, and went out of the semi-final only on a 3-4 shoot-out after a 0-0 draw with Kindai University High School.

## history

- **1984** — The school opens and the football club starts with it. The first coach stays for 23 years and builds the programme around character education: manners, discipline and school life before technique.
- **1986–87** — Only in its third year, the club reaches the national winter championship for the first time.
- **1987** — A first Inter-High semi-final.
- **1998–99, 2002–03, 2003–04** — Three runs to the semi-finals of the winter championship. OKAZAKI Shinji is at the school in the early 2000s and leaves for Shimizu S-Pulse in 2005.
- **2006** — Winners of the All Japan Youth (U-18) Championship. Before the league format that exists today, this was the one competition in which school teams and professional-club academies played for the same national U-18 title, so it is a significant one for a school side.
- **2010** — Runners-up at the summer Inter-High, beaten by Ichiritsu Funabashi.
- **2010–11** — Champions of the 89th winter championship, the club's 16th appearance. In the final against Kumiyama, HIGUCHI Hiroki scored twice and finished the tournament as top scorer with eight goals. It was the first winter title for a Hyogo school since the Second World War.
- **2025** — Third in Prince League Kansai Division 2, just below the two promotion places.
- **2026** — Qualified for the Inter-High for the second year in a row and the 25th time, and reached the semi-finals. In the league, the team is among the leaders of Prince League Kansai Division 2 again.

## model

For readers new to Japanese youth football: high-school teams and J.League academies play in the same national league pyramid. At the top are the U-18 Premier League (EAST and WEST), then nine regional Prince Leagues, then the prefectural leagues. Two high-school tournaments carry even more prestige than the league: the summer Inter-High and the winter championship, which is televised nationally and ends at the National Stadium in January.

- **One of Hyogo's main schools.** Hyogo also has Vissel Kobe's academy and several strong private schools, so qualifying for national tournaments is hard. Takigawa Daini has done it 22 times for the winter championship and 25 times for the Inter-High.
- **Character first.** The founding coach's belief was that a player who cannot manage school life will not get better at football. The motto has been kept, and it is how the club describes itself.
- **A record of producing coaches.** Two former pupils were managing J.League clubs at the same time in 2026: YOSHIDA Takayuki at Shimizu S-Pulse and KOGIKU Akio at Sagan Tosu. That is rare for one high school.
- **Junior-high links.** The school has an attached junior high school, and the club also recruits players from junior clubs across the Kansai region.

## alumni

- **OKAZAKI Shinji** (forward, retired) — left the school for Shimizu S-Pulse in 2005, then played for VfB Stuttgart and Mainz 05, won the Premier League with Leicester City in 2015–16, and went on to Huesca and Sint-Truiden. He scored 50 goals in 119 matches for Japan, the third-highest total in the national team's history, and played at the 2010, 2014 and 2018 World Cups. After retiring in 2024 he became head coach of FC Basara Mainz in the German amateur leagues.
- **KAJI Akira** (right-back, retired) — Cerezo Osaka, Oita Trinita, FC Tokyo, Gamba Osaka, Chivas USA and Fagiano Okayama. He won 64 caps for Japan and played at the 2006 World Cup.
- **KANAZAKI Mu** (forward, Verspah Oita) — joined Oita Trinita from the school in 2007. His career took in Nagoya Grampus, Nürnberg, Portimonense, Kashima Antlers and Sagan Tosu, and he played for Japan. He is signed with Verspah Oita of the Japan Football League (JFL) for 2026–27.
- **YOSHIDA Takayuki** (manager, Shimizu S-Pulse) — a forward for Yokohama Flügels, Yokohama F. Marinos, Oita Trinita and Vissel Kobe. As Vissel Kobe's manager he won the J1 League in 2023 and 2024, and he has managed Shimizu since the 2026 season.
- **KOGIKU Akio** (manager, Sagan Tosu) — spent more than two decades at Cerezo Osaka as an academy coach, scout and assistant, and was first-team manager from 2021 to 2024. He has been Sagan Tosu's manager since 2025.
- **HIGUCHI Hiroki** (forward, Fukushima United) — top scorer of the 2010–11 title run. He joined Shimizu S-Pulse, was loaned to FC Gifu and Shonan Bellmare, and has played for Fukushima United since 2016.
- **KAGAWA Yuki** (defender, Kataller Toyama) — went to Hannan University after the school, then played for Renofa Yamaguchi, V-Varen Nagasaki, Tokyo Verdy and Oita Trinita.
- **MOCHII Kyota** (midfielder, FC Imabari) — went to Meiji University after the school, then played for Tokyo Verdy and Azul Claro Numazu before moving to Imabari in 2025.
