## intro

Kindai University High School — 近畿大学附属高校, usually shortened to 近大附 — is the high school of Kindai University in Higashiosaka, on the eastern edge of Osaka. In the summer of 2026 its football team produced the surprise of the year. It came back to the Inter-High, Japan's national high-school summer championship, for the first time in twelve editions, won five games in a row by a single goal or on penalties, and reached the final for the first time in the school's history. There it lost 2-1 to Shizuoka Gakuen in the last minute of stoppage time, at J-Village in Fukushima on 1 August. What made the run remarkable was the level the team came from: in 2026 Kindai does not play in the U-18 Premier League or the Prince League, but in the Osaka Prefectural League, the third tier.

## style

The school's own description is "total football — everyone attacks, everyone defends". The team has kept a three-man back line for many years, and its slogan is 全力に悔いなし, "give everything, regret nothing". At the 2026 Inter-High it won by being hard to beat rather than by dominating the ball. It won 2-1 four times in a row, drew 0-0 in the semi-final and went through on penalties. It relied on a compact defence and on set pieces: the equaliser in the final was a header from a long throw. The coaches kept the same starting eleven for the final.

## history

- **1972** — The football club is founded at the high school of what was then Kinki University.
- **1995** — First appearance at the winter championship, as Osaka's representative. The team beats Tokai Sugao 1-0 and then loses 1-0 to Higashi Fukuoka in the last 16. It qualifies again the following winter.
- **2003–2013** — A decade as one of Osaka's regular national qualifiers: the winter tournament in 2003, 2007 and 2011, and the Inter-High several times, the last in 2013. In 2003 it reaches the last 16 again by beating Toyama Daiichi.
- **2011** — Wins Prince League Kansai Division 2 unbeaten (eight wins, three draws) and is promoted to Division 1. In 2016 it finishes second in the Prince League Kansai.
- **2022–2025** — Wins Division 2 again in 2022, then drops back after one season in Division 1. After finishing ninth in Division 2 in 2025 it is relegated to the Osaka Prefectural League for 2026. In autumn 2025 FC Osaka announces that it will sign two of the school's third-years for 2026 — the first players to go straight from this school into the J.League since the late 1990s.
- **2026** — In the fourth year of the current head coach, a former player who took over from the coach who built the programme, the team wins the Osaka qualifying tournament as a prefectural-league side. At the Inter-High it beats Kunimi, Teikyo Asaka, Shohei and Nissho Gakuen, all 2-1, and Takigawa Daini on penalties, then loses the final to Shizuoka Gakuen with the last kick. About 200 members of the club travelled to Fukushima to sing from the stand. No Osaka school has won the Inter-High since 1978.

## model

For readers new to Japanese youth football: high-school teams and J.League academies play in the same league pyramid — the national U-18 Premier League (EAST and WEST) at the top, nine regional Prince Leagues below it, and a league in each prefecture below those. Kindai was relegated from the Prince League Kansai at the end of 2025, so in 2026 it plays in the Osaka Prefectural League Division 1, alongside the reserve teams of stronger clubs. Four things explain how a team from that level reached a national final.

- **A university behind the school.** Players use the high school's artificial pitch and the artificial pitch on Kindai University's E-Campus, which has had LED floodlights since 2020. The university is also where most of them go next: in the 2021 graduating year, 32 of the 37 third-year players went on to Kindai University.
- **School first.** The club says in its own material that it exists for "human development", not only for football, and it aims at a genuine dual career: players are expected to reach university on their academic record, not just their football.
- **Depth.** The club enters several teams in the Osaka leagues, from the first team down to the lower divisions, plus the Kansai under-16 league for first-years. That produces a large squad and the kind of support that followed the team to the Inter-High final.
- **Osaka makes you hard.** Osaka is one of the most crowded football prefectures in Japan, and it sends only two teams to the Inter-High. The qualifying tournament is a string of one-goal games against Prince League and Premier League schools — the kind of match Kindai won five times in a row in Fukushima.

## alumni

- **KAWAMURA Keito** (forward, Kagoshima United FC) — went from this school to Nippon Sport Science University and then to Tokyo Verdy, with a loan spell at Blaublitz Akita. Joined Kagoshima United in 2025 and won the J3 League Outstanding Player award in his first season.
- **ONISHI Shogo** (goalkeeper, Vanraure Hachinohe) — went from this school to Kindai University, then played for Ehime FC, Azul Claro Numazu and Kagoshima United. Named in the 2025 J3 Best XI as Vanraure Hachinohe won promotion to J2 for the first time in the club's history.
- **SAKIMOTO Oki** (defender, FC Osaka) — a 184cm centre-back who played for the U-17 Japan national team in 2024 while still at the school. Joined FC Osaka straight from school for 2026 and was loaned to Taichung Futuro in Taiwan in July 2026.
- **KAWADA Taiyo** (midfielder, FC Osaka) — a midfielder from Higashiosaka who signed for his home-town club, FC Osaka, alongside Sakimoto.

The players in the 2026 Inter-High squad, and the head coach, have no English spellings published by the JFA or the J.League, so they are not named here.
