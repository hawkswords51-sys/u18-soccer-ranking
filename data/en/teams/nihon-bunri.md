## intro

Nihon Bunri High School (日本文理高校, usually just "Nihon Bunri") is a private school in Nishi Ward, Niigata City, on the Sea of Japan coast. The school opened in April 1984. Nationally it is best known for baseball, but its football club has become one of the leading teams in Niigata Prefecture over the past decade, alongside Teikyo Nagaoka, Niigata Meikun and the Albirex Niigata academy. On its first appearance at the winter All Japan High School Soccer Tournament, in 2017–18, Nihon Bunri reached the quarter-finals; it returned in 2022–23 and reached the last 16. In the league it was runners-up in the Prince League Hokushinetsu in 2022, which earned a place in the play-off for the national Premier League. Its best-known former players are two brothers from Tokyo: goalkeeper AIZAWA Peterkwame, now playing in Austria, and striker AIZAWA David of Vissel Kobe. In 2026 the team plays in Prince League Hokushinetsu Division 1 and, after 16 matches, is fifth with seven wins, two draws and seven defeats (23 points).

## style

The club sums up its approach in a single line it gave the JFA in 2022: "Everyone attacks, everyone defends. We will stick to the Nihon Bunri style and fight with everything we have." In practice that means a team built on work rate and collective effort rather than on one or two stars, and that suits knock-out football. Its two winter runs were both built on narrow margins. In 2017–18 it drew 1-1 with Sakuyo in the third round and won a long penalty shoot-out 7-6, with goalkeeper AIZAWA Peterkwame making a series of saves. In 2022–23 it beat Rissho Shonan 2-1 in the first round and then Seiritsu Gakuen 1-0 with a goal in the second minute of stoppage time. The programme is large: the club enters four teams in league competition, from the Prince League side down to the Niigata prefectural divisions, and its squad of more than a hundred players trains on its own floodlit artificial-turf pitch, with a gym and a boarding house for players from outside the area. That matters in Niigata, where heavy winter snow makes grass pitches unusable for long periods.

## history

- **1984** — The school opens in Niigata City, under the name Niigata Bunri High School.
- **2017** — The breakthrough year. Nihon Bunri wins the Niigata Inter-High qualifiers and plays at the national Inter-High for the first time, losing its first match. It finishes fourth in the Prince League Hokushinetsu, then wins the Niigata qualifiers for the winter championship.
- **2017–18** — At its first winter championship, Nihon Bunri goes all the way to the quarter-finals. In the third round, at Urawa Komaba Stadium, it draws 1-1 with Sakuyo of Okayama and wins 7-6 on penalties, goalkeeper AIZAWA Peterkwame starring. Its run ends on 5 January 2018 with a 0-1 defeat to Yaita Chuo High School.
- **2019** — Runners-up in the Niigata winter qualifiers.
- **2021** — Winners of the Hokushinetsu regional high-school tournament.
- **2022** — The club's best league season: second in the Prince League Hokushinetsu. In the Premier League play-off in December it loses 0-1 to Kyoto Sanga F.C. U-18 and stays in the Prince League. It also wins the Niigata winter qualifiers for the second time.
- **2022–23** — Back at the winter championship. Nihon Bunri beats Rissho Shonan 2-1 on 29 December, with two goals from the same forward, and then Seiritsu Gakuen of Tokyo 1-0 on 31 December, the winner coming in stoppage time. On 2 January 2023 it loses 0-3 to Ohzu High School in the third round.
- **2023–2025** — Third, third and sixth in the Prince League Hokushinetsu Division 1. The club's first-year team finishes second in the Hokushinetsu U-16 Rookie League in both 2024 and 2025, which qualifies it for the national rookie finals.
- **2026** — Nihon Bunri reaches the semi-finals of the Niigata Inter-High qualifiers, beating Niigata Shogyo 7-0 and Kaishi JSC 2-0 before losing to Niigata Meikun, who went on to beat Teikyo Nagaoka in the final. The team therefore did not take part in the national Inter-High. In the league it sits fifth in Prince League Hokushinetsu Division 1 after 16 matches. Former player AIZAWA David turns professional with Vissel Kobe; in June his brother AIZAWA Peterkwame moves to Europe.

## model

For readers new to Japanese youth football: high-school teams and J.League academies play in the same national league pyramid. At the top are the U-18 Premier League (EAST and WEST), then nine regional Prince Leagues, then the prefectural leagues. The Prince League Hokushinetsu covers five prefectures: Niigata, Toyama, Ishikawa and Fukui on the Sea of Japan coast, and Nagano inland. The top two at the end of the season go into a national play-off for promotion to the Premier League. Two high-school tournaments carry even more prestige than the league: the summer Inter-High and the winter championship, which is televised nationally and ends at the National Stadium in January. Japanese school years run from April to March, so a "2022" team plays its winter championship in late December 2022 and early January 2023.

- **A newcomer among Niigata's powers.** For most of the 2010s the prefecture's dominant school was Teikyo Nagaoka, and Niigata Meikun had the longer history. Nihon Bunri broke through only in 2017, and it has been one of the handful of teams contesting the Niigata titles ever since.
- **A large, layered squad.** With more than a hundred players across three year groups, the club runs four teams in league football: the first team in the Prince League and others in the Niigata prefectural divisions. That gives players at every level competitive matches each weekend.
- **Facilities against the snow.** A floodlit artificial-turf pitch allows training through the Niigata winter, and a boarding house means the club can take players from beyond the prefecture.
- **Recruiting nationally.** The best-known products of the club, the AIZAWA brothers, came to Niigata from junior clubs in western Tokyo.
- **A route to the professional game through university, too.** AIZAWA Peterkwame went straight from the school to a J.League club; AIZAWA David went to Hosei University first and turned professional after graduating.

## alumni

- **AIZAWA Peterkwame** (goalkeeper, SW Bregenz) — the hero of the 2017–18 run to the winter quarter-finals. He came from FC Fussa and AZ86 Tokyo Ome in western Tokyo, and joined JEF United Chiba from the school. He went on to play for ReinMeer Aomori and Vanraure Hachinohe before joining Tochigi City in 2024. He made his J.League debut in February 2025 and played ten league matches for Tochigi City in 2026 before moving in June 2026, on a permanent transfer, to SW Bregenz in the Austrian second division. He is 1.93 m tall.
- **AIZAWA David** (forward, Oita Trinita, on loan from Vissel Kobe) — Peterkwame's younger brother, a 1.95 m centre-forward. Like his brother he began at FC Fussa; after Nihon Bunri he studied at Hosei University, where Vissel Kobe signed him in November 2025 for the 2026 season. He has since moved to Oita Trinita in J2 on a development loan, and made his J.League debut for Oita on 8 August 2026.

None of the current Nihon Bunri players is named on this page. None of them has an English spelling published by the JFA, the J.League or a club, so their names are not romanised here.
