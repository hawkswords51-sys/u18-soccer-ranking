## intro

Seiwa Gakuen High School (聖和学園, usually just "Seiwa") is a private school in Sendai, the largest city in Miyagi Prefecture in the Tohoku region of north-east Japan. It began as a girls' school, and its women's football team is one of the most decorated in the country. The boys' football club was founded in 2003, when the school started admitting boys. In little more than two decades it has become one of the most recognisable teams in Japanese school football because of the way it plays: Seiwa players are encouraged to dribble at opponents rather than pass the ball away, and the team is widely known in Japan as a "dribbling squad". The club has appeared six times in the winter All Japan High School Soccer Tournament, reaching the last 16 in 2016–17 and again in 2025–26, and six times in the summer Inter-High. In 2026 it plays in the Prince League Tohoku and reached the second round of the Inter-High.

## style

Seiwa's identity is simple to describe and unusual to watch. Where most Japanese school teams have moved towards organised pressing and quick combination play, Seiwa asks its players to take responsibility for the ball themselves: to beat a defender one-on-one, keep possession under pressure, and carry the ball towards goal. The school's own description of the club calls its football "dribble-based football that drives towards goal", and the club slogan is "football that stays in the memory" (記憶に残るサッカーを！). Japanese football media routinely refer to the dribble as the team's trademark.

The style is not only for show. At the 2025–26 winter championship, Seiwa scored seven goals in three matches, and in the second round against Tokushima Shiritsu it sealed a 3-1 win with a dribbling attack in the closing stages. The team also showed it can win tight matches. In the 2026 Inter-High qualifiers it beat Tohoku High School 2-0 in the semi-final and then defeated Tohoku Gakuin on penalties (0-0, 4-2) to retain the Miyagi title. At the national finals it beat Toyokawa High School of Aichi 3-2 in the first round before losing 0-1 to Kyushu International University Affiliated High School in the second.

## history

- **2003** — Seiwa Gakuen, until then a girls' school, begins admitting boys, and the boys' football club is founded the same year. From the start it borrows the technical, ball-playing approach of the school's famous women's team.
- **2008** — In its first season in the Prince League Tohoku, the young club finishes third, behind Aomori Yamada.
- **2011** — Seiwa wins the Prince League Tohoku. In December it travels to Tokyo for the play-off for a place in the new national Premier League, but loses 2-4 to Asahikawa Jitsugyo High School in the first round.
- **2014–2016** — Three consecutive Miyagi titles in the qualifiers for the winter championship. In 2015 the team also finishes third in the Prince League Tohoku.
- **2016** — The best year so far. Seiwa wins the Tohoku High School Championship and, at the 95th winter championship in 2016–17, reaches the third round (the last 16) for the first time.
- **2019** — A second Tohoku High School Championship title, and a Miyagi Inter-High title.
- **2022** — Seiwa wins both Miyagi tournaments. At the summer Inter-High it reaches the third round (the last 16), its best result in that competition. In the winter it returns to the national championship for the first time in six years, making its fifth appearance.
- **2025** — Seiwa wins the Miyagi Inter-High qualifiers. In November it loses the final of the Miyagi winter qualifiers 1-2 to Sendai Ikuei. Sendai Ikuei then withdraws from the national tournament, and Seiwa takes Miyagi's place as runners-up. It makes the most of the second chance: it beats Naha Nishi 3-0 and Tokushima Shiritsu 3-1 to equal its best result, the last 16, and then loses 1-2 to Nichidai Fujisawa (Nihon University Fujisawa High School) in the third round in January 2026.
- **2026** — Seiwa retains the Miyagi Inter-High title, beating Tohoku Gakuin on penalties in the final, and qualifies for the Inter-High for the second year in a row and the sixth time in all. At the finals it beats Toyokawa 3-2 and then loses 0-1 to Kyushu International University Affiliated High School in the round of 32.

## model

For readers new to Japanese youth football: high-school teams and J.League academies play in the same national league pyramid. At the top is the U-18 Premier League (EAST and WEST), below it nine regional Prince Leagues, and below those the prefectural leagues. Two school tournaments carry even more prestige than the league: the summer Inter-High and the winter championship, which is televised nationally and ends at the National Stadium in Tokyo in January. Japanese school years run from April to March, so a "2025" winter championship is played in December 2025 and January 2026.

- **A boys' club built on a women's tradition.** Seiwa's women's team was founded in 1985, won the first All Japan High School Women's Championship and has won four national titles. When the boys' club was founded in 2003, it took over the women's team's technical, ball-playing philosophy rather than starting from scratch.
- **Individual skill first.** Coaching at Seiwa puts the individual ahead of the system. Players are expected to take on opponents, keep the ball under pressure and make their own decisions on the pitch. The club presents this as part of their education as well as their football: a player who dribbles accepts responsibility for what happens next.
- **Recruiting technical players from beyond Miyagi.** Seiwa attracts players who want to play this way, and not only from its own prefecture. Its professional alumni came from very different backgrounds. OHARA Motoki came from AS Laranja Toyokawa, a junior club in Aichi known for its technical coaching. NAGAI Taishi came from the junior-youth team of Yokohama F. Marinos.
- **A route to the professional game through university.** Seiwa's professionals have mostly turned professional after university rather than straight from school. KATO Tetsuya went via Biwako Seikei Sport College, OHARA Motoki via Tokai Gakuen University and NAGAI Taishi via Toin University of Yokohama. The extra years give technically gifted but physically late-developing players time to grow into the professional game.
- **Holding its own in a crowded region.** Miyagi is also home to Vegalta Sendai's academy and to strong school rivals such as Sendai Ikuei, Tohoku Gakuin and Tohoku High School. In the Prince League Tohoku, Seiwa competes with academies and with the second teams of national powers such as Aomori Yamada.

## alumni

- **KATO Tetsuya** (defender, Albirex Niigata) — went from Seiwa to Biwako Seikei Sport College, then played for Nara Club and FC Imabari before joining Albirex Niigata, where he is a first-team defender.
- **OHARA Motoki** (midfielder, Júbilo Iwata) — a dribbler from Aichi who played at the winter championship in his second year at Seiwa. After Tokai Gakuen University he joined Ehime FC in 2021 and played for Mito HollyHock and Albirex Niigata on loan. He signed for Sanfrecce Hiroshima, and since the start of the 2026–27 season he has played for Júbilo Iwata.
- **NAGAI Taishi** (midfielder, Tochigi SC) — came to Seiwa from the Yokohama F. Marinos junior-youth team and went on to Toin University of Yokohama. He joined Tochigi SC after graduating from university.

The school's football alumni also include players who have played in the J.League and lower divisions, and a Philippines international.
