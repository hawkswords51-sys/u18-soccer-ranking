## intro

Yokohama FC Youth is the under-18 team of Yokohama FC, a club with an unusual origin. When Yokohama Flügels were merged out of existence at the end of 1998, their supporters founded a new club rather than follow the merger, and Yokohama FC began in 1999 with no parent company behind it. The youth team followed in 2001. With little money and, for years, no ground of its own, it grew up in the shadow of Yokohama F. Marinos and Kawasaki Frontale. It reached the Premier League in 2020, and in 2024 won the EAST division. The team trains at the club's LEOC Training Centre in Yokohama.

## style

The team's attacking plan has a name: the near zone, or pocket — the space between the corner of the penalty area and the corner of the six-yard box, just behind a centre-back who has been pulled across. Players make coordinated runs into that space to break a defence open. The idea is taught in the same words from the under-12s upward, so that a player promoted into the under-18s already knows what is expected. WADA Takumi, head coach since 2024, is the person most associated with it. He played for Shimizu S-Pulse, Yokohama FC, Tokyo Verdy, JEF United and Avispa Fukuoka before coaching in the club's academy.

## history

- **1999** — Yokohama FC is founded by supporters of the dissolved Yokohama Flügels.
- **2001** — The youth team is founded. A junior-youth team follows in 2006.
- **2000s** — No permanent ground, and competition for players from much richer neighbours. The academy recruits players other clubs have passed over and develops them slowly.
- **2018** — SAITO Koki signs a professional contract while still in the youth team.
- **2019** — Promotion to the Premier League.
- **2020** — Champions of the Premier League Kanto, in the first season at that level.
- **2022** — A pitch at the training centre is opened and named KOKI SAITO FIELD. The team finishes third at the Club Youth Championship.
- **2024** — WADA Takumi moves up from the junior-youth team and wins the Premier League EAST in his first season; the national final is lost to Ohzu. Three academy players sign professional contracts in the same year.
- **2025** — IWASAKI Ryosuke and TSUKUDA Sota are promoted to the first team.

## model

- **The under-12 strengthening category.** The club runs a selected group at under-12 level, and most of the current under-18 squad has been in the club's system since then. Six or seven years of the same coaching language is what makes the near-zone idea work.
- **One site with the first team.** The LEOC Training Centre is shared with the professionals, which ended years of moving between public pitches. Academy players see the first team's standards every day.
- **A route to the first team, not only to the league.** In September 2026 four of the under-18s held first-team numbers, and one of them, SAITO Kakeru, had signed a professional contract in June.
- **Recruiting for intelligence.** The club says its scouts look for football intelligence, the desire to improve and a fit with the club's way of playing, ahead of physical size or early maturity.

## alumni

- **SAITO Koki** (forward, Queens Park Rangers) — through the junior-youth and youth teams, a professional contract in 2018 while still a youth player, then Lommel and Sparta Rotterdam, and called up by Japan in 2025.
- **SAITO Kosuke** (midfielder, Tokyo Verdy) — through the junior-youth and youth teams to the first team in 2015, and part of the side that took Yokohama FC into J1.
- **SUGITA Hayato**, **OGURA Hinata**, **TAKASHIO Hayase**, **HAYASHI Kengo**, **SHIMIZU Yuto**, **HATA Jui** and **MAEDA Kantaro** — academy graduates in the current first-team squad.
- **TSUKUDA Sota** (defender) and **IWASAKI Ryosuke** (midfielder) — the 2025 promotions, both with Yokohama FC.
