## intro

Niigata Meikun High School (新潟明訓, usually just "Meikun") is a private school in Niigata City, the capital of Niigata Prefecture on the Sea of Japan coast. The football club has been part of the national picture since the 1960s: it has played in the winter All Japan High School Soccer Tournament seven times and in the summer Inter-High eight times, reaching the Inter-High quarter-finals in 2011. In the regional Prince League Hokushinetsu it was champion in 2016 and 2018. Niigata is one of the most competitive football prefectures in eastern Japan, dominated in recent years by Teikyo Nagaoka. Meikun has twice broken that grip lately: it reached the winter championship in the 2024 school year and, in June 2026, beat Teikyo Nagaoka 3-0 in the prefectural final to reach the Inter-High. In 2026 the team plays in Prince League Hokushinetsu Division 2, which it finished as unbeaten champions.

## style

The club's slogan for 2026 is "闘頂" (tōchō), roughly "fight for the summit". The club explains it as a target of three titles in one season: the prefectural titles and the Prince League title. The club says it trains with a focus on the basics of passing and control, applied to match situations. It wants every player to work hard, so that the whole team scores and the whole team defends. The squad of around 80 players is supported by a nutrition adviser, a trainer and a fitness coach as well as the coaching staff.

The 2026 results show a team that is hard to break down. In the 14-match Division 2 season Meikun won 11 and drew three, scoring 32 goals and conceding nine. At the Inter-High in July it had a first-round bye and then took the lead against Kashima Gakuen High School, the previous winter's runners-up, through a goal in the 35th minute, the last minute of the first half. Kashima Gakuen scored twice in the second half, in the 59th and 68th minutes, to win 2-1. (Inter-High matches last 70 minutes.)

## history

- **1972–73** — Meikun wins the Niigata qualifying tournament for the summer Inter-High in consecutive years, its first two national Inter-High appearances.
- **2000** — A third Inter-High, after beating Tokyo Gakkan Niigata in the prefectural final.
- **2011** — The club's best national result so far. Meikun beats Hokuetsu on penalties (1-1, 4-1) in the prefectural final and goes on to reach the quarter-finals of the national Inter-High.
- **2012** — Second in Prince League Hokushinetsu Division 2, in a year when the regional league was being reorganised.
- **2013 and 2015** — Two more Inter-High appearances.
- **2015** — Back in the Prince League, Meikun finishes second behind Albirex Niigata U-18 and goes into the play-off for the national Premier League. In the winter it reaches the second round of the national championship.
- **2016** — Champions of the Prince League Hokushinetsu for the first time, ahead of Teikyo Nagaoka. The Premier League play-off again ends without promotion.
- **2018** — A second Prince League title, and another Inter-High after a penalty shoot-out win over Teikyo Nagaoka (1-1, 4-3) in the prefectural final.
- **2022** — Last of ten teams in the Prince League. When the league splits into two divisions for 2023, Meikun starts in Division 2.
- **2023** — Division 2 champions without losing a match, which brings promotion back to Division 1.
- **2024** — Third in Division 1. In the winter Meikun wins the Niigata qualifying tournament for the first time in nine years and reaches the second round of the 103rd national championship.
- **2025** — Last in Division 1, and relegated to Division 2.
- **2026** — In June, Meikun wins the Niigata Inter-High qualifier for its first Inter-High since 2018. It beats Niigata Konan 1-0, Joetsu on penalties (2-2, 4-3), Nihon Bunri 1-0 and, in the final, Teikyo Nagaoka 3-0. At the Inter-High, held at J-Village in Fukushima, it loses 1-2 to Kashima Gakuen High School in the second round. Kashima Gakuen went out to the eventual champions, Shizuoka Gakuen High School, in the next round. In the league, Meikun finishes the Division 2 season unbeaten and top, two points ahead of the Kanazawa Gakuin University affiliated school. The top two in Division 2 normally go up to Division 1. Promotion is confirmed only after the national play-offs in December.

## model

For readers new to Japanese youth football: high-school teams and J.League academies play in the same national league pyramid. At the top are the U-18 Premier League (EAST and WEST), then nine regional Prince Leagues, then the prefectural leagues. The Prince League Hokushinetsu covers five prefectures: Niigata, Nagano, Toyama, Ishikawa and Fukui. Since 2023 the Hokushinetsu league has had two divisions. Two high-school tournaments carry even more prestige than the league: the summer Inter-High and the winter championship, which is televised nationally and ends at the National Stadium in January. Japanese school years run from April to March, so the 2024 winter championship is the "2024 school year" event, played from December 2024 into January 2025.

- **A school side in a crowded prefecture.** Niigata has a J.League academy, Albirex Niigata U-18, and several strong school teams, among them Teikyo Nagaoka, Nihon Bunri, Hokuetsu and Kaishi Gakuen JSC. Each year only one Niigata team reaches each of the two national school tournaments, so beating Teikyo Nagaoka has been the main obstacle. In June 2026 Meikun did it by three goals in a final.
- **A home for club-trained players.** In the 2026 Inter-High squad list published by the JFA, 27 of the 28 players came from junior club teams (under-15 sides) rather than junior-high school teams. Seven came from Albirex Niigata U-15, the J.League club's own junior-youth team. Most of the rest came from other Niigata clubs such as Nagaoka JYFC, Grancena Niigata and ROUSE Niigata. For players who are not promoted to a J.League academy's U-18 side, Meikun offers a strong league and the national tournaments. The midfielder NAKAMURA Ryotaro, now a professional, came to Meikun from Grancena Niigata's junior-youth team.
- **Study as well as football.** The club describes its aim as producing people who can succeed in a global society as well as footballers. Its players are expected to organise themselves in their studies and in the club.
- **The university route.** Most of Meikun's professionals did not go straight from school to the J.League. NAKAMURA Ryotaro (Chuo University), SEKIGUCHI Masahiro and TAKEUCHI Yutaka (both Hosei University) all played four years of university football first. This is a common path in Japan for players who need more time to develop.

## alumni

- **ISHIKAWA Kei** (goalkeeper, Shimizu S-Pulse) — the most experienced of the club's professionals. He joined Vegalta Sendai straight from the school in 2011, and had loan spells at Sony Sendai and Blaublitz Akita. He then played for Tochigi SC, Sagan Tosu, Gamba Osaka and Ventforet Kofu. He joined Shimizu S-Pulse on a permanent transfer in December 2025.
- **NAKAMURA Ryotaro** (midfielder, Montedio Yamagata) — went from the school to Chuo University. As a professional he has played for Ventforet Kofu, Kashima Antlers and, on loan, Shimizu S-Pulse. He moved to Montedio Yamagata on a permanent transfer for the 2025 season.
- **SEKIGUCHI Masahiro** (defender, Albirex Niigata) — a Niigata City native who went from the school to Hosei University. He then played for Ventforet Kofu and, from 2025, V-Varen Nagasaki. In September 2026 he came home, joining Albirex Niigata on a permanent transfer.
- **TAKEUCHI Yutaka** (defender, Verspah Oita, on loan from Kataller Toyama) — joined Kataller Toyama from Hosei University for the 2025 season. On 1 October 2026 he moved to Verspah Oita of the Japan Football League on a development loan that runs until June 2027.
