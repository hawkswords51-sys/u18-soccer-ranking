## intro

Kyoto Tachibana High School (京都橘, usually just "Tachibana") is a private school in Kyoto, part of the Kyoto Tachibana educational group, which also runs Kyoto Tachibana University. The school was for girls until it began admitting boys in 2000, and the boys' football club was founded in 2001. In barely a decade it became the strongest school team in Kyoto and one of the best known in western Japan. In 2012–13, in only its second appearance, it reached the final of the winter All Japan High School Soccer Tournament, and it followed that with a semi-final and a quarter-final in the next two winters. It played two seasons in the national Premier League (2014 and 2015) and reached the semi-finals of the summer Inter-High in 2019. Its alumni include the J1 midfielders KOYAMATSU Tomoya and SENTO Keiya, both top scorers in that 2012–13 final run, and IWASAKI Yuto. In 2026 the team plays in Prince League Kansai Division 1, where it was fourth after 14 matches (eight wins, two draws, four defeats, 26 points). It did not reach this year's Inter-High.

## style

The head coach has led the team since the club's earliest days, which makes him one of the longest-serving coaches in Japanese school football. His football has its roots in the Netherlands: at university he was coached by a Dutch coach, and he has described that experience as the starting point of his own coaching. He sums up the Tachibana approach as football that "does not skip the midfield", building attacks patiently through the centre of the pitch rather than playing long balls over it. That style suits the club's recruitment, which brings in technically strong players from Kyoto and the wider Kansai region, some of them from J.League junior-youth teams.

The 2026 season has shown both sides of the team. Tachibana won its first eight league matches, including a 1-0 win over Cerezo Osaka U-18 and a 4-1 win over Kokoku, and then drew 0-0 with Kyoto Sanga F.C. U-18 to go into the summer break unbeaten at the top of Prince League Kansai Division 1. The autumn has been much harder: after the break the team lost to Riseisha, Sanda Gakuen, Osaka Sangyo University High School and Omi, with only a goalless draw against Kobe Koryo, and slipped to fourth. In May the team also went out of the Kyoto qualifying tournament for the Inter-High in its first match, the round of 16, drawing 1-1 with Ritsumeikan Uji at its own stadium and losing 1-3 on penalties. Tachibana had won the same tournament in 2025.

## history

- **2000–01** — The school, until then a girls' school, starts admitting boys in 2000. The boys' football club is founded the following year.
- **2007** — First Inter-High, held in Saga, where Tachibana loses its opening match 1-4 to Kiryu Daiichi.
- **2008–09** — First winter championship. Tachibana loses 0-2 to Maebashi Ikuei in the first round.
- **2009–10** — The club enters the Prince League Kansai, wins the second division at the first attempt and is promoted to the first division for 2010.
- **2012–13** — The breakthrough season. With KOYAMATSU Tomoya and SENTO Keiya up front, Tachibana beats Shoshi on penalties, then Sendai Ikuei 3-1, Maruoka 4-1, Teikyo Nagaoka 2-1 and Toko Gakuen 3-0 in the semi-final at the National Stadium in Tokyo. The final against Hosho, from Miyazaki, ends 2-2 and Tachibana loses the shoot-out 3-5.
- **2013–14** — Back at the National Stadium for a second year running. Tachibana beats Fujieda Higashi, Naha Nishi and Ichiritsu Funabashi before losing the semi-final 0-4 to Seiryo. In the league, second place in Prince League Kansai and a play-off win earn promotion to the Premier League.
- **2014** — Eighth in the Premier League WEST, playing every week against the top J.League academies in western Japan. In the winter Tachibana reaches the quarter-finals for the third year in a row.
- **2015** — Tenth in the Premier League WEST and relegated to the Prince League Kansai, where the club has played ever since.
- **2017–19** — Second in Prince League Kansai in 2017 and 2019 and third in 2018.
- **2019** — The best Inter-High so far. Tachibana beats Senshu Kitakami on penalties, Nagoya Keizai University Takakura 3-0 and Hokuetsu 2-1, then loses the semi-final 0-1 to Toko Gakuen, who went on to win the tournament.
- **2022** — The KYOTO TACHIBANA Stadium opens in Yamashina Ward, giving the club its own artificial-turf ground.
- **2023–26** — Three winter championships in a row (2023–24, 2024–25 and 2025–26). The last two end in the first round: 1-2 against Teikyo, then 0-0 and 5-6 on penalties against Yamanashi Gakuin High School. In 2025 the team also wins all three Kyoto titles and plays at the Inter-High in Fukushima, losing 0-5 to Toin Gakuen High School in the first round. That autumn forward ITO Sota, a Japan U-18 international, signs for Vissel Kobe.
- **2026** — Eight straight league wins to open the season and top place at the summer break, followed by a difficult autumn. The team goes out of the Inter-High qualifiers in the round of 16.

## model

For readers new to Japanese youth football: high-school teams and J.League academies play in the same national league pyramid. At the top are the U-18 Premier League (EAST and WEST), then nine regional Prince Leagues, then the prefectural leagues. Two high-school tournaments carry even more prestige than the league: the summer Inter-High and the winter championship, which is televised nationally and ends at the National Stadium in January. Because the Japanese school year runs from April to March, a winter championship is usually referred to by both years, such as 2012–13.

- **A new club that rose fast.** Most of Japan's leading school teams have decades of history. Tachibana reached a national final only 12 years after it was founded, and it did so in Kyoto, where it has to beat strong rivals such as Higashiyama to qualify for anything.
- **One coach, one idea.** The same head coach has shaped the club from the start, so the Dutch-influenced, midfield-based style has stayed in place through every generation of players.
- **Its own stadium.** The KYOTO TACHIBANA Stadium, opened in March 2022 for the 120th anniversary of the school group, has an artificial-turf football pitch and AI cameras that follow the ball automatically for live streaming and video analysis. It is shared with Kyoto Tachibana University and hosts official matches, including Tachibana's 2026 Inter-High qualifier.
- **Routes to the professional game.** Tachibana players have gone directly to J.League clubs (KOYAMATSU Tomoya, IWASAKI Yuto, NISHINO Taiyo, ITO Sota) and via university (SENTO Keiya and MATSUDA Keita through Toyo University, YAMADA Gouki through Kwansei Gakuin University).
- **Players from academy backgrounds.** Several alumni came to the school from J.League or JFA academies at junior-high age: NISHINO Taiyo from Tokushima Vortis' junior-youth team and MATSUDA Keita from Cerezo Osaka U-15.

## alumni

- **KOYAMATSU Tomoya** (midfielder, Nagoya Grampus) — joint top scorer of the 2012–13 winter championship with five goals. He went straight from school to Nagoya Grampus in 2014, then played for Kyoto Sanga F.C. and Sagan Tosu before joining Kashiwa Reysol in 2022, where he helped the team finish second in J1 in 2025. In December 2025 he returned to Nagoya after ten years away.
- **SENTO Keiya** (midfielder, FC Machida Zelvia) — KOYAMATSU's strike partner in 2012–13, also with five goals. He went on to Toyo University and then played for Kyoto Sanga F.C., Yokohama F. Marinos, Sagan Tosu, Nagoya Grampus and Kashiwa Reysol before moving to Machida.
- **IWASAKI Yuto** (midfielder, V-Varen Nagasaki) — joined Kyoto Sanga F.C. in 2017. He later played for Hokkaido Consadole Sapporo, Sagan Tosu and Avispa Fukuoka, and moved to Nagasaki on a permanent transfer for 2026.
- **NISHINO Taiyo** (forward, Shonan Bellmare) — came from Tokushima Vortis' junior-youth team and returned to Tokushima as a professional after school. After a loan spell at Tochigi SC he joined Shonan on a permanent transfer in June 2026.
- **MATSUDA Keita** (defender, Fujieda MYFC) — a 190cm centre-back who went from Tachibana to Toyo University. He has played for Mito HollyHock, FC Osaka, Kyoto Sanga F.C., Renofa Yamaguchi and, on loan, Tokushima Vortis, and joined Fujieda on a permanent transfer from Kyoto in June 2026.
- **YAMADA Gouki** (forward, Tokyo Verdy) — a Kyoto-born forward who played at Kwansei Gakuin University before joining Tokyo Verdy, where he made his J.League debut in 2023.
- **NAGAI Tatsunari** (goalkeeper, Shonan Bellmare) — won J3 awards with FC Osaka, including a place among the league's outstanding players in 2024, and joined Shonan on a permanent transfer for 2025.
- **ITO Sota** (forward, Vissel Kobe) — a 2025 graduate and Japan U-18 international. Vissel Kobe announced his signing in September 2025 and he joined the J1 club for the 2026 season.

Current players are not named on this page. None of the 2026 squad has an English spelling published by the JFA, the J.LEAGUE or a club, so their names are not romanised here.
