## intro

Vegalta Sendai Youth is the under-18 team of Vegalta Sendai, the J.League club of Sendai in Miyagi Prefecture, and in 2026 it is playing its first season in the Premier League. That is a bigger step than it sounds. Football in Tohoku, the six prefectures of northern Honshu, has long been dominated by school teams — Aomori Yamada above all, with Shoshi in Fukushima close behind — and the best local teenagers have tended to go to them. Sendai is the first J.League club academy from the region to reach the top division. It got there by beating the play-off in December 2025, a few months after reaching the final of the Japan Club Youth Championship.

## style

Under KATO Nozomu the team presses from the front as a unit and tries to move the ball forward quickly once it wins it back. The coaching is described by the club as patient rather than spectacular: there is no shortcut in developing teenagers, and the academy says its first aim is to produce people who will do well in life, with professional footballers as one result of that rather than the only one. Care of the training ground and equipment, and how players behave off the pitch, are treated as part of the programme.

## history

- **Before the J.League** — The club grows out of the football team of Tohoku Electric Power, a regional company side, and becomes Vegalta Sendai in the 1990s.
- **2000s** — A long period in which the academy produces almost no first-team players. Talent from the region goes either to clubs around Tokyo or to the big school teams.
- **2010s** — The academy rebuilds around two ideas: that the club's own football schools should feed a single pathway from under-12 to under-18, and that players should be developed as people first.
- **2016** — Prince League Tohoku champions, the first of five regional Club Youth titles in six years.
- **2024** — YOKOYAMA Sota is promoted to the first team.
- **2025** — Runners-up in the Club Youth Championship and third in the J Youth Cup, then promotion to the Premier League through the play-off. FURUYA Ayumu and NAGAI Taiga are promoted to the first team for 2026.
- **2026** — The first Premier League season. NAGAI is called up by Japan's under-19s for the Maurice Revello Tournament in France.

## model

- **A target for the first team.** The club has set itself a public goal of producing ten first-team players from the academy in ten years, and an aspiration that half the first-team squad should one day be homegrown. Promotions since 2024 have come faster than that target.
- **A wide base in Miyagi.** The club runs football schools across the prefecture that feed its junior and junior-youth teams, so that the players who reach the under-18s have usually been in the system for years. Most of the 2026 squad came up through the club's own junior-youth team.
- **Mixing the age groups.** Regular sessions bring players from different academy teams onto the same pitch, and first-team goalkeepers coach the academy goalkeepers directly several times a year.
- **A pitch that works in winter.** Snow and frozen ground have always been the region's handicap. The academy now trains on a natural-grass ground on a university campus in the north of Sendai, which can be used through the year.
