## intro

Matsumoto Kokusai High School (松本国際高校, often shortened to "Matsukoku") is a private school in Matsumoto, in the mountains of Nagano Prefecture, with an attached junior high school. The school itself dates back to 1945, but the football club is young: it was founded in 2005, the year the school came under new management and was renamed Sozo Gakuen University Affiliated High School. The club's head coach has been in charge since that first year. In 2011 the school became Sozo Gakuen High School, and in 2018 it took its present name, so older records list the team as "Sozo Gakuen". In two decades the club has gone from nothing to six appearances at the winter All Japan High School Soccer Tournament and five at the summer Inter-High. In 2026 it plays in Prince League Hokushinetsu Division 1, the regional league just below the national Premier League, after finishing second in that division in 2025. It won the Nagano qualifiers for the 2026 Inter-High without conceding a goal, then went out in the second round of the national finals on penalties.

## style

The club does not publish a detailed tactical manifesto, so this page sticks to what the record shows. Its stated aims, on the club's own site, are to become a team that wins regularly at national level, to produce professional players, and to raise people who are useful to society and whom others want to support. The coach's message speaks of carrying on the "Sozo spirit" built up in the Sozo Gakuen years, starting with the development of each individual. On the pitch, the 2026 team has been hard to beat but short of goals. In its first fifteen Prince League matches it won four, drew seven and lost four, scoring 10 and conceding 12, and it beat the current leaders Matsumoto Yamaga U-18 1-0 in May and Albirex Niigata U-18 2-1 in June. In the Nagano Inter-High qualifiers it kept five clean sheets in five matches, including a goalless semi-final against Matsusho Gakuen won on penalties and a 3-0 final against Ichiritsu Nagano. At the national finals it beat KBC High School of Okinawa 5-0 before a 1-1 draw with Asahikawa Jitsugyo High School and a 7-8 penalty shoot-out defeat.

## history

- **2005** — The school, then called Tsukahara Seiun, comes under new management and becomes Sozo Gakuen University Affiliated High School. The football club is founded the same year, and its first head coach is still in charge two decades later.
- **2008** — In only its fourth year, the club reaches the semi-finals of the Nagano qualifiers for the winter championship.
- **2012** — The breakthrough year. The Inter-High is held in Nagano Prefecture, centred on Matsumoto, and Sozo Gakuen takes part after reaching the final of the prefectural qualifiers. At the finals it wins its first-round match on penalties after a 1-1 draw and then loses a 3-3 draw with Sano Nichidai on penalties in the second round. In the autumn it wins the Nagano qualifiers for the winter championship, settling the final on penalties, and makes its first national appearance there, losing 2-3 to Kagawa Nishi in the first round.
- **2013–2015** — The club spends three seasons in the Prince League Hokushinetsu, finishing seventh, seventh and tenth before dropping back to the prefectural leagues. In 2015 it returns to the Inter-High, beats Tokai Gakuen on penalties and loses to Takigawa Daini High School on penalties in the second round.
- **2016–17** — The club's best winter championship: a penalty win over Hiroshima Minami, then a 0-3 defeat by Shochi Fukaya in the third round.
- **2018** — The school is renamed Matsumoto Kokusai High School. The team reaches the Inter-High again and loses its first match on penalties to Urawa Minami.
- **2019–2020** — A fourth Inter-High (lost 2-3 to Kochi in the first round) and back-to-back winter championships. In 2019–20 the team beats Wakayama Kogyo 1-0 before losing 1-2 to Yokkaichi Chuo Kogyo. In 2020–21 it loses 0-6 to Kyoto Tachibana.
- **2021** — The attached junior high school starts its own football team, so that players can be developed over six years, from age 12 to 18. A girls' team is also founded.
- **2022–2023** — Two more winter championships, lost in the first round to Yonago Kita (1-2) and Kamimura Gakuen (0-2). In 2023 the club returns to the Prince League Hokushinetsu, now in Division 2, and finishes fourth.
- **2024** — Second in Division 2 and promotion to Division 1.
- **2025** — Runners-up in Prince League Hokushinetsu Division 1 at the first attempt.
- **2026** — The first players to come through the attached junior high school reach their final year. The team wins the Nagano Inter-High qualifiers without conceding, and returns to the finals for the first time since 2019. It beats KBC High School 5-0 and then loses 7-8 on penalties to Asahikawa Jitsugyo High School after a 1-1 draw.

## model

For readers new to Japanese youth football: high-school teams and J.League academies play in the same national league pyramid. At the top are the U-18 Premier League (EAST and WEST), then nine regional Prince Leagues, then the prefectural leagues. Matsumoto Kokusai plays in the Prince League Hokushinetsu, which covers Niigata, Toyama, Ishikawa, Fukui and Nagano and is split into two divisions. Two high-school tournaments carry even more prestige than the league: the summer Inter-High and the winter championship, which is televised nationally and ends in January. Japanese school years start in April, so a winter championship is often written with two years, such as 2016–17.

- **A young club in a traditional football prefecture.** Nagano's school football was long led by older schools such as Matsusho Gakuen and Ichiritsu Nagano. Matsumoto Kokusai has joined them at the top in less than twenty years, with the same coach throughout.
- **Six-year development.** Since 2021 the attached junior high school has had its own team. In 2026 several players in the Inter-High squad came up from it, alongside local Nagano club players.
- **Recruitment from outside the prefecture.** The 2026 Inter-High squad list also includes many players from junior clubs and schools in Kanagawa, Saitama, Aichi and Tokyo.
- **Sharing a city with a J.League academy.** Matsumoto is home to Matsumoto Yamaga FC, whose U-18 team plays in the same Prince League division. The two meet twice a season in a local derby.
- **The university route.** Of the club's professional players, the best-known went to university before turning professional, rather than joining a J.League club straight from school.

## alumni

The club says it has produced four J.League players.

- **MORI Kodai** (defender, Albirex Niigata) — went from Sozo Gakuen to Biwako Seikei Sport College, then joined Tokushima Vortis. He made his J.League debut in 2023 and now plays for Albirex Niigata, in J2 in the 2026–27 season.
- **DOAN Ritsu's elder brother** — the older brother of the Japan international DOAN Ritsu also played at Sozo Gakuen, from 2011 to 2013. After university he played for AC Nagano Parceiro in J3 and retired in 2021. He is not named here because no official English spelling of his name has been published.
- A midfielder from the club's first years spent eight seasons with Ventforet Kofu after university, from 2013 to 2020, and then joined the Kofu academy staff. Another former player went on to Kamatamare Sanuki via university. Neither is named here because no official English spelling of their names has been found.

The 2026 squad is not listed here. None of the current players has an English spelling published by the JFA, the J.League or a club, so their names are not romanised on this page. For the same reason, the head coach is not named.
