## intro

Sapporo Otani High School (札幌大谷, usually just "Otani") is a private school in Higashi Ward, Sapporo, the capital of Hokkaido, with an attached junior high school. The school itself is more than a century old, but its boys' football club is one of the youngest strong teams in Japan: it was founded in April 2009 with eleven players and two managers. Within four years it had reached the national Inter-High, and within five the winter All Japan High School Soccer Tournament. It has since played at eight Inter-Highs and four winter championships, and in 2025 it won the Prince League Hokkaido for the first time, finishing ahead of the Hokkaido Consadole Sapporo academy. In 2026 the team is defending that title and is third in the Prince League Hokkaido after 14 matches, with eight wins, one draw and five losses (25 points). It did not qualify for the 2026 Inter-High, losing the Hokkaido semi-final 2-3 after extra time to Asahikawa Jitsugyo High School.

## style

The club sums up its approach in two phrases: "football in which every player's face can be seen" and "a strong and well-loved team". The idea is that the whole squad shares the same base of hard work, running and winning individual duels, and that each player's own strengths are then built on top of that base, so that the team does not depend on a single star. Its stated mission is to give "smiles and inspiration" to the people around it and to be a team that younger players look up to; its working motto is to do ordinary things "with an effort that nobody else can copy". Results bear out the idea of a hard-working, competitive side. Many of its national matches have been close: of its four winter-championship and eight Inter-High campaigns, several have been decided by a single goal or on penalties, including a 14-round shoot-out (12-11) against Fujii Gakuen Samukawa at the 2024–25 winter championship. In the 2025 Prince League Hokkaido it lost only twice in 14 matches (11 wins, 1 draw, 2 losses).

## history

- **2009** — The boys' football club is founded in April with eleven players and two managers. The club states its aim as developing teams and players from Hokkaido who can compete nationally.
- **2012** — In the club's fourth year, Otani wins through the Hokkaido qualifiers to reach its first Inter-High. It goes out in the first round.
- **2013** — A second Inter-High, and in the winter the club's first All Japan High School Soccer Tournament, where it loses 0-3 to Kobe Koryo in the first round.
- **2015–16** — Otani wins a winter-championship match for the first time, beating Kagoshima Josei on penalties after a 1-1 draw, before losing 0-3 to Chukyo University Chukyo in the second round.
- **2018** — The club's best Inter-High. Otani beats Sakuyo on penalties (1-1, 3-1) in the second round and is knocked out 2-3 by Shohei in the third round, finishing in the last 16.
- **2020–21** — A third winter championship, ending with a 0-1 defeat to Ohtemae Takamatsu in the first round.
- **2022** — Second in the Prince League Hokkaido, which earns the club's first place in the play-off for the national Premier League. Otani loses 1-2 to Hamamatsu Kaiseikan in the first round.
- **2024** — Otani wins the Hokkaido Inter-High qualifiers. At the national finals it beats Toyama Daiichi High School on penalties after a 3-3 draw, then loses 1-4 to Ichiritsu Funabashi. In November it beats Asahikawa Jitsugyo 3-1 in the Hokkaido final to return to the winter championship after four years. Captained by midfielder SASA Shuta, who had already agreed to join FC Imabari, the team wins a 12-11 shoot-out against Fujii Gakuen Samukawa and then loses 1-2 to the Premier League champions, Ohzu High School.
- **2025** — Otani retains the Hokkaido Inter-High title but goes out of the national finals on penalties (0-0, 6-7) to Toyama Daiichi in its first match. In the autumn it wins the Prince League Hokkaido for the first time. In the Premier League play-off it loses 3-4 to Jubilo Iwata U-18, who come from behind and go on to win promotion. In the winter qualifiers it reaches the Hokkaido final but loses 0-1 to Hokkai.
- **2026** — As Hokkaido Inter-High champions for the previous two years, Otani loses the semi-final 2-3 after extra time to Asahikawa Jitsugyo, who go on to win the tournament. In June, former captain SASA Shuta moves from FC Imabari to Urawa Reds on a permanent transfer.

## model

For readers new to Japanese youth football: high-school teams and J.League academies play in the same national league pyramid. At the top is the U-18 Premier League (EAST and WEST). Below it are nine regional Prince Leagues and then the prefectural leagues. Each Prince League winner, and in some regions the runner-up, goes into a year-end play-off for promotion to the Premier League. Two high-school tournaments carry even more prestige than the league: the summer Inter-High and the winter championship, which is televised nationally and ends at the National Stadium in January. The Japanese school year runs from April to March, so a winter championship played in December and January is dated across two calendar years (for example 2024–25).

- **A new club in a hard region.** Hokkaido is Japan's northernmost and largest prefecture, and its long, snowy winters limit outdoor training for several months of the year. Hokkaido's schools compete for a small number of national places, usually two at the Inter-High and one at the winter championship, against long-established rivals such as Asahikawa Jitsugyo High School and Hokkai. That a club founded in 2009 has reached 12 national tournaments in its first 17 seasons makes it one of the fastest risers in the country.
- **School football against a J.League academy.** The Prince League Hokkaido includes Hokkaido Consadole Sapporo U-18, the youth team of the region's J.League club. Otani's 2025 title, won ahead of the Consadole academy, was the club's biggest league achievement.
- **Junior-high links.** The attached junior high school has its own football team, and players can move up through both schools. SASA Shuta, for example, came to Otani's junior high school from Consadole Sapporo's under-12 team and stayed for six years.
- **Its own ground.** The club trains at the school's Okadama ground in Higashi Ward, Sapporo, which has one artificial-turf pitch, one natural-grass pitch and a two-storey club house for players and staff.
- **Routes into professional football.** Otani players have turned professional both directly from school (OYAMA Musashi to Cerezo Osaka, SASA Shuta to FC Imabari) and via university (KIMURA Takaya, after Konan University).

## alumni

- **SASA Shuta** (midfielder, Urawa Reds) — came through Sapporo Otani's junior high and high schools and captained the team at the 2024–25 winter championship. He was named one of the outstanding players of the 2024 Inter-High and joined FC Imabari straight from school, making his J.League debut in February 2025. In June 2026, at the age of 19, he moved to Urawa Reds on a permanent transfer.
- **KIMURA Takaya** (midfielder, Fagiano Okayama) — a 2017 graduate who went on to Konan University and joined Fagiano Okayama in 2021. He was part of the Okayama squad that won promotion to J1 for the first time in the club's history, made his J1 debut in February 2025, and is still with the club in 2026.
- **OYAMA Musashi** (midfielder, Criacao Shinjuku) — a 2017 graduate of the same year group as KIMURA, who joined Cerezo Osaka directly from the school. He later played for FC Osaka and Kataller Toyama, and in 2026 he joined Criacao Shinjuku of the Japan Football League (JFL), the fourth tier.
