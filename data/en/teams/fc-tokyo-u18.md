## intro

FC Tokyo U-18 is the under-18 team of FC Tokyo, and the academy that produced KUBO Takefusa. The club joined the J.League in 1999 and built a youth structure at the same time; two years later it won the Japan Club Youth Championship, which for a new club competing in the most crowded talent market in the country was not supposed to happen that fast. It has stayed at the top since. The team plays in the Premier League EAST and trains at Kodaira, in western Tokyo, on the same site as the first team.

## style

The programme's phrase is "together with the community", and it treats personal development and football as the same project. On the pitch it works backwards from the first team: pressing high, moving quickly in transition, and building from the goalkeeper. Under KITA Shin the emphasis is on being proactive — taking the initiative with and without the ball — with SATO Yukihiko and KAJIYAMA Yohei, who wore FC Tokyo's number 10 for years, among the coaching staff.

## history

- **1999** — FC Tokyo enters the J.League and builds a professional academy out of the old company club's youth set-up. It has neither the facilities nor the scouting network of the original J.League clubs, so it finds players in Tokyo and develops them hard.
- **2001** — A first national title at the Club Youth Championship, plus a runners-up finish in the J Youth Cup.
- **2002–2006** — The Kodaira training ground opens and is expanded; the under-18s move there, onto a site shared with the first team. Training next to professionals every day changes the standard of everything.
- **2007** — The J Youth Cup, at last.
- **2008** — A second Club Youth Championship.
- **2016** — A third, beating Shimizu S-Pulse Youth in the final.
- **2017** — A fourth Club Youth Championship, and a second in a row: 2-0 against Urawa Red Diamonds Youth in the final, KUBO Takefusa scoring the first. Then Premier League EAST, and the national title after that comeback. BANGNAGANDE Kashif comes out of the same period.
- **2026** — KITA Shin is head coach; the squad continues the club's habit of touring, with trips to the United States and France built into the programme.

## model

- **Two junior-youth teams, not one.** FC Tokyo runs under-15 sides in Fukagawa in the east of the city and in the Musashino area in the west, which together cover Tokyo and parts of Saitama, Chiba and Kanagawa. The best of both compete for the same under-18 places. No other club in Japan has this, because no other club has this population inside one commuting network.
- **One site with the first team.** The academy and the professionals train at Kodaira. First-team staff watch under-18 sessions directly, and a player who is ready can be registered for the first team immediately. The artificial pitch there also hosts Premier League matches, so home games are played on familiar ground.
- **Development as a public thing.** The club's stated position is that an academy should not be a sealed elite institution. Home matches are played in front of supporters and local residents, and since 2012 the ground has been open to community groups on weekdays. Playing under real noise from 16 is part of the training.
- **Long-haul touring on purpose.** Trips to the United States and France are scheduled with the travel included as the point: more than 24 hours to Paris via Dubai, jet lag, unfamiliar food, and meetings where the players are expected to argue with each other rather than wait to be told.

## alumni

- **KUBO Takefusa** (midfielder, Real Sociedad) — came through Barcelona's academy, returned to FC Tokyo, broke the J.League's youngest-appearance records, and moved to Real Madrid. He wore Japan's number 8 at the 2026 World Cup.
- **GONDA Shuichi** (goalkeeper) — Japan's goalkeeper at the 2022 World Cup.
- **MUTO Yoshinori** (forward) — Mainz, Newcastle and Eibar in Europe, then back to Japan.
- **BANGNAGANDE Kashif** (left-back, FC Tokyo) — a Japan international, and the academy's current public face.
- **SATO Ryunosuke** (midfielder, FC Tokyo) — left-footed, came through the Musashino under-15 side, was named the J.League's best young player after a loan season, and missed the 2026 World Cup squad at 19.
- **NOZAWA Taishi Brandon** (goalkeeper, Royal Antwerp) — moved to Belgium in 2025.
- **HASHIMOTO Kento** (midfielder, FC Tokyo) — played in Russia and Spain before returning.
- **HARA Taichi** (forward), **KIMURA Seiji** (defender), **OMORI Rio**, **OTAKE Yohei**, **NOZAWA Hideyuki**, **SHIGEMATSU Kentaro** and **KAMATA Jiro** — a long list of professionals across every position.
- **YOSHIMOTO Kazunori** (defender) — now a scout for the club he played for.

The academy does not specialise in one type of player, which for a club drawing from a city of this size is the sensible design.
