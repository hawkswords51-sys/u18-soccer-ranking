## intro

Aomori Yamada High School — 青森山田 in Japanese — is the most successful school football programme in Japan over the past decade. It is based in Aomori, the northernmost prefecture of the main island, where snow covers the ground for months, and it has turned that into an advantage rather than an excuse. The club has won the All Japan High School Soccer Tournament, the winter knockout competition that draws the biggest crowds in Japanese youth football, four times, and the national title of the U-18 Premier League three times — more than any other school in the league's history. It plays in the Premier League EAST.

## style

The identity is intensity. Duels are contested as if the match depended on each one, the running volume is very high, and the pressing is co-ordinated rather than individual. On top of that sits an unusually professional set-piece department: corners and free-kicks are worked with several designed variations, and the long throw is used as a way of winning territory. Japanese reporters describe the side as having "a completeness beyond what a single school team should have".

The technical side comes from somewhere specific: an indoor artificial pitch, built in 2003, that lets the squad train in boots at full speed in the middle of a blizzard.

## history

- **1970** — The football club is founded, and spends two decades as one school among many in the prefecture.
- **1989** — TAGUCHI Mitsuhisa, a former Japan goalkeeper, becomes manager and starts a serious programme. Two years later the school reaches both national tournaments for the first time.
- **1995** — KURODA Go takes over. He stays for 28 seasons and builds everything the club is now known for.
- **2003** — The indoor artificial pitch (591 m²) opens, and winter stops being a limit.
- **2005** — A first national title, at the Inter-High.
- **2016** — The first double: the national U-18 league title and the winter championship.
- **2021** — The 100th winter championship, plus the Inter-High and the EAST division.
- **2023** — Another double. At the end of it KURODA Go leaves to manage FC Machida Zelvia in the J.League — a direct move from school football to a professional first team, which almost never happens in Japan.
- **2023–** — MASAKI Masanori, a graduate of the school and for years its head coach's assistant, takes over.
- **2025** — A 29th consecutive Aomori prefecture title, and a place at the 104th winter championship.
- **2026** — A 28th Inter-High appearance; they beat Higashiyama 1-0 and then lost 2-1 to Yamanashi Gakuin in the second round.

## model

Three things explain the record, and none of them can be copied easily.

- **Snow as a training tool.** Winter sessions on snow — what the club calls the "snow march" — force players closer together because the ball will not run, so contact is constant. Core strength, balance and the willingness to fight for the ball are built in conditions no southern school can reproduce.
- **An indoor pitch since 2003.** The same squad walks indoors and works on shape, patterns and finishing at match speed. The combination of the two, rough outdoors and precise indoors, is what the club means by turning a disadvantage into an advantage.
- **A junior high school of its own.** Aomori Yamada Junior High School is a national power in its own right, with five titles including four in a row from 2014 to 2017. Players arriving at 15 have already spent three years in the same language and the same standards, which is why the senior team can play a demanding system with first-year players in it.

The programme is also now openly international: players who grew up abroad, and players with more than one nationality, have come to treat the school as one of the strongest options available in Japan.

## alumni

- **SHIBASAKI Gaku** (midfielder) — the school's most famous graduate, a Japan international who wore the number 10.
- **MATSUKI Kuryu** (midfielder) — moved from FC Tokyo to Southampton in England.
- **MUROYA Sei** (full-back) — a Japan international, with FC Tokyo and in Germany.
- **UNO Zento** (midfielder) — joined Borussia Mönchengladbach from Shimizu S-Pulse in 2026.
- **GOKE Yuta** (Vissel Kobe), **TAKEDA Hidetoshi** (Vegalta Sendai) and **FUJIWARA Yudai** (Albirex Niigata) — all first-team regulars in the J.League.
- **MIKUNI Kennedy Egbus** (defender) — a full-back turned centre-back now with Avispa Fukuoka.
- **MATSUDA Shun** (goalkeeper) — captain of the side at the 104th winter championship, and a professional with Fagiano Okayama from 2026.

The range is what stands out: goalkeepers, defenders, ball-winners and playmakers, rather than one type of player.
