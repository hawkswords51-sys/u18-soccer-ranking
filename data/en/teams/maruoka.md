## intro

Maruoka High School (丸岡高校, officially Fukui Prefectural Maruoka High School) is a public school in the Maruoka district of Sakai, a city in the north of Fukui Prefecture on the Sea of Japan coast. For decades its football club has been the standard-bearer for Fukui, one of the smaller football prefectures in the Hokushinetsu region. The club has played 34 times at the winter All Japan High School Soccer Tournament and 37 times at the summer Inter-High. Its best results are a place in the winter semi-finals in 1997–98 and the Inter-High quarter-finals in 1996. In 2020 it won the Prince League Hokushinetsu, the regional league for Niigata, Toyama, Ishikawa, Fukui and Nagano. In 2026 the team plays in Prince League Hokushinetsu Division 2. It won the Fukui Inter-High qualifying tournament for the seventh tournament running, then went out in the second round of the national finals.

## style

The club's motto, published on the school's website, is "humble, wholehearted and tenacious" (謙虚に・ひたむきに・粘り強く), and the squad commits to gratitude towards the people who support it and respect for team-mates. In a 2020 interview with the website Koko Soccer, the head coach summed up his approach as "doing the ordinary things properly", on the grounds that football and everyday life are connected. Tactically he said he does not tie the team to one fixed system. Instead he asks players to read situations for themselves, and he stresses speed in every sense: speed of decision, of control and passing, and of the switch between attack and defence. The team that reached the national semi-finals in 1997–98 is remembered for its resilience. It needed two penalty shoot-outs on the way, after a 1-1 draw with Hosho and a 0-0 draw with Numata, and then beat Takigawa Daini High School 1-0 in the quarter-final.

## history

- **1997–98** — The club's best run in the winter championship. Maruoka beats Hosho on penalties, Numata on penalties and Takigawa Daini High School 1-0 to reach the semi-finals, where it loses 1-3 to Higashi Fukuoka. Higashi Fukuoka go on to beat Teikyo in the final, which was played in snow and is still one of the tournament's best-remembered matches. Defender KIMURA Makoto, a member of that Maruoka team, later plays in the J.League for Kawasaki Frontale and Montedio Yamagata.
- **2014** — Ninth in the Prince League Hokushinetsu, and relegated to the Fukui prefectural league.
- **2019** — Back in the Prince League after promotion, Maruoka briefly tops the table as a newly promoted side and finishes third. In the winter championship it beats Nagasaki Sogo Kagaku Daigaku Fuzoku 3-2 before losing 0-3 to Shizuoka Gakuen High School, who go on to win the tournament.
- **2020** — Champions of the Prince League Hokushinetsu in a season reorganised because of the pandemic, with no promotion or relegation.
- **2021** — A hard season ends in tenth place and relegation from the Prince League.
- **2022** — At the Inter-High, Maruoka reaches the last 16 and loses 2-3 to Teikyo, conceding the winner deep into stoppage time.
- **2023** — The Prince League Hokushinetsu expands to two divisions, and Maruoka finishes third in Division 2. It wins the Fukui winter qualifying tournament for the sixth year running, but loses 0-3 to Saga Higashi in the first round of the national finals.
- **2024 and 2025** — Maruoka reaches the Fukui winter final in both years and loses both on penalties to Fukui Shogyo, ending its run of winter titles in the prefecture. It keeps winning the summer qualifying tournament.
- **2026** — A seventh tournament in a row as Fukui's Inter-High representative, sealed with a 2-0 win over Fukui Shogyo in the final. At the national finals Maruoka draws 1-1 with Setouchi and wins the shoot-out 7-6, then loses 1-4 to Yamagata Meisei High School in the second round (the last 32).

## model

For readers new to Japanese youth football: high-school teams and J.League academies play in the same national league pyramid. At the top is the U-18 Premier League (EAST and WEST), then nine regional Prince Leagues, then the prefectural leagues. Two high-school tournaments carry even more prestige than the league: the summer Inter-High and the winter championship, which is televised nationally and ends at the National Stadium in Tokyo in January. Each prefecture sends one or two teams to each tournament through its own qualifying competition. Japanese seasons follow the school year, so a winter championship such as "1997–98" starts in late December and finishes in January.

- **A public school in a private-school era.** Many of Japan's strongest school teams are private schools that recruit nationally and offer football scholarships. Maruoka is a prefectural school, and it has stayed at the top in Fukui for decades. Its 37 Inter-High appearances are a reminder of how consistent it has been.
- **The top school in a small prefecture.** Fukui is one of the smaller football prefectures, and Maruoka has long been its leading team. The club's main rival in recent years has been Fukui Shogyo, who beat Maruoka in the winter qualifying finals of 2024 and 2025.
- **Deep roots in the local game.** Maruoka calls itself a "football town" (サッカーのまち). Its local adult club, Sakai Phoenix, has given several former Maruoka players, including KIMURA Makoto, NATSUME Yuki and UMEI Daiki, a place to keep playing after their professional careers.
- **Through university or straight to the J.League.** Maruoka alumni have reached the professional game both ways: UMEI Daiki joined Yokohama F. Marinos straight from school, while KIMURA Makoto, HASHIMOTO Hayato and NATSUME Yuki all went via Komazawa University in Tokyo.

## alumni

- **HASHIMOTO Hayato** (midfielder, retired) — a midfielder who joined Omiya Ardija from Komazawa University in 2004 and stayed for ten seasons, playing more than 100 J1 matches. He then played in Thailand for Chonburi and in Malaysia for DRB-Hicom before retiring in 2015. After retiring he became a coach in Omiya's academy.
- **KIMURA Makoto** (defender, retired) — a member of the 1997–98 semi-final team. After Komazawa University he played for Kawasaki Frontale and Montedio Yamagata, then for Zweigen Kanazawa before returning home to play for Sakai Phoenix.
- **UMEI Daiki** (defender, former J.League player) — joined Yokohama F. Marinos from the school in 2008. He went on to play for Zaspa Kusatsu (now Thespa Gunma) on loan, Oita Trinita, Fukushima United and SC Sagamihara, where he spent five seasons. He later played in the regional leagues and joined Sakai Phoenix in 2023.
- **NATSUME Yuki** (forward, former J.League player) — a tall striker from Fukui who went from the school to Komazawa University and joined Kawasaki Frontale in 2011. He spent 2012 on loan at Tochigi SC and moved to Matsumoto Yamaga in 2014, before returning to Fukui to play for Sakai Phoenix.
