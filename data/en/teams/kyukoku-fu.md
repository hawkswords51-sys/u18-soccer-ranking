## intro

Kyushu International University Affiliated High School (九州国際大学付属高校, usually shortened to "Kyukoku" or 九国大付) is a private school in Yahatahigashi Ward, Kitakyushu, in the north of Fukuoka Prefecture. It opened in 1958 as the boys' high school attached to Yawata University, took its present name in 1989 when the university became Kyushu International University, and has admitted girls since 2010. An attached junior high school opened in 2009. Kyukoku is the leading football school in Kitakyushu, a city at the opposite end of the prefecture from Fukuoka City, where most of its traditional powers are based. It has played twice in the winter All Japan High School Soccer Tournament and three times at the summer Inter-High, and its best-known former player is Japan and Nagoya Grampus forward NAGAI Kensuke. In 2026 the team plays in Prince League Kyushu Division 2. It won the Fukuoka Inter-High qualifier and reached the last 16 of the national finals.

## style

The club's stated aim is to play football that captivates the people who come to watch, with players, parents, former players and coaches pulling together under the banner "TOTAL FOOTBALL". Its written principles put the emphasis on fundamentals: never giving up until the final whistle, mastering basic technique, looking after one's own nutrition, rest and equipment, and not being afraid to make mistakes. The 2026 season showed a team that is hard to score against. In the Fukuoka Inter-High qualifier it won all four matches without conceding: 1-0, 1-0, a 0-0 semi-final against Chikuyo Gakuen won on penalties, and a 3-0 final against Tokai University Fukuoka. At the All-Kyushu Championships in June it knocked out Kamimura Gakuen, the reigning national champions, on penalties after a 1-1 draw, before a 0-6 semi-final defeat by Nissho Gakuen High School. At the national Inter-High it beat Seiwa Gakuen (Miyagi) 1-0 and then lost 0-2 to Toin Gakuen High School in the round of 16.

## history

- **1958** — The school opens in Kitakyushu as the boys' division of Yawata University's attached high school.
- **1989** — The university is renamed Kyushu International University, and the school takes its present name.
- **2006–07** — Kyukoku wins the Fukuoka qualifier for the winter championship for the first time. At the 85th national tournament, with NAGAI Kensuke in attack, it beats Toyama Daiichi 5-0 in the first round and then loses 0-1 to Toko Gakuen High School.
- **2009–2010** — The attached junior high school opens in 2009, and the high school becomes co-educational in 2010.
- **2010–11** — The most famous match in the club's history. The Fukuoka final against Higashi Fukuoka, the prefecture's dominant school, ends 1-1 after extra time, and the shoot-out lasts 44 kicks before Kyukoku wins it 21-20. At the 89th national tournament the team beats Akita Shogyo 4-0 and goes out 1-2 in the second round. The same year Kyukoku finishes runner-up at the All-Kyushu Championships.
- **2013** — The Inter-High is held in northern Kyushu, which gives Fukuoka an extra place. Kyukoku qualifies for the first time and reaches the round of 16.
- **2015** — Second in the Prince League Kyushu, the club's best league finish.
- **2019** — Third in the Prince League Kyushu and runner-up at the All-Kyushu Championships.
- **2022** — Kyukoku wins the Fukuoka Inter-High qualifier for the first time, beating Iizuka High School 12-11 on penalties after a 1-1 draw in a final played in heavy rain. At the national finals it beats Kanto Daiichi 1-0 and then loses 0-4 to Shonan Institute of Technology High School in the second round.
- **2026** — A second Fukuoka Inter-High title, won with four clean sheets, sends Kyukoku to the national finals for the third time, four tournaments after the last. In June the team beats Kamimura Gakuen on penalties at the All-Kyushu Championships. At the Inter-High it enters in the second round, beats Seiwa Gakuen 1-0 to reach the third round for the first time since 2013, and loses 0-2 to Toin Gakuen High School in the round of 16.

## model

For readers new to Japanese youth football: high-school teams and J.League academies play in the same national league pyramid. At the top are the U-18 Premier League (EAST and WEST), then nine regional Prince Leagues, then the prefectural leagues. Kyushu's Prince League has two divisions. Two high-school tournaments carry even more prestige than the league: the summer Inter-High and the winter championship, which is televised nationally and ends at the National Stadium in January. Each prefecture usually sends one team to each, so the prefectural qualifiers matter a great deal.

- **A Kitakyushu school in a crowded prefecture.** Fukuoka is one of the hardest prefectures in Japan to qualify from. Higashi Fukuoka High School has won the national championship several times, and Tokai University Fukuoka, Chikuyo Gakuen and Iizuka are all strong. The prefecture also has two J.League academies, Avispa Fukuoka U-18 in Fukuoka City and the academy of Giravanz Kitakyushu in Kyukoku's own city. Kyukoku's national appearances have mostly come after long, close knockout runs, including two finals settled on penalties.
- **Local players.** Many of the club's best-known players came from Kitakyushu and the surrounding area: NAGAI Kensuke from Asakawa Junior High School in Kitakyushu, and MATSUYAMA Kenta from Shiida Junior High School in eastern Fukuoka. The captain of the 2022 Inter-High team came from a local junior-high school side.
- **University links.** The club trains on Kyushu International University's artificial-turf pitch as well as on the school's own ground.
- **A route to the professional game through university.** Kyukoku players have more often turned professional after university than straight from school. NAGAI Kensuke and YAMASHITA Keita both went on to Fukuoka University, and MATSUYAMA Kenta to Momoyama Gakuin University, before joining J.League clubs.

## alumni

- **NAGAI Kensuke** (forward, Nagoya Grampus) — the star of the 2006–07 team that reached the winter championship for the first time. He went on to Fukuoka University and joined Nagoya Grampus in 2011. After a spell with Standard Liège in Belgium in 2013, he returned to Nagoya, then played for FC Tokyo from 2017, winning the 2020 J.League YBC Levain Cup and being named in the 2019 J1 Best XI. He moved back to Nagoya in 2022, where he still plays. He has won 12 caps for Japan and played at the 2012 London Olympics, where Japan finished fourth.
- **YAMASHITA Keita** (forward, Tochigi City) — went from Kyukoku to Fukuoka University and has played for Renofa Yamaguchi, JEF United Chiba, Sagan Tosu and FC Tokyo, among others. He joined Tochigi City on a permanent transfer in January 2026 and renewed his contract for the 2026–27 J2 season.
- **MATSUYAMA Kenta** (goalkeeper, Tegevajaro Miyazaki) — went to Momoyama Gakuin University after Kyukoku and turned professional with Iwate Grulla Morioka. He has since played for Kagoshima United, Mito HollyHock, Urawa Reds and Avispa Fukuoka, and joined Tegevajaro Miyazaki on a permanent transfer in June 2026.
