## intro

Oita High School (大分高校, usually just "Oita" or "Oita-ko") is a private, co-educational school in Oita City, the capital of Oita Prefecture on the island of Kyushu. It has an attached junior high school, and the school itself opened in 1952. It should not be confused with Oita Trinita U-18, the academy of the local J.League club, or with the public school Oita Tsurusaki. Oita is one of the prefecture's most successful school teams: it has played in the winter All Japan High School Soccer Tournament 12 times and reached the semi-finals in 2011–12, and it has 14 Inter-High appearances, with four quarter-final finishes. Its best-known recent graduate is Avispa Fukuoka midfielder SHIGEMI Masato. In 2026 the team plays in the Oita prefectural league, OFA League Division 1, after dropping out of the Prince League Kyushu at the end of 2024. In the summer it won the prefectural Inter-High qualifier, then won a penalty shoot-out in the first round of the national finals before going out in the second round.

## style

For a long period up to 2016 the team was coached by a Korean-born teacher whose ideas still shape the club's reputation. His approach became known in Japan as "Freeman football" (フリーマンサッカー). Players are not fixed to a position in a system: the coach studies each player's strengths and changes the shape of the team to fit the players he has. Players are expected to read the game and make decisions for themselves. In one well-known example from the 2011–12 winter championship, Oita's players did not go back to the dressing room at half-time, keeping to that routine even at the National Stadium. Oita has usually not had the recruiting power of the biggest private schools in Kyushu, so this way of developing players has been central to how it competes.

When Oita reached the winter championship in 2022–23, the coach at the time described the team's football as attacking, possession-based football built on passing. The 2026 team showed both sides of a school side that has dropped out of regional league football. It won the prefectural Inter-High qualifier with five wins and 23 goals, conceding one, but in the national finals it lost 0-8 to Shizuoka Gakuen High School.

## history

- **1952** — The school opens in Oita City. (The football club's own founding year is not confirmed by the school, so it is not given here.)
- **1998–99** — Oita reaches the quarter-finals of the Inter-High in two years running.
- **2000–2003** — Forward UCHIMURA Yoshihiro plays in three winter championships in a row, the 79th, 80th and 81st editions. He joins Oita Trinita in 2003, the start of a long professional career.
- **2003, 2005** — Two more Inter-High quarter-finals, the club's best summer result.
- **2011–12** — The club's best season. At the 90th winter championship Oita beats Hokuriku 10-0 in the first round and goes on to reach the semi-finals at the National Stadium in Tokyo. There it loses 1-2 to Ichiritsu Funabashi, who go on to win the title. In Japan there is no third-place play-off, so both losing semi-finalists are counted as third.
- **2016** — The long-serving Korean-born head coach steps down at the end of the year.
- **2018–19 and 2019–20** — Back-to-back winter championship appearances. Midfielder SHIGEMI Masato, who came up through the attached junior high school, plays in both.
- **2022–23** — Another winter championship appearance, at the 101st edition.
- **2023** — Oita plays in Prince League Kyushu Division 2, the second tier of the regional league. It finishes last of seven teams.
- **2024** — Oita finishes tenth and last in Prince League Kyushu Division 2, with three wins from 18 matches, and is relegated to the Oita prefectural league.
- **2025** — The first season back in the prefectural league.
- **2026** — In February the team plays at the Kyushu U-17 tournament, beating Naha Nishi and Shugakukan but losing to Kamimura Gakuen. In May and June it wins the prefectural Inter-High qualifier. It beats Yoshikan 7-0, Usa 9-0, Oita Technical 2-1 and Yanagigaura 4-0, then Nakatsu Higashi 1-0 in the final on 1 June. That earns its first Inter-High place in five editions, and its 14th in all. At the Kyushu High School Championship in June it loses 2-3 to Tokai University Fukuoka in the first round. At the Inter-High, held in Fukushima Prefecture, it draws 1-1 with Rissho University Shonan High School on 25 July and wins the shoot-out 7-6, a shoot-out that lasts nine kicks each and in which the goalkeeper saves two penalties. It is the school's first win in the opening round of the Inter-High in seven years. The next day it loses 0-8 to Shizuoka Gakuen High School in the second round, the last 32.

## model

For readers new to Japanese youth football: high-school teams and J.League academies play in the same national league pyramid. At the top is the U-18 Premier League (EAST and WEST). Below it are nine regional Prince Leagues; Kyushu's has two divisions. Below those are the prefectural leagues. Two knock-out tournaments for school teams carry even more prestige than the league. One is the summer Inter-High. The other is the winter championship, which is televised nationally and ends in Tokyo in January. Japanese school years run from April to March, so a winter championship that starts in late December and ends in January belongs to one school year. That is why it is written here as, for example, "2011–12".

- **A school side in a J.League prefecture.** Oita Trinita's academy takes many of the prefecture's best young players. Oita High School competes with it and with strong school rivals such as Nakatsu Higashi, Yanagigaura and Oita Tsurusaki, largely by developing players rather than by recruiting ready-made stars.
- **Six years with one club.** The attached junior high school has its own football team, and some of its players move up to the high-school squad. SHIGEMI Masato is the clearest example: he went from the junior high school to the high school, then to Fukuoka University, then into the J1 League.
- **Players who think for themselves.** The "Freeman" approach expects players to change position and make decisions on the pitch without waiting for instructions from the bench.
- **Back in the prefectural league.** Since relegation from the Prince League Kyushu at the end of 2024, Oita has played in OFA League Division 1, the top division of the Oita prefectural league. Winning it gives a chance of promotion back to the regional league.
- **A route to university and the professional game.** Graduates have gone straight to J.League clubs, as UCHIMURA Yoshihiro did, or via university football, as SHIGEMI Masato did.

## alumni

- **SHIGEMI Masato** (midfielder, Avispa Fukuoka) — he came up through Oita Junior High School and Oita High School and played in the 2018–19 and 2019–20 winter championships. He then went to Fukuoka University. In 2023, while still a student, he won a silver medal with Japan's U-22 team at the Asian Games in Hangzhou. He made his J1 debut for Avispa Fukuoka as a special designated player that year and joined the club as a professional in 2024. He wears number 6 for Fukuoka in 2026.
- **UCHIMURA Yoshihiro** (forward, retired) — played in three winter championships for Oita (2000–01 to 2002–03), then joined Oita Trinita in 2003. He later played for Ehime FC and spent nine seasons, from 2009 to 2018, as a forward at Consadole Sapporo. He finished his career at FC Imabari and announced his retirement in February 2020.
