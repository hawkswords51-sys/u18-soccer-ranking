## intro

Ehime FC U-18 is the under-18 team of Ehime FC, the professional club of Matsuyama on the island of Shikoku. It is older than the professional club it feeds: the club founded its youth team in 1994, twelve years before Ehime FC entered the J.League in 2006. It is the strongest academy on Shikoku. It has won the Prince League Shikoku, the regional second tier of Japanese youth football, more often than any other team, and has played three seasons in the national Premier League WEST (2011, 2012 and 2019). In 2026 it leads the Prince League Shikoku again, with 12 wins and one defeat from its first 13 matches, and its aim is the same as in each of the last few seasons: to win the region and then win a play-off for a place back in the Premier League.

## style

The club's academy has had a friendship agreement with the German club SC Freiburg since 2008, a link that grew out of the twinning of Matsuyama and Freiburg, and the two clubs exchange coaches. Freiburg is known in Germany for producing its own players, and the Ehime academy uses the relationship to learn how a club outside the big cities can do the same. The club's own stated aim is to develop players who can play for the first team, for Japan and abroad, and also people who can contribute to society. On the pitch, the results speak for the gap between Ehime and the rest of the region: the 2025 title was settled with two matches to spare, and the 2026 team has dropped points only once in its first 13 league matches.

Japanese youth football is organised as a pyramid. At the top is the Premier League, two national divisions of twelve (EAST and WEST) whose winners meet in a final. Below it are nine regional Prince Leagues, and below those the prefectural leagues. The Prince League winners, and some runners-up, do not go up automatically: they enter a knockout play-off in December, and only the play-off winners are promoted. This is why Ehime have won their region seven times in the list above but have been promoted only twice. The play-offs have gone against them in most recent years: in 2024 they lost in the first round, and in December 2025, after winning the league with two matches to spare, they drew 0-0 with Shimizu S-Pulse Youth after extra time and lost 3-1 on penalties.

## history

- **1994** — The club, then playing in the regional amateur leagues, founds its youth team. A junior-youth (under-15) team follows in 1997.
- **1997** — In the 77th Emperor's Cup, Japan's open national cup, Ehime FC Youth beat the adult side Arrows Hokuriku 2-1 after extra time in the second round. It was the first time a team registered in the youth (high-school age) category had beaten an adult team in the competition.
- **2006** — Ehime FC enters the J.League (J2). The academy is now the youth system of a professional club.
- **2008** — Friendship agreement with SC Freiburg.
- **2009–2010** — Two Prince League Shikoku titles in a row.
- **2011–2012** — Two seasons in the newly created Premier League WEST: seventh in the first, last in the second and relegated.
- **2011** — A second junior-youth team is founded in Niihama, in the east of Ehime prefecture, and a partnership begins with St. Catherine University in Matsuyama.
- **2013** — Prince League Shikoku champions.
- **2018** — Champions again, and this time the play-off is won. In the same year KAWAI Kenta, a graduate of the youth team, becomes head coach of the first team; the club describes him as the first academy graduate to manage a J.League club's first team.
- **2019** — A third Premier League season ends in relegation. A dormitory for the under-18s is completed, allowing the club to recruit players from outside the prefecture.
- **2022** — Prince League Shikoku champions.
- **2024–2025** — Back-to-back regional titles; both play-offs are lost. In 2025 SHIMA Yusei moves directly from the under-18 squad to Hammarby in Sweden.
- **2026** — The team leads the Prince League Shikoku after 13 matches, with 36 points.

## model

- **A club built from Matsuyama outwards.** The academy has two junior-youth teams, in Matsuyama and Niihama, which supply the under-18s alongside players recruited at fifteen. Since 2019 the under-18 dormitory has allowed the club to take players from all over western Japan; recent graduates include players who joined at fifteen from the Vissel Kobe and Renofa Yamaguchi academies.
- **School and football together.** Unlike the high-school teams that dominate Japanese youth football, a club academy does not play in the Inter-High (the national high-school tournament in summer) or the winter All Japan High School Championship. Its players attend an ordinary school and train with the club in the evenings. The club has had a partnership with St. Catherine Gakuen High School in Matsuyama since 2016, and graduates such as ONO Narumu studied there while playing for the under-18s.
- **The club-youth competitions.** Instead of the high-school tournaments, academies play the Japan Club Youth (U-18) Championship and the J.League Youth Cup alongside their league. The Club Youth Championship has traditionally been held in summer; for 2026 it has been moved to the winter, with the finals from 23 to 29 December. The Shikoku qualifying rounds are played from late October into November, so Ehime's 2026 result in that competition is not yet known. In 2025 the team reached the national finals of the Club Youth Championship.
- **Several routes to a professional career.** Some graduates are promoted straight to the Ehime first team; others go to university first, often in the Kansai region, and turn professional at 22; and the most recent example went from the under-18 squad straight to a European club.

## alumni

- **KAWAI Kenta** (head coach, Hokkaido Consadole Sapporo) — played for Ehime FC Youth while at school in Matsuyama, then went to Momoyama Gakuin University and played for Ehime FC as a forward from 2004 to 2006. He managed the Ehime first team from 2018 to 2020 and Sagan Tosu in the J1 League from 2022 to 2024, and was appointed head coach of Hokkaido Consadole Sapporo for 2026.
- **SHIMA Yusei** (defender, Schwarz-Weiß Bregenz) — came through the Vissel Kobe academy and joined Ehime FC U-18 in 2022. He played for Japan's under-17s, was registered with the Ehime first team in 2025 while still at school, and in June 2025, as a third-year high-school student, moved permanently to Hammarby IF in Sweden. In July 2026 he joined Schwarz-Weiß Bregenz in Austria.
- **YUKUTOMO Toki** (midfielder, Ehime FC) — joined the under-18s from the Renofa Yamaguchi academy, was promoted to the first team, and spent time on loan at FC Famalicão in Portugal at the age of eighteen before returning to Ehime.
- **SONEDA Yutaka** (midfielder, Ehime FC) — came through the club's junior-youth and youth teams, went to Biwako Seikei Sport College, and played for Ventforet Kofu, Kyoto Sanga and Mito HollyHock before returning to Ehime, where he wears the number 7.
- **MAKIGUCHI Kazuma** (goalkeeper, Ehime FC) — joined the under-18s from the Vissel Kobe academy, was promoted to the first team, and spent 2025 on loan at Cerezo Osaka.
- **ONO Narumu** (defender, Shimizu S-Pulse from 2027) — came through the club's under-15 and under-18 teams and was the most valuable player of the 2022 Prince League Shikoku, which Ehime won. He went on to captain Kyoto Sangyo University, and in June 2026 Shimizu S-Pulse announced that he will join them in January 2027.
