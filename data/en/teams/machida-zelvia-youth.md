## intro

FC Machida Zelvia Youth is the under-18 team of FC Machida Zelvia, the club from the south-western suburbs of Tokyo that reached the J1 League for the first time in 2024 and won the Emperor's Cup in November 2025. The academy is older than the professional club that now carries it. Football in Machida was organised from the youngest age group upwards — first a selection of primary-school players, then a junior-youth team, then the under-18s — and the adult team that became today's J.League side was only formed in 1989. The youth team has a national final in its past, but for most of the last decade it has been climbing the Tokyo league ladder. In 2026 it is playing its first season in the Prince League, the regional tier directly below the national Premier League, and after 12 of its 18 matches it is second in Prince League Kanto Division 2.

## style

The academy states its aim as sending children "out into the world by way of Machida", and its philosophy, which the club says it inherited from its late founder, is the pursuit of beautiful, creative football. Player development is built on three words the club publishes in both languages: **skilful** (able to change direction freely), **clever** (able to read the flow of a game) and **tough** (willing to fight to win). On the pitch the academy asks for attacking football based on possession, under four principles: control the opponent and the space; attack and defend as one unit; play collectively; and care about winning.

Support follows the first team's model. The first team's registered dietitian provides balanced meals straight after training, and a partner orthopaedic and rehabilitation clinic gives academy players access to the same kind of equipment the professionals use, with the emphasis on preventing injuries and returning from them early.

Current players are not named on this page, because none of the 2026 squad yet has an official English spelling of his name from the JFA, the J.LEAGUE or the club.

To understand these lines, it helps to know how Japanese youth football is organised. Club academies such as Machida's do not take part in the two great school tournaments — the summer Inter-High and the winter All Japan High School Championship — which are reserved for school teams. Their national cups are the **Japan Club Youth (U-18) Championship**, held every summer for club teams only, and the **J Youth Cup**, an autumn knock-out for J.League academies. All teams, school and club, meet in the league pyramid: the national **Premier League** (EAST and WEST) at the top, nine regional **Prince Leagues** below it, and the prefectural leagues — in Tokyo, T1, T2, T3 and T4 — below those.

The 1990 final was against Yomiuri Youth, the academy of the club that is today Tokyo Verdy, then the dominant force in Japanese club football; in 1993 Yomiuri won the competition again, with Machida one of the two beaten semi-finalists. Machida's junior-youth team had reached a national final of its own, in the Club Junior Youth Championship, in 1988.

## history

- **1970s** — Machida's local football association begins selecting the best primary-school players in the city for a training squad. A junior-youth team (ages 12–15) follows as those players grow up.
- **1986** — According to the Machida Football Association's own history, the youth team is formed in the autumn by 15 local high-school pupils who had grown up in Machida's boys' clubs and did not play for their schools' teams.
- **1989** — An adult team is formed, completing a pyramid built from the bottom up. That team eventually becomes FC Machida Zelvia.
- **1990** — Runners-up in the Japan Club Youth (U-18) Championship, losing the final to Yomiuri Youth.
- **1993** — Third place in the same competition.
- **2014** — The first youth-team graduate to be promoted since the club turned professional, a goalkeeper, joins the first team.
- **2015** — The team comes through the Kanto qualifying round — including a 1-1 draw with Ventforet Kofu U-18 — and reaches the national finals of the Club Youth Championship for the first time under the Zelvia name.
- **2017** — HASHIMURA Ryu Joseph, a second-year youth player, is called up by Japan's under-17 team for a tour of the United States.
- **2025** — While the first team wins the Emperor's Cup, the under-18s win the Tokyo T1 League for the first time, clinching the title on 16 November with a 2-0 win over Kokushikan. On 22 December they beat Tokyo Seitoku University Fukaya 4-2 in the deciding play-off match to win promotion.
- **2026** — A first season in the Prince League. After losing heavily to Yokohama F. Marinos Youth on the opening day, the team has won seven of its 12 league matches, including the last four in a row, and stands second in Prince League Kanto Division 2.

## model

- **Built from below.** The pathway runs from the club's football school, through the junior-youth team (ages 12–15), into the under-18s. All three of the most recent graduates to turn professional followed exactly that route, and the club's academy goals put producing first-team players first, alongside results and a balance between football and schoolwork.
- **Two teams, two leagues.** The first team of the under-18s plays in the Prince League. A second team, FC Machida Zelvia Youth B, plays in the Tokyo T3 League, so that players outside the first eleven still play competitive league football every weekend. In 2026 the B team has won all eight of its T3 matches so far.
- **A close link to the first team.** Academy players eat meals planned by the first team's dietitian and use the same medical partner. Promising youth players can be registered with the first team while still at school, as HIGUCHI Ken was in 2021, and graduates were promoted to the first team for 2022 and 2025.
- **The 2026 season so far.** In the league: defeats by Yokohama F. Marinos Youth (0-5), Nittaidai Kashiwa (1-2), Toko Gakuen High School (0-1), Seibudai (1-3) and Kashima Gakuen High School (0-3); wins over Nihon University Fujisawa (twice, both 2-0), Tochigi SC U-18 (2-1 and 2-0), Yaita Chuo High School (4-2), Kashima Antlers Youth B (2-1) and Nittaidai Kashiwa (1-0 in the return match). In the J Youth Cup the team went out in the first round in May, 0-4 to Mitsubishi Yowa SC Youth. In February it finished third in the Tokyo Club Youth U-17 Championship, beating Yokogawa Musashino FC U-18 on penalties in the third-place match.

## alumni

Only players who played for the under-18 team are listed. Several well-known professionals who appear on lists of "Machida products" either left after the junior-youth team to play for a high school, or came from a different Machida club altogether, and are not included.

- **HIGUCHI Ken** (midfielder, Y.S.C.C. Yokohama) — came through Machida's junior-youth and youth teams, was registered with the first team while still a youth player in 2021 and promoted for 2022. After two loan spells at Okinawa SV he joined Y.S.C.C. Yokohama in January 2026.
- **MAYAKA Chui Hiromu** (midfielder, FC Machida Zelvia) — a left-footed midfielder from Machida's school, junior-youth and youth teams, promoted to the first team for 2025. Since June 2026 he has been on a development loan at SHIBUYA CITY FC in the Kanto Soccer League.
- **AOKI Yoshitaka** (defender, Kagoshima United FC) — joined Machida's junior-youth team from a Hachioji club, played for the youth team, then went to Takushoku University before returning to Machida as a professional. Loans at ReinMeer Aomori and V-Varen Nagasaki followed, and in 2026 he plays for Kagoshima United.
- **HASHIMURA Ryu Joseph** (forward) — registered with the first team as a 16-year-old youth player in 2017, the year he played for Japan's under-17s; he stayed with Machida's first team until 2020.

The academy has also produced the first team's first homegrown promotion of the professional era, a goalkeeper who joined the senior squad in 2014. A club that started with its children has now put a team of its own teenagers into the Prince League.
