## intro

Setouchi High School (瀬戸内高校, often written "Hiroshima Setouchi" to tell it apart from other schools of the same name) is a private school in Hiroshima City, run by the Matsumoto Gakuen educational foundation. Its football club is one of the strongest in Hiroshima Prefecture. On its first appearance at the winter All Japan High School Soccer Tournament, in 2018–19, it reached the semi-finals. It won the regional Prince League Chugoku in 2013 and has played in that league every season since at least 2010. Its best-known former player is ABE Hiroki, who went from the school to Kashima Antlers, was named the J.LEAGUE Best Young Player in 2018 and later played for FC Barcelona's reserve team. In 2026 Setouchi won the Hiroshima Inter-High qualifying tournament for the third year in a row, and in the Prince League Chugoku it was third after 14 matches, with nine wins, one draw and four defeats (28 points).

## style

The current head coach, a former J.League player who took over in 2025, sums up the team's approach as "good attacking play comes from good defending". He has concentrated on building a solid defensive structure while giving the team several different ways to attack. After the 2026 Inter-High he admitted that the side still lacked a "standout weapon" to go further at national level. The league table backs up the first half of that description: after 14 rounds of the 2026 Prince League Chugoku, Setouchi had scored 31 goals, the third-highest total in the division, and conceded 16. In the Hiroshima Inter-High qualifiers it was even more convincing, beating its old rival Hiroshima Minami 4-1 in the semi-final and Numata 6-0 in the final.

The club is large. Its official site lists 93 members in 2026: 28 third-years, 32 second-years, 30 first-years and three managers. As well as the first team in the Prince League, it enters a second team in the Hiroshima prefectural league Division 1, a third team in Division 2, and its first-years in the Chugoku Rookie League. Training takes place at the Matsumoto Gakuen Sports Center in the neighbouring town of Fuchu (Aki District), which has an artificial-turf pitch, as well as at the school ground.

## history

- **Before 2018** — For years Setouchi was the nearly team of Hiroshima football. It repeatedly reached the final of the prefectural qualifying tournament for the winter championship and repeatedly lost to Hiroshima Minami, the prefecture's most successful school team.
- **2010–2012** — Setouchi plays in the top division of the Prince League Chugoku, the regional league for the five prefectures of western Honshu, finishing between fourth and sixth.
- **2013** — The Prince League Chugoku is reorganised into a single division, and Setouchi wins its first season.
- **2016** — The Inter-High is held in Hiroshima, and Setouchi takes part as the prefecture's second representative. It beats Ichiritsu Nagano 5-1, Kindai Wakayama on penalties after a 0-0 draw, and wins its third-round match 4-0, before losing the quarter-final 1-2 to Ichiritsu Funabashi, who go on to win the title. ABE Hiroki, a member of that team, joins Kashima Antlers in 2017.
- **2017** — Second in the Prince League Chugoku.
- **2018–19** — The breakthrough. Setouchi finally qualifies for the winter championship, the 97th edition, and goes all the way to the semi-finals. It beats Tokyo City University Shiojiri 1-0 in its opening match in the second round, Okayama Gakugeikan 2-1 in the third round and Nihon Koku 1-0 in the quarter-final. In the semi-final it is beaten 0-5 by RKU Kashiwa. Third place is shared with Shoshi, as there is no third-place match.
- **2021–22** — A second winter appearance, at the 100th edition. Setouchi draws 0-0 with Shoshi in the first round and loses 3-4 on penalties. In the league it is again second in the Prince League Chugoku.
- **2022–2025** — Fourth in the league in 2022, 2023 and 2024, and fifth in 2025. After losing the Hiroshima Inter-High final in 2023, Setouchi wins it in 2024 and again in 2025, when it beats Hiroshima Minami 3-2 in the semi-final and Hiroshima Shoyo 2-0 in the final. At the 2025 Inter-High it loses its first-round match to Samukawa (Kagawa) 3-4 on penalties after a 0-0 draw. A former J.League player becomes head coach the same year.
- **2026** — A third straight Hiroshima Inter-High title, sealed with a 4-1 semi-final win over Hiroshima Minami and a 6-0 final against Numata. At the national tournament on 25 July, Setouchi draws 1-1 with Maruoka in the first round and goes out 6-7 on penalties. In the Prince League Chugoku it is third after 14 of its 18 rounds, behind Okayama Gakugeikan and Rissho University Shonan. In July, ABE Hiroki moves from Urawa Reds to FC Imabari.

## model

For readers new to Japanese youth football: high-school teams and J.League academies play in the same national league pyramid. At the top is the U-18 Premier League (EAST and WEST), then nine regional Prince Leagues, then the prefectural leagues. Two high-school tournaments carry even more prestige than the league: the summer Inter-High and the winter championship, which is televised nationally and ends at the National Stadium in Tokyo in January. Japanese school years start in April, so a winter tournament that ends in January belongs to the previous year's season — hence "2018–19".

- **A private school in a prefecture of public-school giants.** For decades Hiroshima high-school football was dominated by public schools, above all Hiroshima Minami. Setouchi's rise to the top of the prefecture, and its 2018–19 run to the national semi-finals, made it one of the private schools that now share power in Hiroshima, alongside rivals such as Hiroshima Kokusai Gakuin.
- **Sharing a region with a J.League academy.** Hiroshima is also home to Sanfrecce Hiroshima, whose academy is one of the best in Japan. Setouchi's own alumni include players who came through Sanfrecce's junior-youth team and others who came from Renofa Yamaguchi's under-15 side in the neighbouring prefecture, before moving to a school team rather than a club U-18 team.
- **A big squad and several teams.** With more than 90 players, the club enters teams at three levels of the league pyramid at once, so that players who are not in the Prince League side still play competitive league football every weekend.
- **Professional facilities.** The club trains at its own sports center in Fuchu, with an artificial-turf pitch, close to the school.
- **Two routes to the professional game.** ABE Hiroki went straight from Setouchi to the J.League. More recent alumni, including ARIYOSHI Hayato and SANO Ryoma, turned professional after four years of university football.

## alumni

- **ABE Hiroki** (midfielder, FC Imabari) — the club's most famous player. He grew up in Tokyo, played for the S.T.FC junior club, and came to Setouchi for high school. He joined Kashima Antlers in 2017 and was named the J.LEAGUE Best Young Player for 2018. In 2019 he was called up to the Japan senior squad for the Copa América and moved to FC Barcelona, where he played for the reserve team. He returned to the J.League with Urawa Reds in July 2023, and after leaving Urawa at the end of his contract he joined FC Imabari on a permanent transfer in July 2026.
- **ARIYOSHI Hayato** (defender, Zweigen Kanazawa) — a 183 cm centre-back from Yamaguchi Prefecture who came to Setouchi from Renofa Yamaguchi's under-15 team. After Setouchi he played for Konan University and joined Zweigen Kanazawa in the J3 League.
- **SANO Ryoma** (forward, Kamatamare Sanuki) — went from Setouchi to Osaka University of Health and Sport Sciences and joined Kamatamare Sanuki of the J3 League. He made his J.League debut in August 2026, at the start of the 2026–27 season.
