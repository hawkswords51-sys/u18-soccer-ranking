## intro

Kashima Gakuen High School (鹿島学園) is a private school in Kashima, a coastal city in the south-east of Ibaraki Prefecture, best known abroad as the home of the J.League club Kashima Antlers. The school opened in 1989. Its football team is a school (high-school federation) side and has no connection to Kashima Antlers Youth, the professional club's own U-18 team, although some Antlers Youth players study at the school. The team became a national force in the 2000s: it reached the semi-finals of the winter All Japan High School Soccer Tournament in 2008–09, and in 2025–26 it went all the way to the final, losing to Kamimura Gakuen in front of 60,142 spectators, a record for the tournament. Its best-known former player is Japan striker UEDA Ayase, who scored twice at the 2026 World Cup. In 2026 the team plays in Prince League Kanto Division 2 and reached the round of 16 at the Inter-High.

## style

The current head coach arrived in 2001 at the age of 25 and has led the team for a quarter of a century. He has said that he began with a hard, old-fashioned approach, with practice sessions of up to six hours. Annual trips to Spain changed him. The team has gone to Spain every year since 2002, and the coach saw young Spanish players reading the game for themselves and adapting without instructions from the bench. Since then he has coached through questions and dialogue rather than orders, asking players to explain their decisions on the pitch. Spanish influence runs through the programme: in 2018 the school's football organisation became Villarreal CF's first affiliate academy in Japan. The 2026 Inter-High showed the team's resilience. After a 4-1 win over Unebi (Nara) and a 2-1 win over Niigata Meikun, Kashima Gakuen went 0-2 down to Shizuoka Gakuen High School in the round of 16 and came back to 2-2 with two second-half goals. It then lost the penalty shoot-out 3-5, and Shizuoka Gakuen went on to win the tournament.

## history

- **1989** — Kashima Gakuen High School opens in Kashima, Ibaraki.
- **2001** — The current head coach joins the school as a teacher and takes charge of the football team, which has not yet played in a national tournament.
- **2002** — The first of the annual training trips to Spain, which have continued every year since.
- **2003** — Kashima Gakuen wins the Ibaraki qualifiers for both the Kanto tournament and the summer Inter-High for the first time, and plays at the Inter-High for the first time.
- **2004–05** — First appearance at the winter championship (the 83rd edition), the start of three appearances in a row. In 2005–06 the team reaches the quarter-finals, and in 2005 it is also runner-up at the Kanto tournament.
- **2008–09** — The club's best run until 2025. After beating Ohzu High School in the quarter-finals, Kashima Gakuen loses 0-1 in the semi-finals to Hiroshima Minami, the eventual champions. In the summer of 2008 the team also reaches the round of 16 at the Inter-High.
- **2010–12** — Three winter championship appearances in a row.
- **2013 and 2017** — The school builds an artificial-turf football ground (2013) and an indoor training hall (2017).
- **2016** — With UEDA Ayase in the team, Kashima Gakuen reaches the round of 16 at the Inter-High and plays at the winter championship.
- **2018** — The school's football organisation signs an agreement in June to become Villarreal CF's first affiliate academy in Japan, formalising a relationship built on the Spain trips.
- **2020–22** — Three winter championship appearances in a row, and Inter-High appearances in 2021 and 2022. In December 2021 the team wins the last place in the new Prince League Kanto Division 1 through the play-off, beating Ventforet Kofu U-18 1-0 and Kanto Daiichi 6-1.
- **2022–25** — Four seasons in Prince League Kanto Division 1 (eighth, eighth, seventh and seventh). Although it finished seventh of ten in 2025, the team was placed in Division 2 for 2026 once the national Premier League's promotions and relegations had been settled.
- **2025–26** — The breakthrough season. Kashima Gakuen wins the Ibaraki final 3-1 against Meishu Hitachi to qualify for the winter championship for the 12th time, its first appearance in three years. In Tokyo it beats Nitta (Ehime) 7-0, Kanazawa Gakuin University High School 1-0, Horikoshi 4-1 and Kokoku 3-1, then knocks out Ryutsu Keizai University Kashiwa 1-0 in the semi-final with a goal in the 90th minute. The final against Kamimura Gakuen, on 12 January 2026, draws 60,142 spectators, the first crowd above 60,000 in the tournament's history. Kamimura Gakuen win 3-0 to complete a summer and winter double.
- **2026** — The team plays in Prince League Kanto Division 2. It qualifies for its 12th Inter-High, held in Fukushima, and reaches the round of 16 before losing on penalties to Shizuoka Gakuen High School, the eventual champions. In August the club announces the return of a former general director (sōkantoku), who played for Teikyo High School, Chuo University and Fujita Industries (now Shonan Bellmare).

## model

For readers new to Japanese youth football: high-school teams and J.League academies play in the same national league pyramid. At the top are the U-18 Premier League (EAST and WEST), then nine regional Prince Leagues, then the prefectural leagues. Two high-school tournaments carry even more prestige than the league: the summer Inter-High and the winter championship, which is televised nationally and ends at the National Stadium in January.

- **A school side in an Antlers town.** Kashima is a small city dominated by one of Japan's most successful professional clubs. The school accepts players from Kashima Antlers Youth as students, but they play for the Antlers, not for the school. Kashima Gakuen's own team recruits separately, from junior-high school and junior club teams across Japan, and in recent years has also taken players from overseas, including a goalkeeper from Thailand.
- **A Spanish connection.** Few Japanese school teams have such a long overseas link. The team has travelled to Spain every year since 2002, and since 2018 the school's football organisation has been an affiliate academy of Villarreal CF, with coaching exchanges and staff education.
- **Facilities on campus.** The school has its own artificial-turf ground, opened in 2013, and an indoor training hall, opened in 2017. The surrounding area has many good grounds and strong opponents, which the club says lets it play high-level matches without long journeys.
- **Squad depth.** Like many big Japanese school clubs, Kashima Gakuen fields several teams at once. In 2025, besides the first team in the Prince League, its B, C and D teams played in the first, second and third divisions of the Ibaraki prefectural league.
- **Different routes to the professional game.** Former players have reached the J.League and beyond mostly through university: UEDA Ayase (Hosei University), NISHIYA Kazuki (Ryutsu Keizai University), NISHIYA Yuki (Sendai University, then Germany), DOI Koki (Kanto Gakuin University) and SAKURAI Ryo (Hosei University).

## alumni

- **UEDA Ayase** (forward, LOSC Lille) — joined the school from the Kashima Antlers junior-youth team, which had not promoted him to its U-18 side, and played for Kashima Gakuen from 2014 to 2016. He went on to Hosei University and turned professional with Kashima Antlers in 2019, then played for Cercle Brugge in Belgium and, from 2023, Feyenoord in the Netherlands. In 2025–26 he was the Eredivisie's top scorer with 25 goals. At the 2026 World Cup he wore number 18 for Japan and scored twice in a 4-0 win over Tunisia, his first World Cup goals. On 31 August 2026 he moved to Lille in France's Ligue 1.
- **NISHIYA Kazuki** (forward, Tochigi City) — played for the school before going to Ryutsu Keizai University. He has played for Tochigi SC, Tokushima Vortis and Zweigen Kanazawa, and is now with Tochigi City.
- **NISHIYA Yuki** (midfielder, Zweigen Kanazawa) — Kazuki's twin brother, who also played for the school. After Sendai University he played in the lower divisions in Germany and then returned to Japan with Zweigen Kanazawa.
- **DOI Koki** (midfielder, Blaublitz Akita) — went from the school to Kanto Gakuin University and turned professional with Vanraure Hachinohe in 2025. He moved to Blaublitz Akita on a permanent transfer in August 2025.
- **SAKURAI Ryo** (defender, Hosei University / Tochigi SC) — a former Omiya Ardija U-15 player who came through Kashima Gakuen and is now at Hosei University. In July 2026 Tochigi SC announced that he will join the club in January 2027. He is registered as a JFA/J.League special designated player, so he can already play for Tochigi while at university.

The 2026 squad is not listed here. None of the current players has an English spelling published by the JFA, the J.League or a club, so their names are not romanised on this page.
