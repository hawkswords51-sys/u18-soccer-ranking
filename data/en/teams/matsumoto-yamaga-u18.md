## intro

Matsumoto Yamaga F.C. U-18 is the under-18 team of Matsumoto Yamaga, the professional club of Matsumoto, a city in the mountains of central Nagano. The club's academy began in May 2003 with 45 members, years before the first team reached the J.League, and it now runs teams from primary-school age upwards. The under-18s are not one of Japan's famous academies. They have never won a national title and have spent most of their history in the regional tier of the youth pyramid. What makes them worth following in 2026 is the season itself: after 15 of 18 matches in Prince League Hokushinetsu Division 1 they are top of the table, with 8 wins, 4 draws and 3 defeats (28 points), two years after winning promotion back from Division 2.

Some context for readers outside Japan. Club academies such as this one do not play in the two school tournaments that dominate Japanese youth football: the Inter-High in summer and the national high school championship in winter. Those are for school teams only. Instead, academy and school teams meet in the year-round Takamado Cup league pyramid (national Premier League, nine regional Prince Leagues, then prefectural leagues). Academies also have competitions of their own: the Japan Club Youth (U-18) Championship in summer, and the J Youth Cup for J.League academies.

## style

The club does not publish a detailed playing philosophy for its under-18s, and this page does not try to invent one. What can be said is that the academy is built around the first team. The under-18 team is coached by KAKIMOTO Michiaki, who is also the club's academy director, so the head of the whole academy also takes charge of its oldest team on match days. In 2026 the results have followed: a run of four straight wins in September put the team top of the table.

The 2016 J Youth Cup run is still the high point. Matsumoto beat Yokohama F.Marinos Youth in the quarter-finals, a result the Japanese football press called a giant-killing, and went out in the semi-finals. FC Tokyo U-18 went on to win the competition. The Club Youth Championship works in two stages. Regional qualifying comes first, then the national finals in late July. Matsumoto came through the Hokushinetsu qualifying three times in four years, between 2017 and 2020.

## history

- **2003** — Matsumoto Yamaga, years before reaching the J.League, starts a youth academy in May with 45 players. The stated aim is to produce professional players and also people who will stay involved in football for life.
- **2016** — The under-18s reach the semi-finals of the J Youth Cup, beating Yokohama F.Marinos Youth on the way.
- **2017** — In April the club signs a partnership agreement with Matsusho Gakuen High School in Matsumoto, where most of the academy's under-18 players now go to school. The team also reaches the national finals of the Club Youth Championship.
- **2019–2020** — Two more Club Youth Championship national finals, the first of them as Hokushinetsu's first qualifier.
- **2020** — In November the club announces that goalkeeper KANDA Shoma and midfielder INAFUKU Taku will join the first team straight from the under-18s for 2021. The club president said at the time that it was the first time players had gone directly from the club's U-18 to the first team.
- **2023** — The team finishes 10th in Prince League Hokushinetsu Division 1 and is relegated to the new Division 2.
- **2024** — Division 2 champions at the first attempt, ahead of Matsumoto Kokusai, so two teams from the same city go up together.
- **2025** — Back in Division 1, the team finishes 8th. Albirex Niigata U-18 are the champions.
- **2026** — Top of Division 1 after 15 matches, with the run-in still to come. The division champions go into the play-offs for promotion to the national Premier League.

## model

- **Club and school together.** Under-18 players train with the club but go to an ordinary high school. Since 2017 most have gone to Matsusho Gakuen High School, under a formal partnership. In November 2020, 33 academy players were students there, spread across all three year groups. The club and school work together on schoolwork and on what players do next. Japanese school years run from April to March, so a "third-year" is a player in his final year, usually aged 17 to 18.
- **Realistic about the odds.** In a 2025 interview the academy's staff said that only around 8% of J.League academy players turn professional. They said they want players who do not make it to leave as people who contribute to their community. The academy runs extra classes in English and maths for its under-15s, and a career programme with monthly talks from people outside football. Both are open to players joining the under-15 and under-18 teams.
- **Paid for by the town.** The academy's facilities and programmes are partly funded by RAZUSO, a membership scheme started by the club in which supporters, local residents and businesses pay small amounts to support the academy. This matters for a club without a wealthy parent company: Matsumoto Yamaga is a community club, and the academy is funded the same way.
- **Two routes to the first team.** Some players go straight from the under-18s to the first team, as KANDA Shoma, INAFUKU Taku, TANAKA Sora and HAGIWARA Shotaro did. Others go to university first and come back, as KOMATSU Ren, HIGUCHI Daiki and MATSUMURA Gen did.

## alumni

- **KOMATSU Ren** (forward, Vissel Kobe) — came through the club's under-15 and under-18 teams, then played at Sanno Institute of Management before returning to Matsumoto. After spells at Zweigen Kanazawa and Renofa Yamaguchi he was the J3 League's top scorer and in its Best XI in 2023. He then moved to Blaublitz Akita and, in July 2025, to Vissel Kobe in the J1 League. He is the academy's best-known graduate.
- **TANAKA Sora** (forward, Matsumoto Yamaga) — under-15, under-18, then the first team, with a loan spell at Geylang International in Singapore along the way. He received a J3 individual award in 2025 and is one of the first team's homegrown regulars.
- **HIGUCHI Daiki** (defender, Matsumoto Yamaga) and **MATSUMURA Gen** (midfielder, Matsumoto Yamaga) — both under-18 graduates who went to Senshu University and came back to the club. Both are in the first team in 2026-27.
- **HAGIWARA Shotaro** (midfielder) — from Takamori in southern Nagano. Promoted from the under-18s for 2025, and on loan at Fukui United FC from July to December 2026.
- **KANDA Shoma** (goalkeeper) and **INAFUKU Taku** (midfielder) — the first players to go straight from the under-18s to the first team, for the 2021 season. Both have since left Matsumoto: INAFUKU joined FC Tiamo Hirakata for 2026, and KANDA moved on in January 2026.
