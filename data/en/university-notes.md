## intro
University football is the second great road to the professional game in Japan. Many players who are not promoted from a J.League academy, and many who come through high school football clubs, spend four years at university and join J.League clubs at 22. This page explains how university football is organised, follows the two national competitions, and shows the current Division 1 table in all nine regions, updated twice a week from the regional federations' official results.

## structure
There is no national university league. Each of the nine regional federations (Hokkaido, Tohoku, Kanto, Hokushinetsu, Tokai, Kansai, Chugoku, Shikoku and Kyushu) runs its own league, and the strongest of them, Kanto and Kansai, are the top level in practice. They are older than the J.League: the Kanto University League plays its **100th season** in 2026 and the Kansai Student League its 104th.
Most regions play a first half from April to July and a second half from September to November, with a break in August. Matches last 90 minutes, as in the professional game (most high school competitions use 80). Promotion, relegation and the places in the winter championship are decided when the leagues end in November.
Two national competitions sit on top of the leagues: the **Prime Minister Cup** (All Japan University Football Tournament) in late summer, a 32-team knockout, and the **All Japan University Football Championship** in winter, known in Japan as the "Incolle". The Prime Minister Cup winner goes straight into the final round of the winter championship. Universities also enter their second teams in the I-League, a separate competition for squad players.

## pathway
University football is not a fallback for those who missed out at 18. Seven of the 26 players in Japan's squad for the 2026 FIFA World Cup played university football, and all seven came through high school football clubs rather than J.League academies.
In Kanto alone, the regional federation's official list shows **85 players** from the class of 2025 joining J.League clubs (J1 to J3) or clubs abroad for the 2026 season. Players now also move abroad straight from university: SHIOGAI Kento left Keio University for NEC Nijmegen in the Netherlands in 2024.
A familiar story is the academy player who is not promoted at 18, goes to university and returns to his club four years later. SUWAMA Kosei came through the Yokohama F. Marinos academy and was registered with the first team while still at school, but was not offered a contract. He went to the University of Tsukuba and joined Yokohama F. Marinos for the 2026 season.
