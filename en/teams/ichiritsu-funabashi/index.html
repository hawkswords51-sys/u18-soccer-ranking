Ichiritsu Funabashi High School

Ichiritsu Funabashi High School (市立船橋, known in Japan as "Ichifuna") is a municipal high school run by the city of Funabashi in Chiba Prefecture, just east of Tokyo. It is one of the most successful school football teams in Japanese history. The school lists five wins in the winter All Japan High School Soccer Tournament, nine in the summer Inter-High (the most of any school) and one in the national youth championship that came before today's U-18 Premier League. It achieved all of this as a state school, without the resources of the private schools and J.League academies it usually plays against. Its alumni include SUZUKI Yuito of SC Freiburg, who was in Japan's squad at the 2026 World Cup. The team was relegated from the Premier League at the end of 2025. In 2026 it plays in Prince League Kanto Division 1 and was top of the table in late September. At the 2026 Inter-High it went out in round 2 on penalties.

Playing style

Ichifuna's reputation was built on organisation, work rate and defending. Its record in finals shows it: in all five of its winning winter finals it conceded no more than one goal, and three were won with clean sheets. The club's own description puts the weight on "human education through football": developing players who can think and solve problems for themselves, and who grow as people as well as players.

The 2026 team fits that pattern. In the Chiba qualifier for the Inter-High it drew 1-1 with its great local rival, Ryutsu Keizai University Kashiwa (RKU Kashiwa), after extra time and won the penalty shoot-out 3-0 to take the prefecture's place. At the national finals it lost 0-0 (4-5 on penalties) to Toyama Daiichi High School in round 2, the last 32, without conceding in open play. In the league it lost its opening match and then went eleven games unbeaten, leading Prince League Kanto Division 1 at the end of September.

History

Japanese school years run from April to March. The winter tournament starts in late December and finishes in January, so each edition covers two calendar years. The summer Inter-High sits within a single year.

  • 1987 — Ichifuna wins the Inter-High at its first attempt, beating Kunimi 2-1 in the final in Hokkaido.
  • 1988 — A second Inter-High title follows. That winter the team reaches the final of the 67th winter championship and loses to Shimizu Shiritsu Shogyo.
  • 1994–95 — The first winter title, won with a 5-0 win over Teikyo in the final.
  • 1996–97 — The second winter title. Ichifuna beats Toko Gakuen High School 2-1 in a final remembered for NAKAMURA Shunsuke playing on the losing side.
  • 1998 — A third Inter-High title.
  • 1999–2000 — The third winter title, won 2-0 against Kagoshima Jitsugyo.
  • 2001 — A fourth Inter-High title.
  • 2002–03 — The fourth winter title, won 1-0 against Kunimi, the strongest school side of that era.
  • 2003 — Champions of the All Japan Youth (U-18) Championship, which brought high schools and J.League academies together.
  • 2004–05 — Runners-up in the winter championship, beaten on penalties by Kagoshima Jitsugyo after a goalless final.
  • 2007–2008 — Inter-High titles in consecutive years. The 2008 title is shared with RKU Kashiwa, and the two Chiba schools are listed as joint champions.
  • 2010 — A seventh Inter-High title.
  • 2011–12 — The fifth winter title. The captain, IZUMI Ryuji, scores both goals in a 2-1 win over Yokkaichi Chuo Kogyo in the final.
  • 2013 — An eighth Inter-High title.
  • 2016 — The ninth Inter-High title, won by beating RKU Kashiwa in an all-Chiba final. HARA Teruki, now with Nagoya Grampus, is in the team.
  • 2023–24 — Semi-finals of the 102nd winter championship. Forward GUNJI Riku is reported as the tournament's top scorer and joins Shimizu S-Pulse in 2024.
  • 2025 — Ichifuna finishes 12th and last in Premier League EAST and is relegated to the Prince League.
  • 2026 — In June the team beats RKU Kashiwa on penalties in the Chiba final to reach its 32nd Inter-High. It goes out in round 2 on penalties to Toyama Daiichi High School. In the league it leads Prince League Kanto Division 1 into the autumn, with promotion straight back to the Premier League as its aim.

Development pathway

For readers new to Japanese youth football: high-school teams and J.League academies play in the same national league pyramid. At the top are the U-18 Premier League (EAST and WEST), then nine regional Prince Leagues, then the prefectural leagues. Two high-school tournaments carry even more prestige than the league: the summer Inter-High and the winter championship, which is televised nationally and ends at the National Stadium in Tokyo in January.

  • A state school among private schools and academies. Most of Japan's leading school teams are private schools. Ichifuna is run by the city of Funabashi, and it has won more national titles than almost any private rival.
  • The Chiba rivalry. Chiba is one of the most competitive football prefectures in Japan. Only one school per prefecture goes to the Inter-High and the winter championship, so Ichifuna and RKU Kashiwa often meet in the Chiba final with a national place at stake. The two schools have also met in Inter-High finals, and the 2008 title was shared between them. Chiba also has the academies of JEF United Chiba and Kashiwa Reysol.
  • A large squad. The club lists close to 90 members across three year groups, so there is strong competition for places in the first team.
  • Two routes to the professional game. Ichifuna players have gone straight from school into the J.League (SUZUKI Yuito, HARA Teruki, SHIIHASHI Keiya, GUNJI Riku) and via university (IZUMI Ryuji, through Meiji University).

Former players

  • SUZUKI Yuito (midfielder/forward, SC Freiburg) — wore the number 10 in his final year at the school and joined Shimizu S-Pulse in 2020. He played for RC Strasbourg in France and Brøndby in Denmark before moving to Freiburg in the German Bundesliga in 2025. He was named in Japan's squad for the 2026 World Cup, and he started the 2026–27 Bundesliga season in strong scoring form.
  • HARA Teruki (defender, Nagoya Grampus) — a member of the 2016 Inter-High-winning team. He joined Albirex Niigata from the school and then played for Sagan Tosu, Shimizu S-Pulse and Grasshoppers in Switzerland. He moved to Nagoya on a permanent transfer for 2025.
  • SHIIHASHI Keiya (midfielder, Avispa Fukuoka) — went from the school to Vegalta Sendai, then played for Kashiwa Reysol and Nagoya Grampus. He joined Avispa Fukuoka on loan in March 2026, and the move became permanent in June 2026.
  • IZUMI Ryuji (midfielder, Nagoya Grampus) — captain of the 2011–12 winter champions, scoring both goals in the final. He went on to Meiji University, joined Nagoya in 2016, spent 2020–2022 at Kashima Antlers and then returned to Nagoya.
  • GUNJI Riku (forward, Renofa Yamaguchi FC) — reported as top scorer at the 2023–24 winter championship, when Ichifuna reached the semi-finals. He joined Shimizu S-Pulse in 2024 and scored on his only J1 appearance in 2025. In July 2026 he moved to J3 club Renofa Yamaguchi on a development loan until June 2027.
