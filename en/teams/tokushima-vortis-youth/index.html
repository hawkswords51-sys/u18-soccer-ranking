Tokushima Vortis Youth

Tokushima Vortis Youth is the under-18 team of Tokushima Vortis, the J.League club from Tokushima on the island of Shikoku. It plays in the Prince League Shikoku, the regional tier directly below the national Premier League, and it is one of the two academies — with Ehime FC U-18 — that have dominated that league for the past decade. Since 2017 it has won the Prince League Shikoku four times and finished second twice more, and three of those titles have taken it to the national play-off for a Premier League place. It has not yet won one. In 2026, under head coach ITO Takehiro, the team is again in the race: second in the table after 13 matches, with 10 wins, 1 draw and 2 defeats (31 points).

Playing style

The club describes its academy football as "aggressive in attack and defence" — the phrase it uses in its official team introductions for national competitions — with the aim of playing brave football to the final whistle. The academy's stated pillars are player development, character building and service to the local community. In practice, the youth team is built to play the same way as the professional side, so that a player stepping up to the first team already knows the patterns he will be asked to use. Since 2022 the club has also had a youth-development partnership with the Spanish club Real Sociedad.

The Prince League Shikoku is decided over a full home-and-away season (18 matches in recent years). The champion does not go up automatically: it enters a national play-off in December against the champions of the other regional Prince Leagues, and only the winners reach the Premier League. Tokushima's record in that play-off is the story of the club's near-misses:

  • 2017 — In the club's first play-off run past the opening round, Tokushima beat Shoshi 2-0, then met RKU Kashiwa, that summer's Inter-High champions, in the deciding match on 17 December. RKU Kashiwa won 5-1 and went up.
  • 2019 — Shikoku champions again; beaten in the play-off.
  • 2020 — Champions, but there was no promotion that season because of the pandemic.
  • 2023 — Champions with 14 wins, 1 draw and 3 defeats. In the first round of the play-off, at Edion Stadium Hiroshima on 8 December, Tokushima and Urawa Red Diamonds Youth drew 5-5 after extra time, and Urawa won the penalty shoot-out 4-2.

History

  • 2007 — The first Prince League Shikoku title, finishing ahead of Kagawa Nishi High School and Ehime FC's youth team.
  • 2017 — A second title, and the club's best run in the Premier League play-off: a win over Shoshi, then the deciding match against RKU Kashiwa.
  • 2018 — Runners-up to Ehime FC U-18.
  • 2019–2020 — Back-to-back Shikoku titles.
  • 2022 — The club announces its youth-development partnership with Real Sociedad.
  • 2023 — A fifth title and a third play-off, lost only on penalties to Urawa after a 5-5 draw.
  • 2024 — Third, behind Ehime FC U-18 and Tokushima Ichiritsu.
  • 2025 — Runners-up to Ehime FC U-18, who won the league and the play-off place.
  • 2026 — Second after 13 matches (10 wins, 1 draw, 2 defeats, 31 points), again chasing the title under ITO Takehiro.

Development pathway

How a club academy fits into Japanese youth football. Japan has two parallel paths for players aged 15 to 18. Most play for their high school, and their great national events are the summer Inter-High (the All Japan High School Championship) and the winter All Japan High School Soccer Tournament. Professional clubs run their own under-18 teams instead, and these teams do not enter either school competition. Tokushima Vortis Youth therefore has no Inter-High record by design. Its own national events are the Japan Club Youth (U-18) Championship in summer, for the under-18 teams of professional and amateur clubs, and the J.League Youth Cup, for J.League academies. Both kinds of team meet in the league pyramid run by the Japan Football Association: the national Premier League (EAST and WEST) at the top, nine regional Prince Leagues below it, and prefectural leagues below those. Japanese school years run from April to March, so a "third-year" player is in his final school year, aged 17 or 18.

A pathway from age 12. Tokushima runs a junior-youth (under-15) team as well as the under-18s, and the core of the youth team comes up from it. The two most recent academy players to reach the first team — ONOE Yusei and FUKUTA Muku — both went from the junior-youth team to the youth team and then straight into the professional squad, and both are registered by the J.League as home-grown players.

The university route. Not every graduate turns professional at 18. Several of the academy's J.League players went on to university football first and joined a professional club after graduating — a common path in Japan, where university leagues are strong and closely scouted.

What promotion would mean. Tokushima Vortis Youth has never played in the Premier League, created in 2011. Reaching it would give the academy a full season against the strongest under-18 sides in western Japan every week, rather than only in the December play-off, where the gap in intensity has repeatedly shown. Winning the Prince League Shikoku is only the first step; the play-off is a short knockout competition in which one match decides a whole season's work.

A different kind of local rival. Shikoku is still strong high-school football country, and Tokushima Ichiritsu — runners-up in the Prince League Shikoku in 2019, 2022, 2023 and 2024 — is the academy's main rival within the prefecture.

Former players

All of the following came through the under-18 team itself (clubs as of late September 2026):

  • ONOE Yusei (midfielder, Tokushima Vortis) — from the junior-youth team through the youth team into the first team; a home-grown player in the professional squad.
  • FUKUTA Muku (midfielder, Tokushima Vortis) — the same route, junior-youth to youth to first team; made his first-team debut as a teenager.
  • NAKANO Kojiro (goalkeeper, AC Nagano Parceiro) — a 2-metre goalkeeper who went from the youth team to Hosei University, then played for Hokkaido Consadole Sapporo and Zweigen Kanazawa before joining Nagano.
  • MORITA Rin (midfielder, Nara Club) — promoted from the youth team to Tokushima Vortis, with spells at Nara Club and FC Ryukyu, and now at Nara Club.
  • SAWAZAKI Ryota (midfielder, Matsumoto Yamaga FC) — youth team, then Osaka Gakuin University and FC Osaka before moving to Matsumoto.
  • SHINOMIYA Yusei (midfielder, Zweigen Kanazawa) — youth team, then Osaka Gakuin University, then Kanazawa.
  • GOTO Takuma (midfielder, Nara Club) — youth team, then Kansai University of International Studies, followed by FC Tokushima, Kamatamare Sanuki and FC Tiamo Hirakata before Nara Club.

Several well-known players spent their junior-youth years at Tokushima but went to a high school, not the club's youth team, at 15 — among them SHIOTANI Tsukasa, NISHINO Taiyo, ISHIDA Yusuke and NAKATA Shunki. They are products of the club's academy, but not of its under-18 team.
