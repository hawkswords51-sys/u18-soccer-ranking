Kagoshima Josei High School

Kagoshima Josei High School (鹿児島城西, usually just "Josei") is a private school in Ijuin, Hioki City, in Kagoshima Prefecture at the southern tip of Kyushu. It belongs to the Nissho Gakuen educational foundation, and from April 2027 it will be renamed Kagoshima Nissho Gakuen High School (鹿児島日章学園高校); the 2026–27 winter championship will be the last played under the Josei name. The football club was founded in 1993, so it is young by the standards of Japanese school football, yet it reached the final of the winter All Japan High School Soccer Tournament within fifteen years. That 2008–09 run is remembered for one player: OSAKO Yuya scored in every match and finished with ten goals, a single-tournament record that still stands. Josei has won the Prince League Kyushu three times (2003, 2008 and 2023) and spent the 2024 season in the national Premier League WEST. In 2026 the team plays in Prince League Kyushu Division 1, where it is second after 13 matches with 29 points (9 wins, 2 draws, 2 defeats). It did not reach the 2026 Inter-High, losing the Kagoshima final 0-1 to Kamimura Gakuen.

Playing style

Josei have long been known as an attacking side. The 2008–09 team scored freely on the way to the National Stadium, and the club's reputation for producing strikers rests largely on that run. Recent Josei teams look more balanced: in 2026 they have conceded fewer league goals than any other team in the division. In the 2026 Kagoshima Inter-High final the team lined up in a 3-4-1-2. Late goals have been a theme of the 2026 league campaign. In round 13 Josei beat Kunimi, who had won seven league matches in a row, with a winner in stoppage time. That result kept them second, four points behind V-Varen Nagasaki U-18.

Within Kagoshima, Josei's great rival is Kamimura Gakuen. Between them the two schools have dominated the prefecture for a decade. The 2026 Inter-High qualifying final was the sixth final in a row between them, and Kamimura went on to win both national high-school titles in the 2025 school year.

History

  • 1993 — The football club is founded and a former Japan Soccer League player becomes its first head coach. He leads the club for a quarter of a century and later becomes its general manager.
  • 2003 — Josei win the first season of the Prince League Kyushu, the regional league then introduced across Japan.
  • 2008 — The club's greatest year. Josei win the Prince League Kyushu again and reach the quarter-finals of the summer Inter-High.
  • 2008–09 — In the 87th winter championship, Josei score goals in every round on the way to the final at the National Stadium in Tokyo on 12 January 2009. They lose it 2-3 to Hiroshima Minami. OSAKO Yuya scores in all six matches and finishes with ten goals, still the record for one tournament. A remark by a beaten opponent's captain, "Osako is hanpanai" (roughly, "Osako is unreal"), later became one of Japan's catchphrases of 2018, when OSAKO scored at the World Cup in Russia. He joined Kashima Antlers straight from school.
  • 2016 — Josei represent Kagoshima at both the Inter-High and the winter championship. This is the last time they play at the Inter-High.
  • 2023 — The club's third Prince League Kyushu title. In the Premier League play-off in Hiroshima in December, Josei beat Albirex Niigata U-18 2-1 and Omi 1-0 to win promotion to the national top flight for the first time.
  • 2024 — The first season in the Premier League WEST ends in twelfth place and relegation. In November, Josei beat Kamimura Gakuen 1-0 in the Kagoshima final to reach the winter championship for the first time in eight years. At the national tournament they draw 0-0 with Kanazawa Gakuin University High School in the first round and lose 3-4 on penalties.
  • 2025 — Second in the Prince League Kyushu behind Nissho Gakuen High School, which earns another play-off place. Josei lose 0-2 to Yonago Kita in the first round of the play-off. In the winter qualifiers they lose the Kagoshima final to Kamimura Gakuen.
  • 2026 — In May the school loses the Inter-High qualifying final 0-1 to Kamimura Gakuen in front of about 1,500 spectators. In the league, Josei and V-Varen Nagasaki U-18 have pulled clear of the rest of Prince League Kyushu Division 1. The school announces that it will take the name Kagoshima Nissho Gakuen High School from April 2027.

Development pathway

For readers new to Japanese youth football: high-school teams and J.League academies play in the same national league pyramid. At the top are the U-18 Premier League (EAST and WEST), then nine regional Prince Leagues, then the prefectural leagues. Each December the leading Prince League teams meet in a play-off for promotion to the Premier League. Two high-school tournaments carry even more prestige than the league. The summer Inter-High is the national high-school sports championship. The winter championship is televised nationally and ends at the National Stadium in January, which is why its seasons are written as "2008–09". In both, each prefecture usually sends a single team, so a strong school in a strong prefecture can miss out altogether.

  • A school side in a prefecture of school sides. Kagoshima has a J.League club, Kagoshima United, but its youth football has long been led by schools: Kagoshima Jitsugyo in earlier decades, and now Josei and Kamimura Gakuen. Because each prefecture sends only one team to the high-school tournaments, the Kagoshima final is often as hard as a national quarter-final.
  • A six-year pathway. The school's affiliated junior high school, Kagoshima Ikueikan (鹿児島育英館中), feeds players into the high-school squad. OSAKO Yuya, UEEBISU Katsunori, IKOMA Jin, HASEGAWA Yushi, HILL Cailen and IKOMA Kei all came through Ikueikan before Josei, according to their J.LEAGUE profiles.
  • Recruiting beyond Kagoshima. Not every player is local. Sagan Tosu goalkeeper IZUMORI Ryota, for example, grew up in Osaka and came to Josei from a junior club there.
  • Several routes to the professional game. OSAKO and IKOMA Jin joined J.League clubs straight from school. Others turned professional after university: UEEBISU at Meiji University, HILL at Waseda University, IZUMORI at Osaka University of Health and Sport Sciences, and HASEGAWA and IKOMA Kei at universities in Kyushu and Osaka.

Former players

  • OSAKO Yuya (forward, Vissel Kobe) — the star of the 2008–09 run to the winter final and the record scorer of the tournament. He joined Kashima Antlers in 2009, then played in Germany for TSV 1860 Munich, 1. FC Köln and Werder Bremen before returning to Japan with Vissel Kobe. He played for Japan at the 2014 and 2018 World Cups and has won 57 caps. In 2023 he was the J1 League's top scorer and Most Valuable Player as Kobe won the league for the first time. He still wears number 10 for Kobe in 2026.
  • UEEBISU Katsunori (defender, Júbilo Iwata) — came through Ikueikan and Josei, then went to Meiji University. He has played for Kyoto Sanga, Oita Trinita and Sagan Tosu, and is now a centre-back at Júbilo Iwata.
  • IKOMA Jin (defender, Giravanz Kitakyushu) — joined Yokohama F. Marinos from the school and spent several seasons on loan, at Kataller Toyama and in more than one spell at Giravanz Kitakyushu. He later played for Renofa Yamaguchi and Iwaki FC, and is back at Kitakyushu in 2026.
  • IZUMORI Ryota (goalkeeper, Sagan Tosu) — went to Osaka University of Health and Sport Sciences after the school and turned professional with Kagoshima United. He is now Sagan Tosu's number 1 and has been their regular starter in autumn 2026.
  • HASEGAWA Yushi (midfielder, AC Nagano Parceiro) — joined Oita Trinita from Miyazaki Sangyo-keiei University. He has also played for Tokushima Vortis, in two spells, and SC Sagamihara, and is now a midfielder at Nagano.
  • HILL Cailen (goalkeeper, Blaublitz Akita) — a 194cm goalkeeper who went from Josei to Waseda University and then to Sanfrecce Hiroshima. In June 2026 he joined Blaublitz Akita on loan from Hiroshima.
  • IKOMA Kei (defender, Nara Club) — after Osaka Gakuin University he played for FC Awajishima and FC Tiamo Hirakata, and now plays for Nara Club in the J3 League.
