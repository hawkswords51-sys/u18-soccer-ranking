AIE International High School

AIE International High School (AIE国際高校) has a football club unlike most in Japan. It is based on Awaji Island in Hyogo Prefecture, across the water from Kobe, and is run by KACHI, a football company, in partnership with AIE International High School, a correspondence (distance-learning) school that is also authorised to teach the International Baccalaureate. The club was formally founded in November 2023 and grew out of a project started on Awaji Island in 2021 under the name of another school, Aioi Gakuin. Its stated aim is to be "the closest organisation in Japan to the Bundesliga", and it puts individual development and sending players abroad or into the J.League before they finish school ahead of winning national titles. On 14 November 2026 it beat Sanda Gakuen High School 3-2 in the final of the Hyogo qualifying tournament for the winter All Japan High School Soccer Tournament, and will play at the national finals for the first time in 2026–27.

Playing style

The club's founder and general manager played professionally in Germany, and the training is built on his own coaching method. The head coach is Gerry Peyton, who kept goal for the Republic of Ireland at the 1990 World Cup and then spent fifteen years as Arsenal's goalkeeping coach under Arsène Wenger, before coaching in Japan at Vissel Kobe and Shimizu S-Pulse. He is responsible for the defensive organisation. A Brazilian coach who spent twelve years on Kawasaki Frontale's first-team staff works on individual technique.

The team plays with a high line and a high press, and asks for strength in one-against-one duels and a quick switch between defence and attack. Within that structure, players are encouraged to beat opponents with the dribble and to make their own decisions on the pitch.

History

  • 2021 — The club's founder starts a football project on Awaji Island under the name of Aioi Gakuin High School, recruiting players from across Japan and appointing former professionals as coaches.
  • 2023 — In October the school and the founder fall out over how the team should be run, and he is dismissed. Most of the 66 players choose to stay with the coaches rather than the school. On 9 November 2023 his company, KACHI, forms a partnership with AIE International High School, and the AIE International football club is formally founded.
  • 2024 — The team wins Division 3 of the East Harima and Awaji regional league without losing a match. In the Hyogo winter qualifiers it beats Sanda Gakuen 1-0 in the semi-final, then draws 0-0 with Takigawa Daini in the final and loses the shoot-out.
  • 2025 — Promoted again as Division 2 champions. In the Hyogo Inter-High qualifying final, AIE International draws 3-3 with Takigawa Daini and loses the shoot-out 4-5.
  • 2026 — In June the team beats Kobe Koryo 3-1 in the Inter-High qualifying semi-final but loses the final 0-1 to Takigawa Daini, its third defeat by the same opponent in a Hyogo final. Takigawa Daini go on to reach the semi-finals of the national Inter-High. In the winter qualifiers AIE International enters as a seed in the second round and beats Nishiwaki Kogyo 18-0, Sokai 1-0 and Kwansei Gakuin High School 4-0. The semi-final against Kobe Koryo, the holders, ends 0-0 after extra time, and AIE International wins the shoot-out 3-2. On 14 November it beats Sanda Gakuen 3-2 in the final, at its fourth attempt at a Hyogo final, to qualify for the national finals (from 28 December 2026) for the first time. Across the five matches it scores 26 goals and concedes 2.

Development pathway

For readers new to Japanese youth football: most strong teams at under-18 level are either school clubs, run by teachers as part of school life, or the academies of J.League clubs. Both play in the same national league pyramid, from the U-18 Premier League down through nine regional Prince Leagues to prefectural and local leagues. Two high-school tournaments carry even more prestige than the league: the summer Inter-High and the winter championship, which is televised nationally and ends at the National Stadium in Tokyo in January. Each prefecture sends one or two teams to each tournament through its own qualifying competition. AIE International is neither a traditional school club nor a J.League academy, which makes it unusual. In 2026 it plays in a local league several levels below the Prince League, yet it has beaten the prefecture's strongest school teams in knockout football.

  • A correspondence school and a football company. Because the school teaches by distance learning, its timetable is flexible. Players can keep up their studies while spending long periods training with clubs in Europe. KACHI has links with clubs in Germany and elsewhere in Europe, and encourages promising players to move abroad as soon as an opportunity comes, without waiting to finish school.
  • Professional coaches. The coaching staff includes Gerry Peyton, a former Arsenal goalkeeping coach and Republic of Ireland international, and a Brazilian coach with twelve years of J.League first-team experience at Kawasaki Frontale.
  • Facilities on Awaji Island. The club uses five grass pitches, one artificial pitch and an indoor artificial pitch. The players live in a dormitory thirty seconds' walk from the school, deliberately in shared rooms of two to four, and eat in a canteen that uses local Awaji produce.
  • Players from all over Japan. The squad includes players from J.League academies and junior clubs from Hokkaido to Okinawa. Some have family roots overseas.

Former players

The club is young, so its first graduates have been professionals for only a few years. No former player was in Japan's squad for the 2026 World Cup.

  • HIDAKA Mitsuki (midfielder, Vissel Kobe) — joined Vissel Kobe of the J1 League in 2022 from the club in its Aioi Gakuin days. Shortly after joining, he was loaned to CD Atlético Paso in Spain's fourth tier (Segunda Federación), and returned to Kobe in 2023. He has since moved from defensive midfield to full-back.
  • FUKUI Eugene (forward) — another player from the Aioi Gakuin days. In June 2021, while still at school, he signed with Kamatamare Sanuki and was registered as a special designated player, which allowed him to play J.League matches as a student. He played in J3 for Sanuki from 2021 to 2024 and has played for Toho Titanium in the Kanto regional league since 2025.

Players to watch in 2026

NISHIMURA Towa (forward) will join Tochigi City in February 2027. The move was announced in June 2026, while he was still a student.
