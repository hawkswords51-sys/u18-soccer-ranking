Yamanashi Gakuin High School

Yamanashi Gakuin High School (山梨学院高校, often shortened to "Yamagaku") is a private school in Kofu, the capital of Yamanashi Prefecture, west of Tokyo. It belongs to the same education group as Yamanashi Gakuin University, and until it was renamed it was called Yamanashi Gakuin University High School. That older name is still seen in the records of many former players. The football club is the most successful in the prefecture's history. It has won the winter All Japan High School Soccer Tournament twice, in 2009–10 (at its first attempt) and in 2020–21, and the summer Inter-High once, in 2018. Two former players were in Japan's squad at the 2026 World Cup: forward MAEDA Daizen and defender WATANABE Tsuyoshi. In 2026 the team plays in Prince League Kanto Division 1 and reached the round of 16 at the Inter-High.

2026 season

Currently 6th in the Prince League Kanto Division 1 with 18 points from 13 matches (5W 3D 5L, 18-18). Updated daily.

Playing style

Under the current head coach, the team calls its approach "vertical football": quick, direct attacks that try to reach the opponent's goal in as few passes as possible, played with an aggressive attitude. The captain at the 2025–26 winter championship described the aim as "attacking quickly towards goal and using each player's strengths". The 2026 squad had no single star forward, so it relied on organisation and preparation instead. At the 2026 Inter-High, held at J-Village in Fukushima, Yamanashi Gakuin was seeded into the second round and beat Aomori Yamada, a Premier League side, 2-1. The coaches had studied Aomori Yamada's first-round match, and the opening goal after two minutes came from a set play rehearsed the day before. The run ended in the round of 16 with a 0-1 defeat by Takigawa Daini High School, who scored in the 64th minute. In 2025 the team had reached the Inter-High quarter-finals, where Kamimura Gakuen beat it on penalties after a 1-1 draw.

History

  • 2009–10 — In its first appearance at the winter championship, the school goes all the way. In the final at the old National Stadium it beats Aomori Yamada 1-0 with a goal in the 11th minute. No school had won the tournament on its debut since Tokai Dai-ichi in 1986.
  • 2010–11 — Back at the winter championship, the team reaches the quarter-finals.
  • 2011 — Champions of Prince League Kanto Division 2, which earns promotion to Division 1, the regional league just below the national Premier League.
  • 2016 — MAEDA Daizen leaves the school to join Matsumoto Yamaga.
  • 2018 — The first Inter-High title, at the school's fifth attempt. In the final against Toko Gakuen, Yamanashi Gakuin falls behind in the 21st minute. Forward MIYAZAKI Jumma equalises at the end of normal time, and an own goal in the fifth minute of extra time wins the match 2-1. The players later said that a 100-kilometre run over four days, two weeks before the tournament, gave them the stamina for a long final.
  • 2020–21 — The second winter title, under a head coach who had arrived from Yamanashi Gakuin University during the 2019 season. In the final, played at Saitama Stadium 2002 in January 2021, Yamanashi Gakuin leads Aomori Yamada twice. After a 2-2 draw it wins the shoot-out 4-2. The goalkeeper and captain, KUMAKURA Takumi, later turns professional.
  • 2021 — The team finishes bottom of Prince League Kanto Division 1 and drops to Division 2. The championship-winning head coach leaves at the end of the school year.
  • 2024 — Second place in Division 2 brings promotion back to Division 1. In the winter the school returns to the national championship after missing two editions.
  • 2025 — Sixth in Prince League Kanto Division 1. At the Inter-High, Yamanashi Gakuin beats Morioka Shogyo 4-1, Kindai Wakayama 4-1 and Hamamatsu Kaiseikan 2-0 to reach the quarter-finals for the first time since its 2018 title. There it loses 2-4 on penalties to Kamimura Gakuen after a 1-1 draw. In the winter, after winning the Yamanashi final 3-0 against Nirasaki, it beats Kyoto Tachibana on penalties in the first round and then loses 1-2 to Shoshi.
  • 2026 — The school wins the Yamanashi Inter-High qualifier for the fifth year in a row, beating Nihon University Meisei 4-0 in the final, and plays at its 10th Inter-High. It beats Aomori Yamada 2-1 and loses 0-1 to Takigawa Daini in the round of 16. In June, MAEDA Daizen and WATANABE Tsuyoshi both play at the World Cup.

Development pathway

For readers new to Japanese youth football: high-school teams and J.League academies play in the same national league pyramid. At the top are the U-18 Premier League (EAST and WEST), then nine regional Prince Leagues, then the prefectural leagues. Two high-school tournaments carry even more prestige than the league: the summer Inter-High and the winter championship, which is televised nationally and ends in January.

  • A small prefecture's flagship. Yamanashi has a population of under one million and one J.League club, Ventforet Kofu. Its traditional football power was the public Nirasaki High School. Yamanashi Gakuin has become the prefecture's leading school side, and in 2026 it plays in Prince League Kanto Division 1 alongside the academies of large Tokyo-area clubs.
  • Recruitment from across Japan. The 28-player squad registered for the 2026 Inter-High included players from junior clubs in Yamanashi, but also from Tokyo, Saitama, Chiba and Kanagawa, and from as far away as Osaka, Shizuoka, Fukushima and Hokkaido. Several came from J.League junior-youth teams, including those of JEF United Chiba, Shonan Bellmare and Ventforet Kofu.
  • A second chance for academy players. WATANABE Tsuyoshi was in FC Tokyo's junior-youth (U-15) team but was not taken into its U-18 team. He moved to Yamanashi Gakuin, went on to Chuo University, and later signed for FC Tokyo as a professional. KUMAKURA Takumi, the 2020–21 captain, also came from FC Tokyo's U-15 team.
  • First-year development. The school enters a team of first-year players in the Kanto Rookie League, a U-16 league for the region's leading high schools, so that new players get competitive matches before they reach the senior squad.
  • A school-to-university pathway. The group's university has its own football club. ICHINOSE Taiju, a local player from Ventforet Kofu's junior-youth team, went from the high school to Yamanashi Gakuin University and then signed for Ventforet Kofu.
  • Producing players at every level. Former players have gone straight to J.League clubs (MAEDA Daizen, SHIRASAKI Ryohei, MIYAZAKI Jumma) and via university (WATANABE Tsuyoshi, KATO Takumi, KUMAKURA Takumi, ICHINOSE Taiju).

Former players

  • MAEDA Daizen (forward, Ipswich Town) — joined Matsumoto Yamaga from the school in 2016. He played on loan for Mito HollyHock, Marítimo in Portugal and Yokohama F. Marinos, and in 2021 he was joint top scorer in J1 with 23 goals. He moved to Celtic at the start of 2022 and won five Scottish Premiership titles there. He scored for Japan against Croatia at the 2022 World Cup and scored again at the 2026 World Cup. In July 2026 he joined Ipswich Town in the English Premier League on a three-year contract.
  • WATANABE Tsuyoshi (defender, Feyenoord) — after the school he went to Chuo University and then joined FC Tokyo. He moved to Belgium with KV Kortrijk and then KAA Gent, and joined Feyenoord in the Netherlands in July 2025. He was named in Japan's squad for the 2026 World Cup.
  • MIYAZAKI Jumma (forward, Tokushima Vortis) — scored the late equaliser in the 2018 Inter-High final. He joined Ventforet Kofu from the school in 2019 and stayed there for more than six seasons, including the club's 2022 Emperor's Cup win and its AFC Champions League campaign. He moved to Tokushima Vortis on a permanent transfer in August 2025.
  • KATO Takumi (forward, Kyoto Sanga F.C.) — went from the school to Waseda University, then joined Shimizu S-Pulse, with two loan spells at SC Sagamihara. He moved to Matsumoto Yamaga and, in June 2026, to Kyoto Sanga on a permanent transfer.
  • SHIRASAKI Ryohei (midfielder, FC Machida Zelvia) — joined Shimizu S-Pulse straight from the school. He has also played for Kataller Toyama, Kashima Antlers and Sagan Tosu, and is now at Machida.
  • KUMAKURA Takumi (goalkeeper, Kagoshima United FC) — the captain of the 2020–21 championship team. He went on to Rissho University before turning professional with Kagoshima.
  • ICHINOSE Taiju (defender, Ventforet Kofu) — a Yamanashi player who came through Ventforet Kofu's junior-youth team, Yamanashi Gakuin High School and Yamanashi Gakuin University before signing for his home-town club.
  • Other current J.League players from the school include AIDA Yuki (Júbilo Iwata), YAMAGUCHI Kazuma (Thespa Gunma), ONO Yuya (AC Nagano Parceiro), ITAKURA Kenta (Mito HollyHock) and MOURI Shunya (Zweigen Kanazawa).
