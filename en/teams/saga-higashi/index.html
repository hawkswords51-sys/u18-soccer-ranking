Saga Higashi High School

Saga Higashi High School (佐賀東高校, usually just "Saga Higashi" or, locally, "Higashi-ko") is a prefectural public school in the south of Saga city, the capital of Saga Prefecture in northern Kyushu. Its football club is the dominant school team in the prefecture today. It has qualified for the winter All Japan High School Soccer Tournament 15 times, including three years in a row from 2023–24 to 2025–26, and reached the quarter-finals in 2023–24. It has also played at the summer Inter-High 19 times, finishing third in both 2008 and 2009. Its best-known former player is HIRAKAWA Yu, the Bristol City winger who played for Japan at the Paris 2024 Olympics and made his senior debut in 2025. In 2026 the team plays in Prince League Kyushu Division 2, and it reached the second round (last 32) of the Inter-High.

Playing style

Saga Higashi is known for keeping the ball. Where many school teams in Kyushu rely on physical power and direct play, especially on the heavy pitches of the winter tournament, Saga Higashi has built its reputation on short passing, patient possession and players who are expected to read the game and make their own decisions on the pitch. The head coach has been in charge for many years, so the style has had time to settle, and players from the region who want to play this way often choose the school.

The 2026 season has so far been a strong one. Saga Higashi won the Saga qualifying tournament for the Inter-High without conceding a goal: 10-0, 1-0, 3-0, 4-0 and, in the final on 4 June, 3-0 against Saga Shogyo, for its second Saga title in a row. At the Kyushu regional high-school tournament in June it beat Nagasaki Sogo Kagaku Daigaku Fuzoku 5-1 before losing 0-2 to Nissho Gakuen High School in the quarter-final. At the national Inter-High in late July it beat Saikyo of Yamaguchi 4-2 in the first round, then lost 1-5 to Kamimura Gakuen, the reigning national champions, in the second.

History

  • 1963 — The school opens in Saga city in April. For many years the leading football school in the prefecture is Saga Shogyo (Saga Commercial High School), and Saga Higashi is one of the challengers.
  • 2008 — Saga Higashi reaches the semi-finals of the Inter-High and finishes third.
  • 2009 — A second Inter-High semi-final in a row. The same year the school wins the Saga qualifying competition for the Emperor's Cup, Japan's national open cup, and plays in it for the first time.
  • 2022 — Midfielder YOSHIDA Jimpei joins Albirex Niigata straight from the school.
  • 2023–24 — The club's best winter championship. Saga Higashi wins three matches, scoring nine goals, including a 5-1 win over Toyama Daiichi High School in the third round, before losing 1-2 to Horikoshi of Tokyo in the quarter-final.
  • 2024 — Third place in Prince League Kyushu Division 2. Saga Higashi wins the Saga winter qualifier again, for its 14th winter championship.
  • 2025 — Fifth in Prince League Kyushu Division 2, a 15th winter championship and a return to the Inter-High.
  • 2026 — The team wins the Saga Inter-High qualifier without conceding and plays in its 19th Inter-High, going out in the second round to Kamimura Gakuen. After 12 of its 18 league matches it leads Prince League Kyushu Division 2 with 23 points (seven wins, two draws, three defeats). The Saga qualifier for the winter championship begins on 4 October, and Saga Higashi, seeded into the third round, is aiming for a fourth winter championship in a row.

Development pathway

For readers new to Japanese youth football: high-school teams and J.League academies play in the same national league pyramid. At the top are the U-18 Premier League (EAST and WEST), then nine regional Prince Leagues (Kyushu has two divisions), then the prefectural leagues. Promotion and relegation link every level. Two high-school tournaments carry even more prestige than the league: the summer Inter-High and the winter championship, which is televised nationally and ends at the National Stadium in Tokyo in January. Because the Japanese school year runs from April to March, a winter championship is usually written as two years (2023–24).

  • A public school at the top of a small prefecture. Saga is one of Japan's smallest prefectures by population, and its only J.League academy belongs to Sagan Tosu. Saga Higashi is a prefectural state school, yet it has become the prefecture's regular representative at both national tournaments.
  • A fixed footballing identity. Long continuity on the coaching staff has given the club a clear style: possession football and players who think for themselves. That identity attracts players who want to play that way, and it carries over from year to year even though the whole squad changes every three years.
  • Local club links. Many players come from junior clubs in Saga and neighbouring prefectures. HIRAKAWA Yu, for example, played for FC Revona in Saga before joining the school.
  • Late developers. Saga Higashi's best-known alumni were not finished products at 18. Several went to university first (HIRAKAWA Yu to Yamanashi Gakuin University, NAKANO Yoshihiro to the University of Tsukuba) and became professionals afterwards. Others, such as YOSHIDA Jimpei, have gone directly to J.League clubs.
  • A senior league side and a second team. The first team plays in Prince League Kyushu Division 2, where its opponents include the second and third teams of strong schools and academies such as Nissho Gakuen, Ohzu and Sagan Tosu.

Former players

  • HIRAKAWA Yu (winger, Bristol City) — from Kashima in Saga Prefecture, he played for FC Revona and Saga Higashi, then for Yamanashi Gakuin University. He joined FC Machida Zelvia from university and was part of the team that won the J2 League in 2023. He was in Japan's squad at the Paris 2024 Olympics. He moved to Bristol City in the English Championship on loan in 2024 and permanently in 2025. On 5 June 2025 he made his senior Japan debut, against Australia in a World Cup qualifier in Perth. In January 2026 he joined Hull City on loan and helped them win promotion to the Premier League, setting up the winning goal in the play-off final against Middlesbrough. He returned to Bristol City for the 2026–27 season.
  • NAKANO Yoshihiro (midfielder, Blaublitz Akita) — came to the school from Partida Kagoshima, then studied at the University of Tsukuba before joining Kawasaki Frontale. He has since played for Vegalta Sendai, Hokkaido Consadole Sapporo, Sagan Tosu, Shonan Bellmare and Yokohama FC, and moved to Akita in J2 on a permanent transfer for 2026.
  • YOSHIDA Jimpei (midfielder, Fukushima United FC) — joined Albirex Niigata straight from the school in 2022 and had two loan spells at Kamatamare Sanuki. In 2026 he is playing for Fukushima United in J3, on loan from Niigata.
