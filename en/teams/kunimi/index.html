Kunimi High School

Kunimi High School (国見高校, run by Nagasaki Prefecture and almost always called simply "Kunimi") is a public school in the small town of Kunimi, now part of Unzen City, on the Shimabara Peninsula in Nagasaki Prefecture. In football it is one of the most famous names in Japan. Under the coach KOMINE Tadatoshi, who arrived in 1984, this rural state school won the winter All Japan High School Soccer Tournament six times between 1987–88 and 2003–04 — the joint post-war record, shared with Teikyo of Tokyo — as well as five summer Inter-High titles and two national youth championships. Its alumni include the J1 League's all-time top scorer OKUBO Yoshito, the "Cannon of Asia" TAKAGI Takuya and HIRAYAMA Sota. After a long quiet spell the school has rebuilt itself: it returned to the winter tournament in 2022, reached the Inter-High semi-finals in 2023, and in 2026 plays in Prince League Kyushu Division 1, where it stood third after 13 matches.

Playing style

The KOMINE-era teams were known for running harder than their opponents, physical power and a refusal to give up; for years they also had a strict regime that included shaved heads for every player. The modern Kunimi keeps the work-rate but plays differently. The current head coach, a former Kunimi player who played professionally in the J.League, took charge in 2018 and moved the team away from defend-and-counter football built on long balls towards a passing game that keeps the ball and breaks opponents down. The club's own motto sums it up: "fight aggressively in attack and defence — run more than the opponent, move the ball, move the opponent, and break them down." In 2021 the coach also ended the shaved-head rule and allowed players to use mobile phones, arguing that Kunimi was never strong simply because of its haircuts. The squad trains on the school's floodlit clay pitch and on an artificial-turf field at Hyakkadai Park nearby, which has made the possession style much easier to coach.

History

  • 1984 — KOMINE Tadatoshi, who had already won the Inter-High with Shimabara Shogyo in 1977, moves to Kunimi as a social-studies teacher and football coach.
  • 1986 — Kunimi wins its first national title at the summer Inter-High, and that winter reaches the final of the winter championship, losing to Tokai Dai-ichi.
  • 1987–88 — First winter title, beating Tokai Dai-ichi in a rematch of the previous year's final.
  • 1990–93 — Two more winter titles (1990–91 and 1992–93) and a second Inter-High in 1993. Kunimi becomes a fixture at the winter tournament: it qualifies 21 years in a row, a run that lasts into the 2000s.
  • 2000 — The summer–winter double: champions of the Inter-High and, with OKUBO Yoshito in attack, of the winter championship, beating Kusatsu Higashi 3-0 in the final.
  • 2001–02 — A second consecutive winter title, plus the Prince Takamado Cup, where a young HIRAYAMA Sota scored the only goal of the final against FC Tokyo U-18. Kunimi retains the Takamado Cup in 2002, but loses the 2002–03 winter final to Ichiritsu Funabashi.
  • 2003–04 — The last great season: Inter-High champions in the summer and winter champions with a 6-0 final win over Chikuyo Gakuen in HIRAYAMA's final year. A fifth Inter-High follows in 2004.
  • 2006–07 — KOMINE retires from the school's teaching staff in March 2006 and steps down from the football club in January 2007. He later coached at another Nagasaki school. He died in January 2022 and was inducted into the Japan Football Hall of Fame later that year.
  • 2010–11 — Kunimi's last winter appearance for 12 years. As J.League academies take more of the best young players, the school drifts out of the national picture.
  • 2018 — A former Kunimi player, who had played for Avispa Fukuoka and Montedio Yamagata, becomes head coach after a year on the coaching staff.
  • 2021 — Kunimi wins the Kyushu tournament for first- and second-year players for the first time in 14 years, and abolishes the shaved-head rule.
  • 2022–23 — Back at the winter championship for the first time in 12 years (its 23rd appearance), Kunimi reaches the last 16, its best winter run for 17 years.
  • 2023 — Semi-finals of the Inter-High, held in Hokkaido, lost on penalties to Toko Gakuen High School.
  • 2024 — Champions of Prince League Kyushu Division 2, earning promotion to the top regional division.
  • 2026 — The club reaches its 23rd Inter-High, its first since 2024, but loses 1-2 to Kindai University High School in the first round at J-Village on 25 July. In the league it wins seven matches in a row, including a 3-0 win over leaders V-Varen Nagasaki U-18 in September, before Kagoshima Josei ends the run with a stoppage-time winner. After 13 matches Kunimi is third (7 wins, 6 defeats, 21 points).

Development pathway

For readers new to Japanese youth football: high-school teams and J.League academies play in the same national league pyramid. At the top are the U-18 Premier League (EAST and WEST), then nine regional Prince Leagues (Kyushu has two divisions), then the prefectural leagues. Two high-school tournaments carry even more prestige than the league: the summer Inter-High and the winter championship, which is televised nationally and ends in January. Japanese school years run from April to March, so a winter title is written here as "2003–04".

  • A state school that beat everyone. Kunimi is not a private sports academy but an ordinary prefectural school in a small rural town. Its golden age showed that a school team, with a coach who stayed for more than two decades, could dominate national football — and in the Takamado Cup, beat the J.League academies too.
  • A local club with a national reach. Students have always come from across Kyushu and beyond. NAKASHIMA Taika, for example, grew up in Osaka and chose Kunimi because his father was a former student; HARADA Takatora came from Oita. Today the staff includes a coach who also works as a scout, with links to junior clubs across Kyushu.
  • A modern staff. Instead of one all-powerful coach, the club now has a head coach supported by a first-team coach, an adviser, a goalkeeper coach, assistant coaches and an athletic trainer, looking after a squad of around 100 players across three year groups.
  • A pathway to the professional game. Kunimi players have gone straight to the J.League (OKUBO Yoshito to Cerezo Osaka, NAKASHIMA Taika to Hokkaido Consadole Sapporo, HARADA Takatora to Thespa Gunma) and via university (TAKAGI Takuya, YAMAMURA Kazuya).

Former players

  • OKUBO Yoshito (forward, retired) — the attacking star of the 2000–01 winter champions. He joined Cerezo Osaka, played in Spain for Mallorca and in Germany for Wolfsburg, and became the J1 League's all-time leading scorer with 191 goals, winning the J1 Golden Boot three seasons running with Kawasaki Frontale. He won 60 caps for Japan, scoring six goals, and played at the 2010 and 2014 World Cups.
  • TAKAGI Takuya (forward, retired; head coach of V-Varen Nagasaki) — one of KOMINE's first Kunimi players. After university he played for Sanfrecce Hiroshima, Verdy Kawasaki and Consadole Sapporo, won 44 caps with 27 goals for Japan, and scored the winning goal in the final of the 1992 Asian Cup, earning the nickname "Cannon of Asia". As a coach he has had two spells in charge of V-Varen Nagasaki, and in 2026 he leads the club in the J1 League.
  • MIURA Atsuhiro (midfielder, retired) — a Kunimi student in the early 1990s. He played for Yokohama Flügels, Yokohama F. Marinos, Tokyo Verdy, Vissel Kobe and Yokohama FC, won 25 caps for Japan and was part of the Asian Cup-winning squads of 2000 and 2004. He later managed Vissel Kobe.
  • HIRAYAMA Sota (forward, retired) — the towering centre-forward of the 2001–02 and 2003–04 winter champions, and the first player to finish top scorer at the winter tournament two years in a row. After a spell at Heracles Almelo in the Netherlands he played for FC Tokyo and Vegalta Sendai, scored a hat-trick on his senior Japan debut in 2010, and retired in 2018. He has since moved into coaching, taking charge of Sendai University's team in 2024.
  • YAMAMURA Kazuya (defender/midfielder, Wollongong Wolves) — went from Kunimi to Ryutsu Keizai University, then won trophies with Kashima Antlers, Cerezo Osaka and Kawasaki Frontale before a spell at Yokohama F. Marinos. He won one senior cap for Japan in 2010 and Asian Games gold that year. In September 2025 he moved to Australia, joining Wollongong Wolves.
  • NAKASHIMA Taika (forward, Thespa Gunma) — a tall, powerful striker who joined Hokkaido Consadole Sapporo straight from the school in 2021, after being registered as a special designated player while still a student. He played for Japan at U-21 level in 2022, had loan spells at Fujieda MYFC and Mito HollyHock among others, and moved to Thespa Gunma, first on loan in 2025 and permanently in June 2026.
  • HARADA Takatora (midfielder, Thespa Gunma) — the captain of the 2025 team, who joined Thespa Gunma straight from school for the 2026 season.

Many other internationals and J.League players came through Kunimi; only those whose names have an official English spelling are listed here. Current Kunimi players are not named on this page, because none of the 2026 squad has an English spelling published by the JFA, the J.League or a club.
