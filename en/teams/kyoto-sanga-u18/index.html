Kyoto Sanga F.C. U-18

Kyoto Sanga F.C. U-18 is the under-18 team of Kyoto Sanga F.C., the J1 club backed by Kyocera, and one of the best-known academies in western Japan. It won the J Youth Cup in 2001, was a founder member of the national Premier League in 2011 and stayed there for eleven seasons, and has twice finished second in the Premier League WEST. What makes it unusual is the arrangement behind it: since 2006 the club, Kyocera and the Ritsumeikan educational group have run the Scholar Athlete Project, under which players live in a club dormitory, study at Ritsumeikan Uji High School on a scholarship, and can continue to Ritsumeikan University. Graduates include the former Japan forward KUBO Yuya, the Mainz midfielder KAWASAKI Sota and the Real Sociedad centre-back KITA Kazunari. In 2026 the team plays in the Prince League Kansai Division 1, the regional tier directly below the Premier League. After 15 matches it was second, with 9 wins, 3 draws and 3 defeats (30 points), in contention for one of the two places in December's Premier League play-off.

Playing style

In its official team introduction for the Premier League play-off, the club describes its aim as developing "world-class top athletes who excel in mind, technique and body, with a true balance of study and sport", under the motto "From Kyoto to Japan, and to the world". On the pitch it promises "aggressive and collective" football: a team that keeps the ball, presses together and tries to control games rather than react to them. As at most J.League academies, the under-18s are expected to play in a way that lets a promoted player step into first-team training without learning a new game.

Japanese youth football runs on two tracks. High-school teams play the Inter-High (summer) and the All Japan High School Championship (winter); J.League academies such as this one play in neither, so Kyoto Sanga has no Inter-High record. Their national cups are the Japan Club Youth Championship, traditionally held in summer (the 2026 edition is scheduled for 23–29 December in Hiroshima and Yamaguchi), and the J Youth Cup for J.League academies. Both tracks meet in the Takamado Prize league pyramid: the national Premier League (EAST and WEST, twelve teams each), nine regional Prince Leagues below it, and prefectural leagues below those.

History

  • 2000 — As Kyoto Purple Sanga Youth (the club's name at the time), the team reaches the final of the Japan Club Youth Championship, losing to Yokohama F. Marinos Youth.
  • 2001 — The club's first national title: the J Youth Cup, with FC Tokyo U-18 as runners-up. The same year the team reaches the semi-finals of the Club Youth Championship again.
  • 2006 — The Scholar Athlete Project begins; the first group of players enters Ritsumeikan Uji High School in April.
  • 2009–2012 — Semi-finals of the Club Youth Championship in 2009 and 2012, and of the J Youth Cup in 2010. KUBO Yuya is promoted to the first team in 2011.
  • 2011 — The Premier League is created, and Kyoto finish second in the first WEST season behind Sanfrecce Hiroshima F.C Youth.
  • 2017 — The team plays one season in the EAST division instead of the WEST, with the Japan youth internationals WAKAHARA Tomoya (goalkeeper) and FUKUOKA Shimpei (captain) in the side.
  • 2019 — Runners-up in the WEST again, behind Nagoya Grampus U-18, and semi-finalists in the Club Youth Championship.
  • 2021 — Relegation after eleven Premier League seasons in a row (there was no league in 2020 because of the pandemic).
  • 2023 — Champions of the Prince League Kansai Division 1. In the play-off the team draws 1-1 with Fagiano Okayama U-18 in the deciding round, and it is Okayama who go up.
  • 2024 — Second in the Prince League Kansai. In the play-off they beat Vegalta Sendai Youth 5-1, then draw 1-1 with Urawa Reds Youth and lose 4-3 on penalties, one kick away from promotion.
  • 2025 — Fifth in the Prince League Kansai.
  • 2026 — Second after 15 matches and back in the race for a play-off place.

Development pathway

  • The Scholar Athlete Project. Kyocera, Kyoto Sanga and the Ritsumeikan group are the three partners. Under-18 players who meet the academic standard attend Ritsumeikan Uji High School, with Ritsumeikan paying their tuition from a scholarship fund, and those who keep their grades up can go on to Ritsumeikan University. The idea is to remove the biggest worry for families: what happens to a boy who gives up an ordinary school career for football and is not offered a professional contract at eighteen.
  • Everyone lives in. The players live together in the club dormitory, named RYOUMA, and school, dormitory and training ground are all within cycling distance. Most of the 2026 squad attends Ritsumeikan Uji.
  • Recruited nationally. More than half the squad comes up from the club's own U-15 team, but the rest arrive from junior-youth clubs across Japan, including teams in Saitama, Aichi, Yamanashi, Osaka and Kumamoto.
  • Two ways to the first team. Some players are promoted straight from the under-18s; others go to university first. NAKANO Ryuma was the first project player to turn professional with Kyoto by way of Ritsumeikan University, and TATSUKAWA Haruto joined the first-team squad while still at Niigata University of Health and Welfare. In September 2026 seven graduates of the under-18s were in the J1 squad: OKUGAWA Masaya, FUKUOKA Shimpei, NAKANO Ryuma, HIRAGA Sora, YOON Sungjun, SAKAI Kou and TATSUKAWA Haruto.

Japanese school years run from April to March, so a "third-year" in an under-18 team is in his final year of high school, and a player "promoted for 2027" signs while still a student.

Former players

  • KUBO Yuya (forward) — promoted to the first team in 2011, moved to Young Boys in Switzerland in 2013, then played for Gent, Nuremberg and FC Cincinnati, and for Japan. He announced his retirement in March 2026.
  • KAWASAKI Sota (midfielder, 1. FSV Mainz 05) — came through the under-18s to captain the first team, joined Mainz on loan, and made the move permanent in March 2026. A Japan international.
  • KITA Kazunari (defender, Real Sociedad) — a left-footed centre-back from the club's U-15 and U-18 teams and a Japan under-20 international. His loan to Real Sociedad became a permanent transfer in June 2026, on a contract to 2030.
  • OKUGAWA Masaya (midfielder, Kyoto Sanga F.C.) — U-15 and U-18 graduate who spent years in Austria and Germany with Red Bull Salzburg, Arminia Bielefeld, Hamburg and Augsburg before coming home.
  • HARAKAWA Riki (midfielder, Kashiwa Reysol) and TAKAHASHI Yuji (defender, Shimizu S-Pulse) — the generation promoted with KUBO, both still in J1.
  • KOMAI Yoshiaki (midfielder, FC Imabari) — U-15 and U-18 graduate who went on to play for Urawa Reds and Hokkaido Consadole Sapporo.
  • YAMADA Fuki (midfielder, FC Tokyo), KOZUKI Soichiro (midfielder, Shonan Bellmare), ASADA Shogo (defender, Kashiwa Reysol), WAKAHARA Tomoya (goalkeeper, Kashiwa Reysol), SHIMAMURA Takuya (midfielder, Yokohama FC) and UEDA Yuta (defender, JEF United Chiba) — more recent graduates now at other J.League clubs.
  • FUKUOKA Shimpei (midfielder, Kyoto Sanga F.C.) — a Japan youth international and 2017 youth captain, now a first-team regular at his own club.
