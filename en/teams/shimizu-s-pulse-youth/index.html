Shimizu S-Pulse Youth

Shimizu S-Pulse Youth is the under-18 team of Shimizu S-Pulse, the J.League club from the port district of Shimizu in Shizuoka City. Shizuoka calls itself Japan's "football kingdom": the prefecture's high schools have won the national winter championship many times, and its towns have produced more professionals per head than almost anywhere else in the country. S-Pulse's academy has had to compete for local talent with famous schools such as Shizuoka Gakuen High School and with Jubilo Iwata's academy down the road, and it has done so successfully. The under-18s are national Club Youth champions twice (2002 and 2018), J Youth Cup winners twice (1997 and 2005), and spent eleven seasons in the national Premier League, finishing runners-up in the EAST division four times. Since 2023 the team has played one level lower, in the Prince League Tokai, and winning its way back to the Premier League has become the central aim. It won the Tokai league in 2024 and again in 2025, but lost in the promotion play-offs both times. In 2026 the team is second in the Prince League Tokai after 13 matches (9 wins, 2 draws, 2 losses, 29 points).

Playing style

The academy describes its approach in a written "Academy Playing Philosophy" shared by every age group, from the junior teams up to the under-18s. Since January 2026 the head coach has been ICHIKAWA Daisuke, one of the most famous players the academy has ever produced. He came through the club's junior-youth and youth teams, and in 1998, still aged 17, became the youngest player ever to appear for Japan's senior national team; he later played at the 2002 World Cup. After retiring he returned to the club to coach, working his way up from the football school through the under-13, under-14 and under-15 teams, before joining the first-team staff in 2023, latterly (in 2025) as the first team's transition coach. When his appointment was announced, he said he wanted to build an environment in which the players become completely absorbed in football.

The two Club Youth titles came sixteen years apart: in 2002 S-Pulse beat Urawa Reds Youth in the final, and in 2018 they beat Omiya Ardija Youth. In 2016 they reached the final and lost to FC Tokyo U-18. The 2018 season was the club's best of the last two decades: Club Youth champions, J Youth Cup finalists and third in the Premier League EAST.

The club's junior-youth (under-15) team won all three national under-15 competitions in 2016, the JFA Premier Cup, the Club Youth (U-15) Championship and the Takamado Cup, and several of that group went on through the under-18s to professional contracts.

History

How Japanese youth football is organised: the under-18 teams of professional clubs and the football teams of high schools play in the same leagues. At the top is the national Premier League (split into EAST and WEST), below that nine regional Prince Leagues, and below those the prefectural leagues, with promotion and relegation between each level, and play-offs in December for promotion to the Premier League. Club academies such as S-Pulse's do not take part in the high-school tournaments (the summer Inter-High and the winter championship). Their own national competitions are the Japan Club Youth Championship, traditionally played in summer, and the J Youth Cup, a cup for J.League academies.

  • 1990s — Shimizu S-Pulse joins the J.League at its start in 1993 and builds an academy alongside it. The youth team wins its first national title, the J Youth Cup, in 1997.
  • 1998 — ICHIKAWA Daisuke, a product of the junior-youth and youth teams, becomes the youngest player ever to play for Japan's senior team, aged 17.
  • 2002 — A first Japan Club Youth Championship, beating Urawa Reds Youth in the final.
  • 2005 — A second J Youth Cup.
  • 2011–2021 — Members of the Premier League from its first season, always in the EAST division, finishing second four times. (In 2020 the Premier League was replaced by regional leagues because of the pandemic, and S-Pulse won the Tokai one.)
  • 2016 — Club Youth Championship runners-up; the junior-youth team wins the under-15 treble.
  • 2018 — A second Club Youth title, against Omiya Ardija Youth, and runners-up in the J Youth Cup.
  • 2022 — Moved to the Premier League WEST, finished eleventh and were relegated.
  • 2023–2025 — Second in the Prince League Tokai in 2023, then champions in 2024 and 2025. All three seasons ended in the promotion play-offs: in December 2025 the first-round match against Ehime FC U-18 finished 0-0 and was lost 3-1 on penalties.
  • 2026 — ICHIKAWA Daisuke becomes head coach. In August fifteen youth players are registered with the first team.

Development pathway

  • One pathway from the age of six. The club runs teams at every age, from under-12 sides in the Shimizu area to the junior-youth teams and the under-18s. Most of the current under-18 players registered with the first team came up through S-Pulse's own junior-youth team, and several started in the club's under-12 team in Shimizu.
  • Recruited from beyond Shizuoka. The academy also brings in players from outside the prefecture. OGAKI Teppei and MURAKAMI Eijiro both came from Yokohama FC's junior-youth team in Kanagawa; OZAWA Koshi came from FC Fuoriclasse Sendai in Miyagi, as did HARIU Ryota, who has since been promoted to the first team. UCHIDA Conan, the goalkeeper, was born in England.
  • Registered with the first team. In August 2026 the club registered fifteen youth players with the J.League as "type-2" players, a status that allows academy players to appear for the first team while remaining amateurs. They also play in the J.League's new under-21 competition alongside young professionals.
  • Graduates on the staff. The head coach is himself an academy graduate, and the first-team squad includes several homegrown players who came up through the same teams.
