V-Varen Nagasaki U-18

V-Varen Nagasaki U-18 is the under-18 team of V-Varen Nagasaki, the first professional football club in Nagasaki Prefecture and, since the 2026-27 season, a J1 club. The academy is young by J.League standards: the under-15 team was set up in April 2010, the under-12 team in April 2011, and the under-18 team began playing in 2012. It grew up in an unusual place. Nagasaki is the home of Kunimi High School, one of the most successful schools in the history of Japanese high-school football, and for decades the best teenage players in the prefecture went to schools like Kunimi rather than to a club. In 2026 the under-18s are top of Prince League Kyushu Division 1 with 11 wins from 13 matches, chasing a second shot at promotion to the national Premier League.

2026 season

Currently 1st in the Prince League Kyushu Division 1 with 34 points from 14 matches (11W 1D 2L, 36-20). Updated daily.

Playing style

The academy describes its aim as producing players "made in Nagasaki" who can think for themselves, and its credo, which it also uses for the under-18 team, is to spread "the joy of living in the moment" through football, in Nagasaki and beyond. Three values sit beneath that: independence (thinking and acting for yourself), taking the initiative on and off the pitch, and gratitude. In practice that means a team that is expected to keep the ball and make its own decisions rather than simply follow instructions. The 2026 side has been both productive and hard to beat: 33 points from its first 13 league matches, with a 3-1 win over Kunimi in April among them.

Some context for readers outside Japan. Youth football in Japan has two parallel worlds, school teams and club academies, and they meet in one league system. At the top is the national Premier League (EAST and WEST, twelve teams each). Below it are nine regional Prince Leagues, of which Prince League Kyushu is one, and below those the prefectural leagues. Promotion from Prince to Premier is not automatic: the regional champions and some runners-up go into a play-off in December, and only the winners of each small bracket go up.

Club academies such as V-Varen's do not enter the two big school competitions, the summer Inter-High and the winter All Japan High School Championship. Their national tournaments are instead the Japan Club Youth (U-18) Championship, played in the summer, and the J Youth Cup, which is open only to the academies of J.League clubs.

History

  • 2012 — The under-18 team begins playing, two years after the under-15s. In the early days numbers were so short that, by the account of one of its first captains, the team sometimes could not even arrange practice matches, and heavy defeats were common.
  • 2015 — A first appearance at the Japan Club Youth (U-18) Championship, and a first national win: 1-0 against Tokushima Vortis Youth with a goal in the closing minutes of the group stage.
  • 2021 — Champions of Prince League Kyushu, in the team's tenth season. In the Premier League play-off decider on 12 December, at Edion Stadium Hiroshima, they lost 2-0 to Maebashi Ikuei, both goals coming in the first half. Maebashi Ikuei went up instead.
  • 2022 — ABE Taisei, still an under-18 player, signs a professional contract with the club in January.
  • 2023–2025 — Regular appearances at the Club Youth Championship.
  • 2026 — In the J Youth Cup the team beats Oita Trinita U-18 1-0 and Sagan Tosu U-18 2-1 away, then Renofa Yamaguchi U-18 3-1 in the round of 16, before losing 1-0 to Vissel Kobe U-18 in the quarter-final on 14 June. In the league they win their first four matches, beat Kunimi 3-1 in April and lead Prince League Kyushu Division 1 into the autumn. Around 20 September they lose 3-0 to Kunimi, one of only two defeats in their first 13 matches.

Development pathway

  • A whole pathway in one club. The under-12, under-15 and under-18 teams share one way of playing, so a boy who joins at primary-school age can move up without having to unlearn anything. Several of the current under-18s came up from the club's own under-15 team.
  • Recruitment from across Kyushu and beyond. The academy no longer relies only on Nagasaki. The players registered with the first team in 2026 include boys from junior-youth clubs in Miyazaki, Kagoshima, Saga and Kumamoto, from the junior-youth teams of Cerezo Osaka and Sagan Tosu, and from the JFA Academy Fukushima. Competition for places inside the squad is correspondingly stiff.
  • Early contact with the first team. In Japan an academy player can be "second-type registered" with his club's professional squad, which lets him play senior football while still at school. In September 2026 seventeen under-18 players held that registration at V-Varen, and several have already played in the U-21 J.League, the new reserve competition for under-21 players.
  • More than one road to the top. Graduates have reached the professional game directly from the under-18s, by way of university, and by way of loans to lower-division clubs, and the club has been willing to let its best young players try Europe.

Former players

  • ABE Taisei (midfielder, Holstein Kiel, on loan from V-Varen Nagasaki) — the academy's best-known graduate. Called up to a Japan under-19 training camp in 2022 while still in the under-18s, he signed a professional contract in January that year. He started for Japan against Israel at the 2023 FIFA U-20 World Cup in Argentina. After a loan at FC Luzern in Switzerland he joined Holstein Kiel in the German second division on loan in June 2026, until June 2027.
  • EGAWA Yusei (defender, V-Varen Nagasaki) — joined the under-18s from a junior-youth club in Kumamoto, came through to the first team, and moved to Gamba Osaka. He returned to Nagasaki on a permanent transfer in June 2025 and is a regular in the J1 side.
  • NABESHIMA Harumu (midfielder, V-Varen Nagasaki) — another product of the under-18s who went to Toyo University before rejoining the club as a professional in 2026.
  • NANAMURE Aoto (forward, Kochi United SC) — came up through the under-18s to the first team, then went on loan to FC Osaka and FC Nobeoka Agata before a permanent move to Kochi United in June 2026.
