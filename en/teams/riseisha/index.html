Riseisha High School

Riseisha High School (履正社) is a private school in Toyonaka, in the northern suburbs of Osaka. Nationally the school is best known for baseball, but its football club has become one of the most productive in western Japan in a remarkably short time. The club was founded only in 2003, with HIRANO Naoki as its first head coach, and he is still in charge. Within ten years Riseisha had reached the quarter-finals of the winter All Japan High School Soccer Tournament, and it did so again the following year. It has since played three seasons in the U-18 Premier League WEST, the top tier of Japanese youth football. Its alumni include MACHINO Shuto, a member of Japan's squads at the 2022 and 2026 World Cups, and the Tokyo Olympian HAYASHI Daichi. In 2026 the team plays in Prince League Kansai Division 1, where it is third after 14 matches (eight wins, two draws, four defeats, 26 points).

Playing style

HIRANO has spoken often about coaching for "ten years from now" rather than for the next match. His priorities are accurate basic technique, simple one- and two-touch passing, and above all decision-making. He wants players who read the game early enough to create numerical superiority in both attack and defence. When the team entered the 2020–21 winter championship, its stated aim was "dynamic, speedy and technical football that captivates the people watching". Before the 2022 Premier League season he explained a change of emphasis to the JFA. In Riseisha's first Premier season, in 2015, he felt the team had "respected our opponents too much" and concentrated too hard on not conceding. The new aim was to stop opponents from playing to their strengths so that Riseisha could show its own, and to press from the front. His reasoning was that players who hope to reach the Olympic team and the national team, and to beat European and South American sides, have to be able to do exactly that.

History

  • 2003 — The football club is founded, with HIRANO Naoki as head coach. A former Inter-High winner as a player at Yokkaichi Chuo Kogyo, he went on to play for Matsushita Electric, the forerunner of Gamba Osaka, and to coach in the youth systems of Gamba Osaka and Vegalta Sendai. In its early years the club took only 16 players per year group.
  • 2011 — The club's training ground in the city of Ibaraki, north of Osaka, becomes a dedicated artificial-turf football pitch.
  • 2013–14 — First appearance at the winter championship. Riseisha beats Akita Shogyo 2-0 and Tokushima Shiritsu 1-0, then knocks out Aomori Yamada on penalties after a 1-1 draw. In the quarter-final it draws 1-1 with Yokkaichi Chuo Kogyo, HIRANO's old school, and loses the shoot-out 5-6.
  • 2014–15 — Back at the winter championship: Riseisha wins a shoot-out against Hokkaido Otani Muroran after a goalless draw, beats Nakatsu Higashi 5-1, and loses 0-1 to Seiryo in the quarter-final.
  • 2015 — A first season in the Premier League WEST, which ends in ninth place and relegation. In the summer the team reaches the quarter-finals of the Inter-High.
  • 2016 — A second Inter-High quarter-final, lost 0-1 to RKU Kashiwa in Hiroshima.
  • 2018 — HIRANO, now head coach of the Japan High School Selection team, leads it to victory at the Düsseldorf International Youth Tournament in Germany. The selection beats Borussia Mönchengladbach 2-1 in the final.
  • 2020–21 — A third winter championship, which ends with a 1-2 defeat by Teikyo Nagaoka in Riseisha's opening match.
  • 2021 — Champions of Prince League Kansai Division 1. Riseisha then wins promotion through the national play-off, played in Hiroshima.
  • 2022 — Eighth in the Premier League WEST, which secures a second season at the top level. Riseisha reaches the last 16 of the summer Inter-High. In the 2022–23 winter championship it wins two matches before losing on penalties to Sano Nichidai in the third round.
  • 2023 — Twelfth in the Premier League WEST, which means relegation to Prince League Kansai Division 1.
  • 2024–25 — Eighth in Prince League Kansai Division 1 in 2024 and third in 2025. Riseisha is runner-up in the Osaka qualifying tournament for the winter championship in both years.
  • 2026 — MACHINO Shuto is called into Japan's World Cup squad as a late replacement, as he had been in 2022. Riseisha goes out of the Osaka Inter-High qualifiers in the quarter-finals, beaten 0-3 by Tokai University Gyosei. In the league it is third in Prince League Kansai Division 1 after 14 matches.

Development pathway

For readers new to Japanese youth football: high-school teams and J.League academies play in the same national league pyramid. At the top is the U-18 Premier League, split into EAST and WEST. Below it are nine regional Prince Leagues, then the prefectural leagues. Two high-school tournaments carry even more prestige than the league: the summer Inter-High, and the winter championship, which is televised nationally and ends at the National Stadium in January. Japanese school years run from April, which is why winter tournaments are dated across two calendar years.

  • No scholarships, only volunteers. Many leading private schools waive tuition fees for football recruits. HIRANO has said that Riseisha does not. The players who come do so because they want to be coached here, and the club has grown from 16 to about 25 players per year group.
  • The lights that alumni paid for. The floodlights at the Ibaraki ground are LED lamps, and new ones have been added as former players have turned professional. Every evening session is played under lights that the club's professionals helped to pay for.
  • A second chance in a crowded city. Osaka is home to the academies of Gamba Osaka and Cerezo Osaka and to strong school rivals such as Kindai University High School and Tokai University Gyosei. Riseisha has become a destination for players who are not promoted within those academies. MYOGAN Toya came to the school from Gamba Osaka's junior-youth team and joined Kawasaki Frontale three years later.
  • The university route as well as the direct one. Some Riseisha players go straight to the J.League, as MACHINO Shuto and HIRAOKA Taiyo did. Others, such as HAYASHI Daichi and TANAKA Shunta, went first to the Osaka University of Health and Sport Sciences and became full internationals or Olympians from there.

Former players

  • MACHINO Shuto (forward, Borussia Mönchengladbach) — joined Yokohama F. Marinos straight from the school in 2018. He rebuilt his career at Giravanz Kitakyushu and Shonan Bellmare, then moved to Germany with Holstein Kiel in 2023 and to Borussia Mönchengladbach in July 2025. He was added to Japan's squads for both the 2022 and the 2026 World Cups as an injury replacement, the second time for the injured captain.
  • HAYASHI Daichi (forward, Gamba Osaka) — went from Riseisha to the Osaka University of Health and Sport Sciences, then to Sagan Tosu. He played at the Tokyo Olympics and then signed for Sint-Truiden in Belgium. After a loan at 1. FC Nürnberg in Germany he came back to Osaka with Gamba in 2024, and he wears the number 9.
  • TANAKA Shunta (midfielder, Cerezo Osaka) — He won his first Japan cap in December 2019 while still a university student, before joining Hokkaido Consadole Sapporo in 2020. He moved to Cerezo Osaka in 2024 and wears the number 10.
  • HIRAOKA Taiyo (midfielder, Kyoto Sanga F.C.) — joined Shonan Bellmare from the school in 2021 and spent five seasons there. He moved to Kyoto on a permanent transfer for 2026.
  • MYOGAN Toya (midfielder, Tochigi SC) — a dribbler who came through Gamba Osaka's junior-youth team before Riseisha. He joined Kawasaki Frontale in 2023 and had development loans at Vegalta Sendai. Since June 2026 he has been on a development loan at Tochigi SC, which runs until June 2027.
