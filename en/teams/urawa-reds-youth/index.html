Urawa Red Diamonds Youth

Urawa Red Diamonds Youth is the under-18 team of Urawa Reds, the Saitama club with some of the biggest crowds in Japanese football. It won the national under-18 title, the Prince Takamado Cup, in 2008. It won the J Youth Cup and the first J.League International Youth Cup in 2015, and the Japan Club Youth Championship back in 1997. Its graduates include Japan's goalkeeper SUZUKI Zion, who joined Aston Villa in August 2026, and the internationals HARAGUCHI Genki, HASHIOKA Daiki and ITO Atsuki. ABE Yuki, a former Urawa captain and Japan midfielder, has coached the team since 2025. In 2026 it is back in the regional Prince League Kanto Division 1 after one season in the national Premier League. It led the table until mid-September and was fourth after 14 of its 18 matches.

Saitama · head coach ABE Yuki

2026 season

Currently 5th in the Prince League Kanto Division 1 with 21 points from 14 matches (6W 3D 5L, 17-18). Updated daily.

Playing style

In its 2025 JFA team listing, the club gives the team's style as "everyone attacks, everyone defends" (全員攻撃・全員守備). ABE Yuki captained Urawa as a player and played for Japan. He joined the academy staff as a youth-team coach in 2022 and was made head coach of the under-18s for 2025. The club kept him in charge for 2026 even after relegation. For 2026 the club also added a former first-team striker as a coach. He works with both the under-18s and the club's new under-21 side. So the people who teach the academy's football are the people who played it for the first team.

In the 2015 J Youth Cup final, at Yanmar Stadium Nagai in Osaka, Urawa came from a goal down to beat Nagoya Grampus U18 2-1, for the club's first win in the competition. A month later they won the first J.League International Youth Cup, a December tournament against academy teams from Japan and abroad. Along the way they beat AZ Alkmaar 2-0, and HASHIOKA Daiki scored the second goal. The Club Youth Championship finals were lost to Shimizu S-Pulse Youth in 2002, Sanfrecce Hiroshima F.C Youth in 2003 and FC Tokyo U-18 in 2017.

History

  • 1990s — Urawa Reds were one of the original J.League clubs. Their youth team won the Japan Club Youth Championship in 1997, beating Kashima Antlers Youth in the final.
  • 2002–2003 — Two Club Youth Championship finals in a row, both lost.
  • 2007–2008 — Third in the Prince Takamado Cup in 2007, then national champions in 2008. At that time the competition was still a knockout tournament for club academies and high schools from every region.
  • 2011–2012 — The national Premier League starts in 2011, with Urawa as a founder member of the EAST division. They finish fourth in the first season, then ninth, and go down to the Prince League in 2013.
  • 2015 — The club's first J Youth Cup and the first J.League International Youth Cup, both in the autumn.
  • 2016 — Prince League Kanto champions, then promoted through the national play-off. The team stays in the Premier League from 2017 to 2021. Its best finish is third in the EAST in 2019, and it reaches the Club Youth final in 2017.
  • 2021 — Relegated to the Prince League after a poor Premier League season.
  • 2022–2024 — Sixth, second and third in Prince League Kanto Division 1. Third place in 2024 is enough for the promotion play-off in December. There Urawa beat Kyoto Sanga F.C. U-18 on penalties (1-1, 4-3 on penalties) and go up.
  • 2025 — ABE Yuki's first season as head coach ends with relegation. The team finishes eleventh of twelve in the Premier League EAST, with 5 wins, 4 draws and 13 defeats.
  • 2026 — A strong start in Prince League Kanto Division 1: 6 wins, 2 draws and 1 defeat in the first nine matches, and top of the table. Then a 2-2 draw and four straight defeats from September, ending with a 3-0 loss to Kiryu Daiichi on 3 October. That left Urawa fourth with 21 points after 14 matches (6 wins, 3 draws, 5 defeats), with four league games left.

A note on the competitions for readers outside Japan. The top level for under-18 teams is the Premier League, a national league split into EAST and WEST. Below it are nine regional Prince Leagues, and below those the prefectural leagues. The best Prince League teams play a national play-off in December for promotion. Club academies such as Urawa play in these leagues alongside high-school teams. They do not enter the high-school competitions, the summer Inter-High and the winter championship. They have two cup competitions of their own instead. The Japan Club Youth (U-18) Championship is usually played in summer, and the J Youth Cup is for J.League academies. In 2026 the Club Youth Championship has moved. The Kanto qualifying knockout rounds begin in late October, and the national finals are set for late December in Hiroshima and Yamaguchi.

Development pathway

  • A step to the first team, inside the academy. Two current youth players, the defender TANAKA Yoshitaka and the midfielder WADA Takeshi, signed professional contracts at the start of 2026 while still at the academy. WADA was sixteen and had played for Japan at the 2025 Under-17 World Cup. Both hold first-team squad numbers, as do NAKAMURA Kotaro and TAKAHASHI Atsuro. More than a dozen other youth players are registered with the first team, which lets them be called up for senior matches.
  • An under-21 team from July 2026. In 2026 the club set up an under-21 side. It gives players who have just left the youth team, or who are close to the first team, regular competitive football. Some of the youth coaches work with both groups.
  • The club's own junior-youth team and the city around it. Many of the squad come up from Urawa's under-15 team, the Urawa Reds Junior Youth. WADA and TAKAHASHI are examples. Others join at sixteen from strong local clubs in and around Saitama: TANAKA came from FC Liberta and NAKAMURA from Arcobaleno FC.
  • University as a second route. Not every graduate signs straight away. ITO Atsuki went from the youth team to Ryutsu Keizai University and came back to Urawa in 2021. Two years later he was a Japan international. INAGAKI Atsushi, another youth-team graduate now at Meiji University, is registered with the first team as a special designated player while he completes his studies.

Former players

  • SUZUKI Zion (goalkeeper, Aston Villa, England) — came through Urawa's academy, played for the first team as a teenager, then moved to Europe with Sint-Truiden and Parma. He is Japan's goalkeeper and joined Aston Villa in August 2026.
  • HARAGUCHI Genki (midfielder, Beerschot, Belgium) — a youth-team product of the late 2000s. He spent most of his career in Germany and won many caps for Japan. He moved from Urawa to Beerschot in 2025.
  • HASHIOKA Daiki (defender, Borussia Mönchengladbach, Germany) — scored against AZ Alkmaar when the youth team won the 2015 International Youth Cup. A Japan international, he joined Mönchengladbach on loan from Slavia Prague in July 2026.
  • ITO Atsuki (midfielder, Bolton Wanderers, England) — went from the youth team to university and back to Urawa. He scored on his first start for Japan, against Türkiye in 2023. After two seasons with Gent he joined Bolton in August 2026.
  • KUDO Kota (defender), HORIUCHI Yota (midfielder) and HAYAKAWA Jumpei (midfielder) — academy graduates in the Urawa first team in 2026-27. KUDO and HORIUCHI were in the youth side that was relegated in 2021, and HAYAKAWA was a first-year in that team.
  • YAJIMA Shinya (midfielder, Gainare Tottori), MOTEGI Rikiya (defender, RB Omiya Ardija) and OKAMOTO Takuya (defender, Fujieda MYFC) — youth-team graduates who signed for Urawa and have had long professional careers.
  • MATSUO Yusuke (forward, FC Machida Zelvia) — went from the youth team to Sendai University, then turned professional with Yokohama FC and later played for Urawa and for Westerlo in Belgium.
  • ISHII Ryo (goalkeeper, Yokohama FC), IZAWA Haruki (midfielder, Giravanz Kitakyushu) and ARAI Mizuki (midfielder, Mito HollyHock) — more recent graduates now playing elsewhere in the J.League.
