RB Omiya Ardija U-18

RB Omiya Ardija U-18 is the under-18 team of RB Omiya Ardija, the professional club from the city of Saitama, just north of Tokyo. For most of its history the club was simply Omiya Ardija, owned by the telecoms company NTT East. In 2024 the Austrian drinks company Red Bull bought it, and from January 2025 the whole club, academy included, has carried the "RB" initials, joining a group of Red Bull-backed clubs that includes teams in Austria, Germany, the United States and Brazil. The academy itself is older and more successful than its modest public profile suggests. The under-18s spent nine seasons in the Premier League, the national top flight of youth football, between 2015 and 2024, finishing third in the EAST division in 2022; they reached the final of the Japan Club Youth Championship in 2015; and the club's best-known recent graduate, the centre-back ICHIHARA Rion, moved from Saitama to AZ Alkmaar in the Netherlands in January 2026. In 2026 the team plays one level down, in the Prince League Kanto Division 1, and was fifth after twelve matches.

Playing style

The academy has always aimed to produce players for its own first team, and Red Bull's ownership has given that aim a particular shape. Red Bull clubs around the world share a recognisable way of playing — aggressive pressing high up the pitch, quick vertical passes once the ball is won, and a high tempo throughout — and the aim within the group is for teams at every age level to play in the same way, so that a player moving up from the under-18s already knows what the first team expects.

What has not changed is the emphasis on young players being given senior football early. In the 2026–27 season more than a dozen players of high-school age hold numbers in the first-team squad, and some have already made their professional debuts.

Some context for readers outside Japan. Youth football here runs on two tracks: high-school teams and the academies of professional clubs. Both meet in one league pyramid — the national Premier League (split into EAST and WEST), nine regional Prince Leagues beneath it, and prefectural leagues below that. Club academies such as Omiya's do not take part in the high-school competitions, the summer Inter-High and the winter All Japan High School Championship. Their own national cups are the Japan Club Youth Championship, traditionally played in summer (the 2026 finals are scheduled for 23–29 December in Hiroshima and Yamaguchi), and the J Youth Cup for J.League academies. Seasons follow the Japanese school year, which begins in April.

The record in the cups is one of near misses. The 2015 Club Youth final was lost to Yokohama F.Marinos Youth, and in 2019 the team reached the last four of the J Youth Cup, a competition won that year by Nagoya Grampus U-18. The same winter the under-18s reached the final of the J.League International Youth Cup, an invitational tournament for academies from Japan and abroad, before losing to the Ghanaian academy Right to Dream.

History

  • 1999 — Omiya Ardija, the former works team of NTT's Kanto branch, joins the newly created second division of the J.League. The academy develops alongside the professional club.
  • 2005 — A first Club Youth Championship semi-final.
  • 2014 — Champions of the Prince League Kanto, which brings promotion to the Premier League, and semi-finalists in the Club Youth Championship.
  • 2015 — A first Premier League EAST season, and a place in the Club Youth Championship final, lost to Yokohama F.Marinos Youth.
  • 2017 — Relegated from the Premier League after three seasons.
  • 2018–2019 — Third in the Prince League Kanto and promoted again; in 2019 the team reaches the J Youth Cup semi-finals and the final of the J.League International Youth Cup.
  • 2022 — Third in the Premier League EAST, the academy's best league finish.
  • 2024 — Red Bull agrees in August to buy the club from NTT East; on 1 October the operating company is renamed RB Omiya Co., Ltd. On the pitch, the under-18s finish twelfth and last in the EAST and are relegated. The first team, meanwhile, wins the J3 League with a record points total.
  • 2025 — The club plays as RB Omiya Ardija from January. The under-18s finish third in the Prince League Kanto Division 1 and enter the December play-off for promotion: they beat Nissho Gakuen High School 1-0 in the first round, then lose 5-1 to Jubilo Iwata U-18 with a Premier League place at stake.
  • 2026 — ICHIHARA Rion leaves for AZ Alkmaar in January. In the Prince League Kanto Division 1 the under-18s have five wins, four draws and three defeats from twelve matches (19 points), fifth in the table.

Development pathway

  • One club, top to bottom. The academy runs teams at under-12, under-15 and under-18 level, and many of the best players have been at the club since primary school. The goalkeeper KATO Yuki and the midfielders OYAMA Keisuke and KUROKAWA Atsushi all came through the junior-youth (under-15) and youth teams before turning professional.
  • Early contracts. The club offers professional contracts to players while they are still of school age. In the 2026–27 J.League squad, more than ten players born in 2008, 2009 and 2010 hold first-team numbers; some, like KIDERA Sunao, signed straight out of the club's under-15 team.
  • Recruitment beyond Saitama. In January 2026 the club signed the forward MCGHEE Jelani Ren, then 17, from FC Ryukyu in Okinawa after he had played for Japan at the 2025 Under-17 World Cup — an example of the club looking nationwide for players of academy age.
  • The Red Bull network. Being part of a group with clubs on three continents opens new routes. In February 2026 an 18-year-old academy-raised defender was loaned to New York Red Bulls II in the United States, a move within the group that would not have existed before 2024.

Former players

  • ICHIHARA Rion (defender, AZ Alkmaar) — came through the club's academy, made his first-team debut in 2023 and was part of the side that won the J3 title in 2024. He captained Japan's under-23 team and joined AZ in the Dutch Eredivisie in January 2026.
  • OKUNUKI Kanji (midfielder, Gamba Osaka) — joined the first team from the youth team in 2018, then played in Poland for Górnik Zabrze and in Germany for 1. FC Nürnberg before returning to Japan with Gamba Osaka in 2025. He won his first Japan cap on 1 January 2024, against Thailand.
  • SHIBAYAMA Masaya (midfielder, Cerezo Osaka) — joined the junior-youth team from Gunma in 2015 and stayed through the under-18s; he moved from Omiya to Cerezo Osaka in the J1 League in July 2023.
  • KUROKAWA Atsushi (midfielder, Ventforet Kofu) — a youth-team graduate of 2016 whose career has taken in Mito HollyHock, Jubilo Iwata, FC Machida Zelvia and spells in Bulgaria and Latvia.
  • OYAMA Keisuke (midfielder, Zweigen Kanazawa) — at the club from the junior team upwards, and a member of the first-team squad in Saitama for a decade before moving to Kanazawa.
  • KATO Yuki (goalkeeper, Iwaki FC) and TAKADA Soya (midfielder, Tokushima Vortis) — two more graduates still playing in the J.League; TAKADA joined the under-18s from a local junior-youth club in Saitama.
