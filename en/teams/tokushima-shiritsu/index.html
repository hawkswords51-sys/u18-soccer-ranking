Tokushima Shiritsu High School

Tokushima Shiritsu High School (徳島市立高校, "Tokushima Municipal High School", usually shortened to "Shiritsu") is a public high school run by the city of Tokushima, on the island of Shikoku. It is one of very few city-run state schools in Japan to have won national titles in football. In 1991 it won the All Japan Youth (U-18) Championship, a national competition open to both school teams and professional club academies, and in 1992 it won the summer Inter-High. It is also the most successful school side in the Prince League Shikoku, which it won in 2014 and 2015. In 2019 it reached the quarter-finals of both national high-school tournaments, and in December 2025 it played, and won, the opening match of the winter championship at the National Stadium in Tokyo. In 2026 the team plays in the Prince League Shikoku, where it is fourth after 14 matches with 6 wins, 5 draws and 3 defeats (23 points). It did not qualify for the 2026 Inter-High.

Playing style

As a state school, Shiritsu cannot offer the sports scholarships, boarding houses and large squads that many private football schools rely on. Most of its players come from clubs in and around Tokushima, although the official squad list for the 2025–26 winter championship also included players from junior clubs in the Kansai region, a short drive away across Awaji Island, such as Salpa FC in Hyogo and Diablossa Takada FC in Nara.

The 2025–26 winter championship showed both sides of the team. In the opening match at the National Stadium on 28 December 2025, Shiritsu beat Waseda Jitsugyo of Tokyo 4-1, its first win at the championship for six years. Three days later, at Todoroki in Kawasaki, it lost 1-3 to Seiwa Gakuen from Miyagi in the second round, having equalised through a defender before falling away in the second half.

History

  • 1991 — Shiritsu wins the All Japan Youth (U-18) Championship. At the time this was the only national competition in which high-school teams and the youth sides of professional and company clubs met head to head.
  • 1992 — The club wins the summer Inter-High, its first national high-school title.
  • 1993–94 — At the 72nd winter championship, Shiritsu is beaten 0-1 by Toko Gakuen High School, who were making their first appearance.
  • 1995 — A midfielder from the 1993–95 year groups leaves the school for Avispa Fukuoka. He goes on to play more than 400 J.League matches and wins two caps for Japan in 2001, the first in a 2-0 win over Paraguay at the Kirin Cup on 1 July.
  • 2014 — Shiritsu wins the Prince League Shikoku for the first time. It loses in the year-end play-off for a place in the national Premier League.
  • 2015 — A second consecutive Prince League Shikoku title, and a second play-off defeat.
  • 2019 — The best season of the modern era. In the summer the team reaches the Inter-High quarter-finals in Okinawa and is beaten 1-3 by Toyama Daiichi High School. In the winter championship (the 98th edition) it again reaches the last eight, where it loses 0-4 at Komazawa Olympic Park on 5 January 2020 to Shizuoka Gakuen High School, who go on to win the title.
  • 2023 — Runners-up in the Prince League Shikoku, behind Tokushima Vortis Youth.
  • 2025–26 — Shiritsu wins the Tokushima qualifier for the winter championship for the fourth year running and the 22nd time in all. Drawn in the opening match at the National Stadium, it beats Waseda Jitsugyo 4-1, then loses 1-3 to Seiwa Gakuen in the second round.
  • 2026 — In June the team is beaten by Naruto in the semi-finals of the prefectural Inter-High qualifier. It had been bidding for a 12th consecutive prefectural title, and Naruto go on to win the qualifier. In the league, Shiritsu is fourth in the Prince League Shikoku after 14 matches.

Development pathway

For readers new to Japanese youth football: high-school teams and J.League academies play in the same national league pyramid. At the top is the U-18 Premier League (EAST and WEST), then nine regional Prince Leagues, then the prefectural leagues. Two high-school tournaments carry even more prestige than the league: the summer Inter-High and the winter All Japan High School Soccer Tournament, which is televised nationally and ends in January. School years in Japan run from April to March, so a winter championship played in December and January belongs to the school year that began the previous April; this page writes it as "2025–26".

  • A state school among private academies. Shiritsu is funded by the city of Tokushima. It does not run the large, residential football programmes of private schools such as Kamimura Gakuen or Aomori Yamada, and most of its players are local.
  • The leading school side in Shikoku's league. The Prince League Shikoku is usually dominated by the academies of the region's J.League clubs, Ehime FC, Tokushima Vortis and Kamatamare Sanuki. Shiritsu's two titles in 2014 and 2015, and its runner-up finishes since, make it the strongest high-school team in the league's recent history.
  • Tokushima's representative on the national stage. Inside Tokushima Prefecture the club has long been the team to beat. Before its semi-final defeat by Naruto in June 2026 it had won the prefectural Inter-High qualifier eleven times in a row, and it has represented the prefecture at the winter championship 22 times.
  • A route to the professional game through university. Recent graduates have usually reached the J.League after four years of university football rather than straight from school: Kanoya National Institute of Fitness and Sports, Sanno University and Kanto Gakuin University have all been stepping stones for Shiritsu players.

Former players

  • OKUDA Yudai (defender, Nara Club) — came through the junior and junior-youth teams of Tokushima Vortis, then played his high-school football at Shiritsu before studying at Kanoya National Institute of Fitness and Sports. He turned professional with his home-town club Tokushima Vortis, and has since played for Iwaki FC, Tegevajaro Miyazaki and Kamatamare Sanuki. He is now with Nara Club in J3.
  • OKA Kenta (forward, Vanraure Hachinohe) — went from Shiritsu to Sanno University and then played for ReinMeer Aomori in the Japan Football League. He joined Vanraure Hachinohe and made his J.League debut in August 2026.
  • NAKATA Shunki (midfielder, AC Nagano Parceiro) — a Tokushima native who played for a local junior club before Shiritsu, then for Kanto Gakuin University. He joined AC Nagano Parceiro and made his J.League debut on 16 August 2026.

The school's most famous former player is the midfielder described above under 1995, who played for Avispa Fukuoka, Sanfrecce Hiroshima, Nagoya Grampus Eight, Vissel Kobe, Omiya Ardija and Roasso Kumamoto, and was capped twice by Japan in 2001. After retiring in 2014 he coached at Roasso Kumamoto and managed AC Nagano Parceiro, and in June 2026 he joined Júbilo Iwata as head coach, the role of chief assistant to the manager in Japanese football.
