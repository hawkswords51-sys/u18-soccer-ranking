Maebashi Ikuei High School

Maebashi Ikuei High School — 前橋育英 in Japanese — is one of the small group of school teams that have kept pace with J.League academies. It is based in Maebashi, in Gunma, about an hour north of Tokyo, and plays in the Premier League EAST. The football club was founded with the school in 1963 and was unremarkable for twenty years; everything it is now dates from 1982, when YAMADA Kosuke arrived at the age of 22 and began a spell as manager that is still going. The yellow-and-black stripes — "tiger black" — are one of the most recognisable kits in Japanese school football.

Gunma · founded 1963 · head coach YAMADA Kosuke

2026 season

Currently 5th in the Premier League EAST with 22 points from 15 matches (6W 4D 5L, 24-19). Updated daily.

Playing style

The club's slogan translates roughly as "outrun them, win the second ball, win the duel". That sounds like a description of effort alone, but it is paired with a coaching philosophy that deliberately withholds answers: the manager's stated method is to watch, and to push players towards deciding for themselves. Japanese writers call the result "organised freedom" — a clear collective framework, with the individual reading of each moment left to the player. In practice it produces an aggressive, direct team that attacks with several players and defends with all of them.

The 2017 final was won 1-0 against RKU Kashiwa; the 2024 final, played in January 2025, went to penalties. Between 1997 and 2008 the club reached the national semi-finals four times without winning, a period Japanese reporters called its "silver collector" years. What followed is the point: the titles of 2017 and 2024 came from two completely different generations.

History

  • 1963 — The school opens in Maebashi and the football club starts with it. Northern Kanto is baseball country, and for two decades the club is a local one.
  • 1982 — YAMADA Kosuke, a graduate of Shimabara Shogyo in Nagasaki and Hosei University, arrives at 22 as a social studies teacher and manager. He begins not with tactics but with behaviour: punctuality, greetings, how the group lives.
  • 1997–2008 — Four national semi-finals at the winter championship, and no final.
  • 2009 — A first national title, at the Inter-High.
  • 2014, 2016 — Two winter finals lost, the second of them to Aomori Yamada.
  • 2017 — The winter championship, at last, beating RKU Kashiwa 1-0 in the final.
  • 2022 — A second Inter-High title.
  • 2024 — A second winter championship, decided on penalties, in the final played in January 2025.
  • 2026 — YAMADA Kosuke additionally becomes general manager of Thespa Gunma, the professional club in the same city, while remaining the school's manager: an unusual bridge between a school programme and a J.League club.

Development pathway

Four things hold the programme together.

  • A coach who refuses to give the answer. "There is no magic recipe" is his own phrase. Rather than drilling a solution into the team, he asks players to know their own strengths and work out how to use them inside the collective. The stated aim is a footballer who decides, not one who waits for instructions.
  • A slogan that is really a physical standard. Running, transition speed and duels are exactly what modern professional football asks for, so a player who has lived by that slogan for three years arrives at a J.League club already fit for the demands.
  • Its own pitch, and a national scouting net. The club owns a full-size artificial pitch in Takasaki that meets competition standards, so training happens in near-professional conditions all year. The squad is drawn from J.League academies in Tokyo and Saitama, from local Gunma clubs, and from as far away as Fukushima and Sendai.
  • A pipeline from local clubs. Rather than only an attached junior high, the school works closely with strong junior-youth clubs in Gunma, which gives it a long view of local players from the age of 12.

Former players

  • MATSUDA Naoki (defender) — a Japan international at the 2002 World Cup and for years the leader of Yokohama F. Marinos' defence. He died in 2011, while still playing, and remains the figure the school's players are pointed towards.
  • YAMAGUCHI Motohiro (midfielder) — a Japan international at the 1998 World Cup.
  • HOSOGAI Hajime (midfielder) — Urawa Reds, then Hertha Berlin in Germany and Bursaspor in Turkey. He retired in 2024 and is now the president of Thespa Gunma.
  • AOYAMA Naoaki (defender) — a long J.League career including Shimizu S-Pulse.
  • SUZUKI Tokuma (Gamba Osaka) and WATANABE Ryoma (Urawa Reds) — both from the beaten finalist sides of the 2010s.
  • TAKENOYA Yugasubedi (midfielder) — joined Montedio Yamagata straight from school in 2026.

Scouts tend to describe the school's graduates the same way: players who solve problems on the pitch themselves, and who do not avoid a physical contest.

Players to watch in 2026

Players worth knowing in the 2026 season:

  • TATEISHI Hinata (forward, third year) — the side's main scorer, with the winning goal against FC Tokyo U-18 and a hat-trick against Kashima Antlers Youth in the 2026 Premier League EAST.
  • YAMAMOTO Tsubasa (midfielder, third year) — the captain, a defensive midfielder who covers ground and gets on the scoresheet.
  • SEKI Aoba (forward, third year) — scored against Kawasaki Frontale U-18.
  • FUKAMI Shota (defender, third year) — played every minute of the 2026 Inter-High and scored in it.
  • ANDO Daichi (midfielder, third year) — also on the scoresheet at the Inter-High.
  • NEGISHI Seiya (goalkeeper, third year) — the first choice, one of three goalkeepers in the year group who came from three different regions.

The defence is a good illustration of the scouting net: players from FC Tokyo's and Urawa's junior-youth teams, from clubs in Saitama and Chiba, from Sendai, and from the local Maebashi FC, in the same back line.
