Nissho Gakuen High School

Nissho Gakuen High School (日章学園, usually just "Nissho") is a private school in Miyazaki City, on the south-east coast of Kyushu, Japan's third-largest island. It is the strongest football school in Miyazaki Prefecture. It has played at the winter All Japan High School Soccer Tournament 19 times and the summer Inter-High 20 times, and it has reached the quarter-finals of each twice. Most of its recent success has come under the current head coach, a former player at the school who took over in 2020. In 2025 the team won Prince League Kyushu Division 1 for the first time. In February 2026 it won the Kyushu U-17 tournament, beating Ohzu High School 4-1 in the final, and that summer it reached the Inter-High quarter-finals. Its best-known former player is TAKAOKA Rento, who scored all four of Japan's group-stage goals at the 2023 FIFA U-17 World Cup and went from the school to Southampton. In 2026 the first team plays in Prince League Kyushu Division 1, and a second team plays in Division 2.

Playing style

The head coach sums up the team's football in four principles: winning duels, fast transitions, work rate and intensity. The club also has a rule that school life comes first and football is built on top of it. The 2026 Inter-High showed a team that keeps games close: four matches, five goals scored and four conceded, and none of them decided by more than one goal. Nissho Gakuen beat Yonago Kita 2-1, got past Meio of Akita on penalties after a 1-1 draw, and beat Toyama Daiichi 1-0. In the quarter-final it lost 1-2 to Kindai University High School, who went on to reach the final.

History

  • 1985 — A new head coach arrives. As a player he had captained Teikyo to a winter national title and then played in the Japan Soccer League for Furukawa Electric. The school was then called Miyazaki Jitsugyo; it later became Nissho Gakuen.
  • 1985–2020 — His 35 years in charge make the school a regular at national tournaments: 15 appearances at the winter championship and 14 at the Inter-High.
  • 2006–2019 — The attached junior high school wins the national junior-high championship four times. It becomes the main source of players for the high-school team.
  • 2010–11 — First run to the quarter-finals of the winter championship, including a penalty shoot-out win over Shizuoka Gakuen High School in the third round.
  • 2013–14 — Quarter-finalists in the winter championship again, losing 0-4 to Toyama Daiichi, who went on to win the title.
  • 2017 — First All-Kyushu High School Tournament title.
  • 2018 — First Inter-High quarter-final.
  • 2020 — The long-serving coach leaves the school in March. In April he is replaced by a former Nissho Gakuen player who had been teaching and coaching there.
  • 2023–2025 — Three seasons in a row near the top of Prince League Kyushu Division 1. Each time the team reaches the play-off for promotion to the national Premier League, and each time it loses its first match by one goal. In 2025 it is beaten 0-1 by RB Omiya Ardija U18.
  • 2024 — Second All-Kyushu title. TAKAOKA Rento finishes at the school and joins Southampton in 2025.
  • 2025 — Wins Prince League Kyushu Division 1 for the first time. The second team wins promotion to Prince League Kyushu Division 2. In the winter championship the first team loses 1-2 to Kanazawa Gakuin University High School in the first round.
  • 2026 — Wins the Kyushu U-17 tournament in February, beating Ohzu 4-1 in the final. In June it beats Hosho 1-0 in the Miyazaki final to qualify for the Inter-High for the third year in a row and the 20th time overall, and finishes as runner-up in the All-Kyushu tournament. At the Inter-High in Fukushima it reaches the quarter-finals.

Development pathway

For readers new to Japanese youth football: high-school teams and J.League academies play in the same national league pyramid. At the top are the U-18 Premier League (EAST and WEST), then nine regional Prince Leagues, then the prefectural leagues. Two high-school knockout tournaments carry even more prestige than the league: the summer Inter-High and the winter championship, which is televised nationally and ends at the National Stadium in January.

  • The dominant school in its prefecture. Its main rivals in Miyazaki are Hosho and Miyazaki Nichidai, but Nissho Gakuen has won the prefecture's winter-championship qualifying four years running (2022–2025) and its Inter-High qualifying three years running (2024–2026).
  • A six-year pathway. The school runs its own junior high, which is one of the most successful in Japan. Five of the professionals listed below — FUJIOKA Kosuke, TAKADA Ryota, MURATA Koichi, YAMADA Takafumi and ABE Ryota — played for Nissho Gakuen Junior High before moving up to the high school.
  • Recruitment beyond Miyazaki. The school also signs players from junior clubs across Kyushu and further away.
  • Strength in depth. The second team plays in Prince League Kyushu Division 2 and a third team also plays league football, so players outside the first team still get regular competitive matches.
  • A related school. The same educational group runs Kagoshima Josei High School in neighbouring Kagoshima Prefecture. OSAKO Yuya, who played for Japan at the World Cup, went to Kagoshima Josei. He is not a Nissho Gakuen alumnus.

Former players

  • TAKAOKA Rento (forward, Patro Eisden Maasmechelen) — left the school in 2025 to sign for Southampton. In 2023, while still a pupil, he scored all four of Japan's group-stage goals at the FIFA U-17 World Cup, and in 2025 he was picked for Japan's squad at the U-20 World Cup in Chile. He spent 2025–26 on loan at Valenciennes in France's third tier, the Championnat National, but a stress fracture in his foot ended that spell in April 2026. In July 2026, without having played for Southampton's first team, he moved to Patro Eisden Maasmechelen in the Belgian second division.
  • FUJIOKA Kosuke (forward, Renofa Yamaguchi) — went straight from the school to Fagiano Okayama, then played for Tegevajaro Miyazaki, FC Gifu and FC Imabari. He was the J3 League's top scorer in 2024 and joined Renofa Yamaguchi for 2026.
  • TAKADA Ryota (defender, Vegalta Sendai) — went to Hannan University, then played for Blaublitz Akita before joining Vegalta Sendai.
  • MINAMI Sota (midfielder, Vegalta Sendai) — joined Vegalta Sendai's first team straight from the school in 2025.
  • ABE Ryota (defender, Ehime FC) — went to Meiji University, played for FC Imabari, and moved to Ehime FC in 2026.
  • MURATA Koichi (defender, Zweigen Kanazawa) — went to Meiji University and then Mito HollyHock, and joined Zweigen Kanazawa for the 2026 season.
  • YAMADA Takafumi (midfielder, FC Imabari) — went to Osaka University of Health and Sport Sciences, played for Honda Lock in the Japan Football League, and then joined FC Imabari.
