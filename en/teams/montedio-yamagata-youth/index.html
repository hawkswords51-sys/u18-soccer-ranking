Montedio Yamagata Youth

Montedio Yamagata Youth is the under-18 team of Montedio Yamagata, the professional club of Yamagata Prefecture in the Tohoku region of north-eastern Japan. It is the only team from Yamagata in the Prince League, the regional tier of Japan's national U-18 league pyramid; every other side in the prefecture, including the strongest high schools, plays in the prefectural league below it. The team has been a fixture in Prince League Tohoku for more than a decade, finished second in 2019, and reached the national finals of the summer Club Youth Championship in both 2024 and 2025. Its best-known graduate is HANDA Riku, the Gamba Osaka defender, who played at the 2019 FIFA U-17 World Cup while still a Montedio Yamagata Youth player. In 2026 the team is fourth in Prince League Tohoku after 16 of its 18 rounds, with eight wins, two draws and six defeats (26 points).

Playing style

The academy describes what it wants from its players in four plain words: tenacity, simplicity, family and challenge. "Tenacity" is the one the club returns to most often. Montedio presents itself as a team that fights with the stubbornness of the north-east, and the academy aims to produce players who carry that into the first team rather than stars who need a team built around them. The 2026 league season has followed that pattern. Through the first half of the season goals came from all over the team, defenders included, rather than from one leading scorer, and the side has been at its best when it keeps a clean sheet: each of its first four league wins came by two goals to nil.

The club's academy has not yet reached the Premier League, the national top tier. In 2019 its second place in Prince League Tohoku, behind Aomori Yamada's second team, earned it a place in the promotion play-offs, but it stayed in the Prince League.

For readers new to Japanese youth football: club academies such as this one do not enter the two big school tournaments, the summer Inter-High and the winter All Japan High School Championship, which are for high-school football clubs only. Their national competitions are the Club Youth Championship, played in the summer between the under-18 teams of professional and amateur clubs, and the J Youth Cup, a knockout competition for J.League academies played in the second half of the year. Both academies and school teams meet in the league pyramid run by the Japan Football Association: the national Premier League at the top, nine regional Prince Leagues below it, and prefectural leagues below those.

History

  • 1984 — The club begins as the football team of NEC Yamagata, a company side.
  • 1996 — It takes the name Montedio, from the Italian *monte* (mountain) and *dio* (god).
  • 1999 — Montedio Yamagata joins the J.League as one of the founding members of J2. The youth team becomes the club's route for local players into professional football.
  • 2019 — The youth team finishes second in Prince League Tohoku, its best league finish. In the same autumn its defender HANDA Riku plays for Japan at the FIFA U-17 World Cup in Brazil, and he goes on to join the first team.
  • 2024 — The team reaches the national finals of the Club Youth Championship. It wins one, draws one and loses one, and does not reach the knockout rounds.
  • 2025 — The national finals again. In July the youth team's new dormitory opens in Tendo, close to the club's stadium: 29 rooms, every one of them single.
  • 2026 — The academy's running is moved into a new organisation, Montedio Yamagata Sports Club, from 1 February, bringing it under the same roof as the first team. In June the team goes out of the J Youth Cup in the second round, drawing 1-1 away to Albirex Niigata U-18 and losing the shoot-out 12-11. In August two current youth players, SATO Rikuto and HOMMA Kaito, are registered with the first team.

Development pathway

  • A whole-prefecture academy. Yamagata is a large, mountainous prefecture split between the inland Murayama basin, where the club is based, and the Shonai plain on the Sea of Japan coast. The club runs two junior-youth (under-15) teams, one in each area, so that boys from the coast do not have to move to train with the club. In the 2026 squad, eighteen of the thirty-five registered players came up through one of those two teams.
  • Players from outside Yamagata as well. The rest come from elsewhere in Tohoku and further afield: Sendai and Akita, Saitama, Chiba, Kanagawa and even Wakayama in western Japan. Several of the club's graduates followed the same path. CHIBA Toraji came from a junior club in Miyagi Prefecture, and KANO Kaisei from a junior-youth team in Miyagi.
  • School alongside football. As at every Japanese academy, the players are full-time high-school students; many of the squad attend the same private high school in Yamagata city. That school has its own football club, which plays in the prefectural league and is a separate team from the academy. Japanese school years run from April to March, so a "third-year" player is in his final year of high school.
  • A home for players from far away. The dormitory opened in 2025 was built for exactly this mix: it lets the club take boys from other prefectures and from the far side of Yamagata and house them a short walk from a station and about a kilometre from the stadium.
  • Second-type registration. Japanese rules allow a club to register academy players with its professional squad while they remain youth players, so they can play in J.League matches. Montedio uses this regularly. In 2026 both SATO and HOMMA were given first-team numbers.
  • Promotion, or university first. Some graduates go straight into the first team; others go to university and return as professionals. TAKAHASHI Junya, SOMA Jo and KANO Kaisei all went to university first and then signed for Montedio.

Former players

  • HANDA Riku (defender, Gamba Osaka) — came from the club's Murayama junior-youth team into the youth team, played at the 2019 FIFA U-17 World Cup as a Montedio Yamagata Youth player, and then joined Montedio's first team. He moved to Gamba Osaka for the 2023 season and wears number 3 there. With Japan's under-23s he was in the squad for the 2024 AFC U23 Asian Cup and was named for the Paris Olympics, but was ruled out through injury shortly before the tournament.
  • TAKAHASHI Junya (forward, Montedio Yamagata) — went from the youth team to Komazawa University, and then signed for Montedio. After loan spells at Azul Claro Numazu and Fukushima United he is back in Yamagata, where he wears number 9 as one of the club's homegrown players.
  • KAMBAYASHI Taisei (goalkeeper, Montedio Yamagata) — from the club's Shonai junior-youth team, through the youth team and into the first team, where he wears number 32.
  • CHIBA Toraji (defender, Fukushima United) — promoted from the youth team to Montedio's first team; on a development loan at Fukushima United in J3, extended in June 2026 until the end of June 2027.
  • KANO Kaisei (midfielder, Fukushima United) — from the youth team to Kanto Gakuin University and then to Montedio. Also on loan at Fukushima United, extended to June 2027.
  • SOMA Jo (defender, Thespa Gunma) — from the Murayama junior-youth team through the youth team and Sendai University to Montedio, with a loan spell at Kagoshima United. He joined Thespa Gunma on a permanent transfer in June 2026.
