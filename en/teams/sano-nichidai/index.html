Sano Nihon University Senior High School

Sano Nihon University Senior High School (佐野日本大学高校, usually shortened to Sano Nichidai, 佐野日大) is a private school in Sano, a city in the south-west of Tochigi Prefecture, north of Tokyo. The school opened in 1964. Its football club has qualified ten times for the winter All Japan High School Soccer Tournament, counting the 2026–27 edition, and ten times for the summer Inter-High. Its best result is third place at the winter championship in 2016–17, the first time the school reached the national semi-finals. It also reached the quarter-finals in 2022–23. In 2025 the team won Tochigi's top prefectural league and reached the final round of the play-offs for the Prince League Kanto. In 2026 it plays in the Tochigi prefectural league. On 14 November 2026 it beat Utsunomiya Kogyo 2-0 in the final of the Tochigi winter qualifying tournament, qualifying for the national finals for the first time in four years.

Playing style

The club's traditional style is solid defending and quick counter-attacks, and its best national runs have been built on narrow wins. In 2016–17 all three of its wins in normal time were by a single goal, and the fourth tie was settled on penalties. At the 2025 Inter-High, the team won the Tochigi final 1-0 and its first national match 1-0 as well. Match reports from 2025 note that it has also been working on keeping and moving the ball, rather than relying only on the counter-attack.

The head coach is a former player of the school. He captained Juntendo University, played for Japan at under-16 and under-18 level, and holds the JFA's A coaching licence. The staff also includes three goalkeeping coaches, a team doctor and a judo therapist (a licensed sports-injury practitioner in Japan).

History

  • 1964 — The school opens in Sano, Tochigi Prefecture.
  • 2016–17 — The school's best run at the winter championship. Sano Nichidai beats Wakayama Kita 1-0 and Yonago Kita High School 1-0, draws 2-2 with Ichijo and wins the shoot-out 4-3, then beats Komazawa University High School 2-1 in the quarter-final. It loses 0-1 to Maebashi Ikuei High School in the semi-final, which gives the school third place.
  • 2020 — A new artificial-turf pitch is completed on the school grounds and opened in September.
  • 2022–23 — Back at the winter championship for the first time in six years. Entering in the second round, Sano Nichidai beats Nara Ikuei 1-0 and draws 1-1 with Riseisha High School before winning the shoot-out 5-4. It loses 0-4 to Okayama Gakugeikan High School in the quarter-finals.
  • 2025 — In March, 26 third-year players go on the club's first tour of Germany. Based around Mainz, they play youth teams from local clubs and watch the Bundesliga match between Eintracht Frankfurt and Union Berlin. In June the team beats Moka 1-0 in the Tochigi final to reach the Inter-High for the first time since 2015. At the national finals it beats Nissho Gakuen High School 1-0, then loses 0-1 to Toko Gakuen High School in the second round. Sano Nichidai also wins the Tochigi prefectural league. In the Prince League Kanto Division 2 play-offs in December, it beats Senshu University Matsudo 1-0, then loses 1-2 after extra time to Nihon University Fujisawa Senior High School and misses promotion.
  • 2026 — The team plays in the Tochigi prefectural league again. In the Inter-High qualifiers it loses 1-2 to Yaita Chuo High School in the semi-finals. In the winter qualifiers it enters in the fourth round and beats Yaita Higashi 5-1. It then beats Utsunomiya Tandai Fuzoku 2-1 after extra time in the quarter-finals and Moka 3-1 in the semi-finals. In the final on 14 November it beats Utsunomiya Kogyo 2-0, with both goals in the first half. The national finals start on 28 December 2026.

Development pathway

For readers new to Japanese youth football: high-school teams and J.League academies play in the same national league pyramid. At the top is the U-18 Premier League (EAST and WEST), then nine regional Prince Leagues, then the prefectural leagues. Two high-school tournaments carry even more prestige than the league: the summer Inter-High and the winter championship, which is televised nationally and ends at the National Stadium in Tokyo in January. Each prefecture sends one or two teams to each tournament through its own qualifying competition. Japanese seasons follow the school year, so a winter championship such as "2016–17" starts in late December and finishes in January.

  • A big squad with room for everyone. The club lists 149 players across its three school years in 2026. First-year players get competitive matches in the Kanto Rookie League, an under-16 league for the Kanto region.
  • Players from across the Kanto region. The squad includes players from junior clubs in Tochigi and from across the wider Kanto region, including Ibaraki, Saitama, Chiba, Tokyo and Kanagawa. Some also come from as far as Shizuoka.
  • Its own junior club. The Sano Football Academy is a junior club in Sano run by former players of the school. Some of its players go on to the high-school team.
  • Through university to the professional game. The best-known graduates of recent years turned professional after university rather than straight from school. NOZAWA Riku went via SANNO University, UTSUGI Shun via Kwansei Gakuin University and MUKAI Shunki via Niigata University of Health and Welfare.

Former players

  • NOZAWA Riku (defender, Ventforet Kofu) — at the school from 2014 to 2016, when he led the attack as the team's centre-forward in the run to third place at the 2016–17 winter championship. He moved to defence later in his career. After SANNO University he joined Ventforet Kofu in 2021, spent 2024 and 2025 on loan at FC Gifu, and returned to Kofu in 2026.
  • UTSUGI Shun (midfielder, Kamatamare Sanuki) — at the school from 2012 to 2014. He went on to Kwansei Gakuin University, then played for Nara Club and Arterivo Wakayama before joining Tochigi City in 2023. In June 2026 he moved to Kamatamare Sanuki on loan from Tochigi City until 30 June 2027.
  • MUKAI Shunki (midfielder, Niigata University of Health and Welfare) — will join Yokohama FC in 2027 and is registered with the club as a special designated player in 2026, which allows a university student to play J.League matches.

Several other graduates have played professionally, including a former Japan under-18 and under-21 international who now coaches at the school. Their names are not romanised here because no English spelling has been published by the JFA, the J.League or a club. No Sano Nichidai graduate was in Japan's squad for the 2026 World Cup.
