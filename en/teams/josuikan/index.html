Josuikan High School

Josuikan High School (如水館高校) is a private school in Mihara, a port city in the east of Hiroshima Prefecture on the Seto Inland Sea. It opened in 1940 as Mihara Technical School and has had its present name since 1994; the football club was founded in 1989, when the school was still Mihara Technical High School. The school is best known nationally for its baseball team. On 8 November 2026 the football team beat Hiroshima Minami 3-0 in the final of the Hiroshima qualifying tournament and will play at the winter All Japan High School Soccer Tournament in 2026–27. It will be the club's first national tournament of any kind, summer or winter. According to Gekisaka, it is only the second time a school from outside the city of Hiroshima has represented the prefecture at the winter championship, and the first since Fukuyama Iyo in 1993–94, 33 years earlier. In 2026 the team plays in Division 1 of Hiroshima's prefectural U-18 league.

Playing style

The club's motto is "solid in defence, many ways to attack", and in 2026 the team calls its approach "entertainment football": an attacking game that mixes individual skill, passing and fluid positioning. The head coach is a former Josuikan player who returned to coach at the school; he was an assistant in 2023 and leads the team in 2026, with the previous head coach staying on the staff.

The 2026 team entered the winter qualifiers in the knockout stage, as one of the four semi-finalists from the summer Inter-High qualifiers. It won its four matches 3-0, 5-1, 4-0 and 3-0. The 4-0 semi-final win was against Numata, who had knocked Josuikan out of the Inter-High qualifiers in June. In the final at Edion Peace Wing Hiroshima the score was 0-0 at half-time. Josuikan took the lead 21 minutes into the second half, when a curling right-footed cross from its third-year left back went over the advancing goalkeeper and straight in. It was the club's first goal in any Hiroshima final. The team then scored a second when a cross was not cleared and a third from a free-kick rebound, finished by the captain. It was Josuikan's fourth Hiroshima final against Hiroshima Minami, after defeats in 2014, 2019 and 2020. After the match the head coach said that the title was "only a stepping stone" and that the team would try to play "entertainment football" at the national championship as well.

History

  • 1989 — The football club is founded at Mihara Technical High School, which becomes Josuikan High School in 1994.
  • 2003 — The team reaches its first Hiroshima final, in the Inter-High qualifiers, and loses 0-3 to Sanyo.
  • 2014 — It beats Hiroshima Kannon 1-0 to reach its first winter qualifying final and loses 0-3 to Hiroshima Minami.
  • 2015–2016 — In December 2015 Josuikan beats Iwami Chisuikan 5-1 and Sanyo 1-0 in the regional play-off and is promoted to the Prince League Chugoku. In its only season there it beats Hiroshima Minami 3-2 with an 87th-minute winner, but finishes ninth, with four wins, one draw and 13 defeats, and returns to the prefectural league.
  • 2017–2018 — Semi-finals of the 2017 winter qualifiers and the 2018 Inter-High qualifiers.
  • 2019–2020 — Two more finals, both against Hiroshima Minami: 0-4 in the 2019 Inter-High qualifiers and 0-3 in the 2020 winter qualifiers.
  • 2021–2025 — Quarter-finals of the 2021 winter qualifiers and semi-finals of the Inter-High qualifiers in 2023 and 2024. In December 2025 the team loses 1-2 to Renofa Yamaguchi U-18 in the first round of the Prince League Chugoku play-off.
  • 2026 — Semi-final defeats in the new-season tournament (0-1 to Setouchi) and the Inter-High qualifiers (1-2 to Numata), then the first Hiroshima title, at the fifth attempt in a final.

Development pathway

For readers new to Japanese youth football: high-school teams and J.League academies play in the same national league pyramid. At the top are the U-18 Premier League (EAST and WEST), then nine regional Prince Leagues, then the prefectural leagues. Josuikan plays in Division 1 of Hiroshima's prefectural U-18 league, one level below the Prince League Chugoku. Two high-school tournaments carry even more prestige than the league: the summer Inter-High and the winter championship, which is televised nationally and ends at the National Stadium in January.

  • A large squad. The club had 142 players in 2026: 30 in the third year, 40 in the second and 72 in the first.
  • Boarding and training. The club has two boarding houses for about 20 and 10 players. The team trains from Tuesday to Friday, with a different theme each day (fitness and technique, individual tactics, group tactics, team tactics), rests on Mondays and plays matches at weekends. Its facilities include a clay pitch with floodlights, an off-site ground, a clubhouse and a gym.
  • The east of the prefecture. Hiroshima's winter qualifiers have long been dominated by schools from the city of Hiroshima and its surroundings. Josuikan, from Mihara in the east, had lost all four of its previous prefectural finals before 2026.

Former players

Two former players are known to have played professionally, both for Avispa Fukuoka and both now retired: a defender who joined Avispa straight from school in 2000 and later played for V-Varen Nagasaki, and a defender who went on to Fukuoka University and joined Avispa in 2010. Their names are not romanised here because no English spelling has been published by the JFA, the J.League or a club. No Josuikan graduate was in Japan's squad for the 2026 World Cup.
