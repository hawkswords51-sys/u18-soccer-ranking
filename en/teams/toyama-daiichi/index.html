Toyama Daiichi High School

Toyama Daiichi High School (富山第一, known locally as "Tomiichi") is a private school in the city of Toyama, on the Sea of Japan coast. The school opened in 1959. It is the most successful team in Toyama Prefecture and one of the strongest in the Hokushinetsu region, the snowy north-west of Honshu. Its greatest moment came in January 2014, when it won the winter All Japan High School Soccer Tournament for the first time. It came back from 0-2 down against Seiryo High School in the final, the last ever played at the old National Stadium in Tokyo. In 2019 it was runner-up at the summer Inter-High. Its alumni include YANAGISAWA Atsushi, who played for Japan at the 2002 and 2006 World Cups. In 2026 the team plays in Prince League Hokushinetsu Division 1, after winning promotion the year before. It reached the round of 16 at the Inter-High, its 33rd appearance in the tournament.

Playing style

Toyama Daiichi has never had a dormitory for its football players. The school chose to keep players living at home with their families, so almost the whole squad comes from Toyama and the neighbouring area. Of the 25 players registered for the 2013–14 winter championship, 23 were from Toyama Prefecture. Many arrive from local junior clubs such as Kataller Toyama's under-15 team, SQUARE Toyama FC and FC Higashi. That is unusual for a national champion: many leading private schools recruit from all over Japan and house their players in dormitories. The club's own description puts schoolwork and family life first. It says players can only commit fully to football when home life, school life and study are in order. The club also runs a training camp in Italy, where the team plays AC Perugia and other sides. The 2026 team is hard to beat and difficult to score against. At the Inter-High it kept clean sheets in its first two matches. Both went to penalties, and its second-year goalkeeper saved two kicks in each shoot-out.

History

  • 1959–60 — The school opens in Toyama in 1959. The football club is founded the following year.
  • 1999–2000 — Toyama Daiichi reaches the semi-finals of the 78th winter championship. It is the first team from Toyama Prefecture to get that far.
  • 2000–01 — A second winter semi-final in a row, in the 79th edition. No team from the Hokuriku region (Toyama, Ishikawa and Fukui) had reached the semi-finals, played at the National Stadium, two years running.
  • 2011 — The U-18 Premier League begins as Japan's national top flight for youth teams. Toyama Daiichi is one of the founding members of the WEST division.
  • 2012 — A new head coach takes over. He had trained as a coach in Europe and worked abroad, including as head coach of Albirex Niigata Singapore.
  • 2013–14 — The 92nd winter championship. The final against Seiryo High School, from neighbouring Ishikawa, is the first all-Hokuriku final and the last match of the tournament at the old National Stadium before it was rebuilt. Seiryo leads 2-0 with 20 minutes left. Toyama Daiichi scores from a counter-attack in the 87th minute, equalises with a penalty in stoppage time, and wins it 3-2 in the 109th minute with a first-time shot after a throw-in. Two of the three goals come from substitutes.
  • 2014 — After four seasons in the Premier League WEST, Toyama Daiichi is relegated to the Prince League Hokushinetsu.
  • 2018 — One more season in the Premier League, this time in the EAST division, which ends in relegation.
  • 2019 — Runners-up at the Inter-High, held in Okinawa. The final against Toko Gakuen High School is goalless until the eighth minute of stoppage time, when Toko scores the only goal with the last kick of the match.
  • 2022 — The head coach of the 2014 title team leaves to take charge of the Mongolia national team. A former Toyama Daiichi player succeeds him.
  • 2025 — Toyama Daiichi plays in Prince League Hokushinetsu Division 2, wins the title and is promoted back to Division 1. At the 104th winter championship it beats Saga Higashi 3-0. It then goes out 1-2 in the third round to Ohzu High School, who score the winner in the sixth minute of stoppage time.
  • 2026 — The team wins the Toyama qualifying tournament for the fifth year in a row, beating Takaoka Daiichi 2-0 in the final. At the Inter-High it draws 0-0 with Teikyo Kani and beats them 4-3 on penalties, then draws 0-0 with Ichiritsu Funabashi and wins 5-4 on penalties. It goes out in the third round, the round of 16, losing 0-1 to Nissho Gakuen High School. In the league, the newly promoted side is in the top three of Division 1 with three rounds left, level on points with second-placed Albirex Niigata U-18.

Development pathway

For readers new to Japanese youth football: high-school teams and J.League academies play in the same national league pyramid. At the top are the U-18 Premier League (EAST and WEST), then nine regional Prince Leagues, then the prefectural leagues. Two high-school tournaments carry even more prestige than the league: the summer Inter-High and the winter championship, which is televised nationally and ends at the National Stadium in January.

  • A home-grown champion. With no football dormitory, Toyama Daiichi depends on players from its own region. The 2014 title showed that a team built almost entirely from one prefecture with a population of about one million could win the national championship.
  • Close ties with local clubs. Toyama has one professional club, Kataller Toyama, which plays in J2. Many Toyama Daiichi players come through local junior clubs, Kataller Toyama's under-15 team among them. Former Toyama Daiichi players have later coached and managed at Kataller.
  • Rivals in the same league. In Prince League Hokushinetsu Division 1 the school plays professional academies, including Albirex Niigata U-18, Matsumoto Yamaga FC U-18 and Kataller Toyama U-18. It also plays leading school teams: its 2014 final opponents Seiryo High School, plus Nihon Bunri and the second team of Teikyo Nagaoka. Only the league winner earns a play-off place for the Premier League, so the margins at the top are small.
  • Football through the snow. Winters on the Sea of Japan coast are long and snowy. Like other Hokushinetsu schools, Toyama Daiichi has to keep training through months when grass pitches are out of use.
  • Producing players at every level. Toyama Daiichi players have gone straight to J.League clubs (YANAGISAWA Atsushi, NAKASHIMA Yuki, NISHIMURA Takuma, TSUBOI Kiyoshiro) and via university (KOMORI Hiiro). Others have gone into coaching (OTAGIRI Michiharu).

Former players

  • YANAGISAWA Atsushi (forward, retired; Kashima Antlers coach) — the most famous player the school has produced. He joined Kashima Antlers from the school in 1996 and also played for Sampdoria and Messina in Italy, Kyoto Sanga and Vegalta Sendai. He won 58 caps for Japan, scoring 17 goals, and played at the 2002 and 2006 World Cups. He retired in 2014 and is a coach on Kashima's first-team staff in 2026–27.
  • NAKASHIMA Yuki (forward, Kataller Toyama) — a Toyama native who joined Kashima Antlers from the school and went on to play for Vegalta Sendai, Montedio Yamagata and FC Machida Zelvia. He spent ten years at Machida. In 2026, at the age of 41, he moved home to Kataller Toyama on a permanent transfer.
  • NISHIMURA Takuma (forward, FC Machida Zelvia) — grew up in Aichi Prefecture and came to Toyama Daiichi for high school. He joined Vegalta Sendai in 2015 and played for CSKA Moscow, Portimonense and Servette in Europe. He won the 2022 J1 title with Yokohama F. Marinos and received a J1 Outstanding Player award that season. He has won five caps for Japan and scored twice on his debut, against Hong Kong at the 2022 EAFF E-1 Championship. He joined Machida in 2025.
  • KOMORI Hiiro (forward, Urawa Reds) — from Toyama. He went from the school to Niigata University of Health and Welfare and then to JEF United Chiba, where he became one of J2's leading scorers. After a loan spell at Sint-Truiden in Belgium in 2025, he joined Urawa Reds on a permanent transfer that summer.
  • TSUBOI Kiyoshiro (forward, Kataller Toyama) — came through Kataller Toyama's under-15 team and Toyama Daiichi, then turned professional with Tokushima Vortis. He has also played for Albirex Niigata Singapore, Kochi United and Blaublitz Akita. He joined Kataller on loan from Tokushima for the first half of 2026, and the loan has been extended to June 2027.
  • OTAGIRI Michiharu (manager, Renofa Yamaguchi FC) — played for Kyoto Purple Sanga, Ventforet Kofu and Kataller Toyama after leaving the school. He then spent more than a decade coaching at Kataller, from the under-15s up to first-team manager. He took over Nara Club in mid-2025, became Renofa Yamaguchi's manager for 2026, and was kept on for the 2026–27 season.
