Cerezo Osaka U-18

Cerezo Osaka U-18 is the under-18 team of Cerezo Osaka, the J1 club from the south of Osaka. It is one of the most productive academies in Japan: two of its graduates, MINAMINO Takumi and SEKO Ayumu, have played at World Cups for Japan and a third, YAMAGUCHI Hotaru, played at the 2014 and 2018 World Cups. As a team it has been national champion once, winning the Premier League Championship in 2014, and has won the Japan Club Youth (U-18) Championship three times, most recently in 2022. The last few seasons have been harder. The team dropped out of the Premier League after 2022 and fell a further level in 2024 before climbing back. In 2026 it is fighting to return to the top flight: after 14 of 18 rounds it leads Prince League Kansai Division 1 with 31 points, and its no. 10, SHIOJIRI Teppei, is the league's top scorer.

Currently 1st in the Prince League Kansai Division 1 with 34 points from 15 matches (11W 1D 3L, 47-21). Updated daily.

Playing style

"Cerezo" is Spanish for cherry blossom, and the academy has long been associated with technical, attacking footballers rather than physical ones — a reputation built on players such as MINAMINO and KITANO Sota. The club describes its aim as developing professionals to a "world standard", capable of competing internationally and of embodying its "Sakura Spectacle" motto. Under head coach MARUYAMA Yoshiaki, the 2026 team has played on the front foot: it has scored 44 goals in 14 league matches, including 5-2, 6-1 and 5-0 wins, and match reports this autumn describe a side that tries to win by dominating the centre of the pitch rather than retreating into it.

How the competitions fit together: Japan's under-18 football mixes school teams and club academies in one league pyramid. At the top is the national Premier League, split into EAST and WEST; until 2019 the two winners met in a one-off final, the Premier League Championship. Below it sit nine regional Prince Leagues — Cerezo play in Prince League Kansai — and below those, prefectural leagues. J.League academies do not enter the school competitions (the summer Inter-High and the winter championship); their own national tournaments are the summer Japan Club Youth (U-18) Championship and the J.League Youth Cup, a knockout competition for J.League academies.

The 2014 national title was the club's first and so far only one, won at Saitama Stadium. The three Club Youth titles came 13 years apart: against Yokohama Marinos Youth in the 1996 final, FC Tokyo U-18 in 2009 and Yokohama F. Marinos Youth in 2022. The J.League Youth Cup has never been won; Cerezo lost the final to Gamba Osaka Youth in 2008 and to Nagoya Grampus U18 in 2011.

History

  • 1993 — Cerezo Osaka is founded from the works team of Yanmar Diesel, one of the most successful clubs of Japan's amateur era. The youth team reaches the Club Youth final as early as 1994.
  • 1996 — A first Japan Club Youth (U-18) Championship.
  • 2008–2011 — A strong generation: J.League Youth Cup finals in 2008 and 2011, and a second Club Youth title in 2009. YAMAGUCHI Hotaru and OHGIHARA Takahiro go up to the first team in this period, and MINAMINO Takumi is selected for Japan at the 2011 Under-17 World Cup as a Cerezo U-18 player.
  • 2011 — The Premier League is founded and Cerezo are founding members of the WEST, finishing 3rd, 4th and 4th in the first three seasons.
  • 2014 — WEST champions, then national champions with a 1-0 win over Kashiwa Reysol U-18 in the final.
  • 2016 — Runners-up in the WEST. From 2016 to 2020 the club also enters a Cerezo Osaka U-23 side in J3, the third tier of professional football, giving academy players senior matches.
  • 2018 — SEKO Ayumu, a central defender, signs a professional contract in October while still at the under-18s.
  • 2020 — With the national leagues cancelled because of the pandemic, the team wins the replacement Super Prince League Kansai.
  • 2022 — A third Club Youth title, but a 10th-place finish in the WEST means relegation to Prince League Kansai Division 1.
  • 2023–2024 — Eighth in Kansai Division 1 brings a second relegation; the team finishes second in Division 2 in 2024 and goes straight back up.
  • 2025 — Seventh on return to Kansai Division 1.
  • 2026 — Ten wins, one draw and three defeats from the first 14 matches put the team top of the table with four rounds left, chasing a place in December's Premier League promotion play-off.

Development pathway

  • A pathway from the age of six. The club runs an under-12 team and three under-15 teams (Cerezo Osaka U-15, Cerezo Osaka Nishi U-15 and Cerezo Osaka Wakayama U-15), and the under-18 squad is largely drawn from them. SHIOJIRI and EZEMUOKWE Chimezie Kai came up from the Nishi U-15, NAGASOE Kouki from the U-15.
  • Second-type registration. The best youth players are registered with the first team while still at the academy — Japan's "second-type" registration, which lets an under-18 player appear in J.League matches. Three current under-18s hold first-team numbers in the 2026 J1 squad: SHIOJIRI (42), EZEMUOKWE (43) and NAGASOE (45).
  • Promotion and loans. Graduates who are not yet ready for J1 are often loaned to J2 and J3 clubs. FUJIO Shota, for example, went up from the under-18s and had spells at Mito HollyHock, Tokushima Vortis and FC Machida Zelvia before establishing himself. The most recent promotion is the goalkeeper ISIBOR Ken, who moved up from the under-18s and is now in the 2026-27 first-team squad.
  • Europe as the destination. The academy's best graduates now tend to move abroad: MINAMINO via Salzburg and Liverpool to Monaco, SEKO to Switzerland and then France, KITANO to Austria.

Former players

  • MINAMINO Takumi (forward/midfielder, AS Monaco) — came through the club's under-15 and under-18 teams, played at the 2011 Under-17 World Cup as a Cerezo U-18 player, and went on to RB Salzburg, Liverpool and Monaco and a long career with Japan. He returned to Monaco's matchday squad in September 2026 after a knee ligament injury.
  • SEKO Ayumu (defender, Le Havre AC) — turned professional from the under-18s in 2018, moved to Grasshoppers in Switzerland and then to Le Havre in France, and wore no. 20 for Japan at the 2026 World Cup.
  • YAMAGUCHI Hotaru (midfielder, V-Varen Nagasaki) — from the club's under-15 and under-18 teams to the first team; later Hannover 96 in Germany and Vissel Kobe, and a Japan regular at the 2014 and 2018 World Cups.
  • OHGIHARA Takahiro (midfielder, Vissel Kobe) — a product of the same under-15 and under-18 route; later Nagoya Grampus and Yokohama F. Marinos, and a Japan international.
  • FUJIO Shota (forward, Kashiwa Reysol) — went up from the under-18s; joined Kashiwa from FC Machida Zelvia on a permanent transfer in August 2026.
  • KITANO Sota (forward) — under-15, under-18 and second-type registered player before breaking into the first team; played for Japan's under-20s at the 2023 AFC U20 Asian Cup and moved to RB Salzburg in Austria in June 2025.
  • ISIBOR Ken (goalkeeper, Cerezo Osaka) — the latest graduate, promoted from the under-18s and now in the first-team squad for 2026-27.

KAGAWA Shinji, who now plays for Cerezo's first team, is sometimes listed as an academy product; he joined the club as a professional from FC Miyagi Barcelona's youth team and did not play for Cerezo's under-18s.
