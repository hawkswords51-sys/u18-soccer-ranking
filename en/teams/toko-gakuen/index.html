Toko Gakuen High School

Toko Gakuen High School (桐光学園, usually just "Toko") is a private school in Asao Ward, Kawasaki, in Kanagawa Prefecture, with an attached junior high school. The football club was founded in April 1978, the year the high school opened. It has been one of the strongest school teams in the Kanto region since the 1990s. In 1996–97 it reached the final of the winter All Japan High School Soccer Tournament, led by NAKAMURA Shunsuke, and in 2019 it won the summer Inter-High for the first time. Its alumni include two members of Japan's 2026 World Cup party: forward OGAWA Koki and assistant coach NAKAMURA Shunsuke. In 2026 the team plays in Prince League Kanto Division 2 and reached the quarter-finals of the Inter-High.

2026 season

Currently 5th in the Prince League Kanto Division 2 with 20 points from 13 matches (5W 5D 3L, 17-14). Updated daily.

Playing style

The current head coach, himself a former Toko player, has led the team since 2013. He describes the aim of the programme as "growth rather than results": developing each player matters more than winning a particular match. The club's own description of its football is "football that is enjoyable to watch and enjoyable to play", built on careful construction through midfield and quick attacks. The 2026 Inter-High showed a team that is hard to beat. In four matches Toko scored five goals and conceded three, beating Naruto 1-0 and Senshu University Kitakami 2-1. It then knocked out Kamimura Gakuen, the reigning summer and winter champions, 5-4 on penalties after a 1-1 draw. Its run ended in the quarter-final against Takigawa Daini High School: another 1-1 draw, this time lost 1-4 on penalties.

History

  • 1978 — The high school opens in Kawasaki and the football club is founded in April of the same year.
  • 1986 — A new head coach arrives. Over the next quarter of a century he builds Toko into one of Kanagawa's leading teams.
  • 1993–94 — At the 72nd winter championship, Toko beats Yonago Higashi 2-0 and Tokushima Shiritsu 1-0 before going out on penalties in the third round.
  • 1996 — The club's best year so far. With NAKAMURA Shunsuke in midfield, Toko reaches the Inter-High semi-finals in the summer. In the winter championship it beats Hatsushiba Hashimoto, Matsusho Gakuen, Urawa Shiritsu and, on penalties, Shizuoka Gakuen High School, before losing the final 1-2 to Ichiritsu Funabashi. NAKAMURA joins Yokohama Marinos in 1997.
  • 2010 — A second Inter-High semi-final.
  • 2012–13 — Semi-finals of the 91st winter championship, at the National Stadium in Tokyo.
  • 2013 — The founding era ends when a former Toko captain, who had played in the J.League, takes over as head coach.
  • 2016 — Second in the Prince League Kanto, which earns a place in the play-off for the national Premier League. Toko does not win promotion.
  • 2018 — Runners-up at the summer Inter-High. In the league, Toko finishes ninth in the Prince League Kanto and drops to the Kanagawa prefectural league.
  • 2019 — Champions of the Inter-High, held in Okinawa. NISHIKAWA Jun, who joined Cerezo Osaka the following year, scores in both the quarter-final and the semi-final. The winning goal against Toyama Daiichi comes with the last kick of the final.
  • 2022 — Toko wins back a regional league place through the year-end play-off, beating Yachiyo on penalties for the last spot in the Prince League Kanto Division 2.
  • 2023 — Third in Prince League Kanto Division 2, and runners-up again at the Inter-High, held in Hokkaido, after a 2-2 draw and a 6-7 penalty shoot-out against Meishu Hitachi.
  • 2026 — The club qualifies for its fourth Inter-High in a row and its 18th in all, and reaches the quarter-finals. In August, defender HAGIHARA Kei becomes the first of the year group to sign with a professional club.

Development pathway

For readers new to Japanese youth football: high-school teams and J.League academies play in the same national league pyramid. At the top are the U-18 Premier League (EAST and WEST), then nine regional Prince Leagues, then the prefectural leagues. Two high-school tournaments carry even more prestige than the league: the summer Inter-High and the winter championship, which is televised nationally and ends at the National Stadium in January.

  • A school side in a J.League region. Kanagawa is home to the academies of Yokohama F. Marinos, Kawasaki Frontale, Yokohama FC and Shonan Bellmare, and to strong school rivals such as Toin Gakuen High School. Toko recruits from both school and club backgrounds: the club says its squads are roughly half players from local junior-high school teams and half players from junior club teams.
  • A second chance for academy players. Some of Toko's best-known players arrived after missing out on promotion within a J.League academy. NAKAMURA Shunsuke is the famous case: he was not promoted from Yokohama Marinos' junior-youth team, came to Toko, and was a professional at Marinos two years later.
  • Professional facilities on campus. The club has its own floodlit artificial-turf ground, which hosts official league matches, as well as a club house, a training room, a trainer's room and a team bus.
  • Junior-high links. The attached junior high school has its own football team, and some of its players move up to the high-school squad.
  • Producing players at every level. Toko players have gone directly to J.League clubs (OGAWA Koki, NISHIKAWA Jun, NARASAKA Takumi, SATO Rinya) and via university (ISSAKA Zain).

Former players

  • NAKAMURA Shunsuke (midfielder, retired; Japan assistant coach) — the star of the 1996–97 run to the winter final. He played for Yokohama F. Marinos, Reggina, Celtic, Espanyol, Júbilo Iwata and Yokohama FC, and won 98 caps for Japan, scoring 24 goals. He played at the 2006 World Cup and was in the 2010 squad. After retiring in 2022 he coached at Yokohama FC, and he was an assistant coach on Japan's staff at the 2026 World Cup.
  • OGAWA Koki (forward, FC Machida Zelvia) — joined Júbilo Iwata straight from the school in 2016, then played for Mito HollyHock on loan and for Yokohama FC before moving to NEC Nijmegen in the Netherlands in 2023. He wore number 19 for Japan at the 2026 World Cup, and in August 2026 he returned to the J.League with a permanent move to Machida.
  • NISHIKAWA Jun (midfielder, Fagiano Okayama) — the attacking star of the 2019 Inter-High-winning team and a Japan youth international. He joined Cerezo Osaka, had loan spells at Sagan Tosu and Iwaki FC, and moved to Okayama on a permanent transfer for 2026.
  • FUKUMORI Akito (defender, Hokkaido Consadole Sapporo) — went from the school to Kawasaki Frontale and moved to Sapporo in 2017. He is known for his left-footed set pieces. After two seasons on loan at Yokohama FC, where he was named in the 2024 J2 Best XI, he is back at Sapporo for 2026.
  • ISSAKA Zain (midfielder, JEF United Chiba) — went to Toin University of Yokohama after the school, then played for Kawasaki Frontale, Yokohama FC and Montedio Yamagata. He moved to Chiba in July 2025.
  • NARASAKA Takumi (defender, Giravanz Kitakyushu) — joined FC Machida Zelvia from the school and has had two loan spells at Kamatamare Sanuki. Since 2025–26 he has been on loan at Giravanz Kitakyushu, a deal extended to June 2027.
  • SATO Rinya (midfielder, Thespa Gunma) — a 2025 graduate who joined Thespa Gunma as a rookie for the 2026 season.

Players to watch in 2026

  • HAGIHARA Kei (defender, third year) — an attacking full-back who was capped by Japan at U-16 level in 2024 and was named one of the outstanding players of the 2026 Inter-High. On 28 August 2026 FC Imabari announced that he will join the club in January 2027. He has also been registered as a JFA/J.League special designated player, so he can play for Imabari while still at school.

The rest of the 2026 squad is not listed here. None of the other players has an English spelling published by the JFA, the J.League or a club, so their names are not romanised on this page.
