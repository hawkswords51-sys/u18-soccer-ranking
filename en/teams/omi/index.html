Omi High School

Omi High School (近江高校, the school itself uses the spelling "Ohmi") is a private school in Hikone, a castle town on the eastern shore of Lake Biwa in Shiga Prefecture. For decades Shiga high-school football was dominated by Yasu and Kusatsu Higashi, and Omi was not a name that national audiences knew. That changed after 2015, when a former professional forward took charge of a club with very few players and set about rebuilding it. Within a decade Omi had become Shiga's leading team. At the 2023–24 winter All Japan High School Soccer Tournament, only its third appearance, it reached the final, losing 1-3 to Aomori Yamada at the National Stadium. In 2026 the first team plays in Prince League Kansai Division 1, one level below the national Premier League, and the club also enters second and third teams in lower leagues. At the 2026 Inter-High it went out in the second round, beaten 1-2 by Toin Gakuen High School.

Playing style

The club's slogan, used across its kit, social media and school materials, is "Be Pirates": the idea that Omi should play as challengers who take risks together rather than as a comfortable favourite. On the pitch that has meant a possession-based, attack-minded game. At the 2023–24 winter championship Omi lined up with a back three in which the left-sided centre-back, the captain, started attacks by carrying and passing the ball forward. The head coach has spoken openly about working on his players' mental approach as much as their technique. Before that tournament he told them to stay relaxed in front of goal rather than snatching at chances, and in the second round against Nihon University Fujisawa he chose to push for a win at 1-1 rather than settle for penalties. Omi scored four goals against Kamimura Gakuen in the quarter-final and three against Horikoshi in the semi-final, which gives a fair picture of a team built to score rather than to protect a lead.

The current head coach has led the team since 2015. He played as a forward for Shimizu S-Pulse and abroad. He is not named on this page because no official English spelling of his name is published by the JFA, the J.League or the club.

History

  • 2015 — A former Shimizu S-Pulse forward becomes head coach of a club with only a handful of players and begins recruiting across the Kansai region.
  • 2017 — Omi qualifies for the summer Inter-High for the first time, ahead of Shiga's established powers.
  • 2020–21 — The club makes its first appearance at the winter championship.
  • 2022–23 — At its second winter championship, Omi reaches the second round, its best result to that point.
  • 2023 — Omi finishes second in Prince League Kansai Division 1. It plays Kagoshima Josei in the play-off for promotion to the U-18 Premier League but loses. Two of the club's early graduates, TAKEMURA Shunji and YAMAUCHI Shusei, become the first Omi old boys to sign for J.League clubs, both after university.
  • 2023–24 — The breakthrough. At the 102nd winter championship Omi beats Nihon University Fujisawa, semi-finalists at that summer's Inter-High, 4-3 on penalties after a 1-1 draw. It then knocks out the reigning summer champions Meishu Hitachi, again on penalties, to reach the last eight for the first time. The quarter-final against Kamimura Gakuen is one of the matches of the tournament: Omi wins 4-3 with a goal in the third minute of stoppage time. It beats Horikoshi 3-1 in the semi-final at the National Stadium before losing the final 1-3 to Aomori Yamada on 8 January 2024.
  • 2024 — Omi plays at the Inter-High for the fourth time.
  • 2026 — Omi wins the Shiga qualifiers and returns to the Inter-High after a year away, for its fifth appearance. Given a bye through the first round, it loses its only match 1-2 to Toin Gakuen High School on 26 July; Toin Gakuen went on to the quarter-finals. Defender FUJITA Junya, a 2026 graduate, joins FC Gifu directly from the school.

Development pathway

For readers new to Japanese youth football: high-school teams and J.League academies play in the same national league pyramid. At the top are the U-18 Premier League (EAST and WEST), then nine regional Prince Leagues, then the prefectural leagues. Two high-school tournaments carry even more prestige than the league: the summer Inter-High and the winter championship, which is televised nationally and ends at the National Stadium in January. Because the Japanese school year runs from April to March, a winter championship that starts in late December and finishes in January is described here as, for example, "2023–24".

  • A new force in an old football prefecture. Shiga has a strong football tradition: Yasu won the winter championship in 2005–06 with a famously technical team. Omi's rise is unusual because it happened so quickly, in less than ten years, and from a very low starting point.
  • Recruiting from all over western Japan. Omi does not rely on an attached junior-high team. Its squads are drawn from junior club teams across Shiga, Kyoto, Osaka, Hyogo and beyond, including the junior-youth sides of J.League clubs. The coaching staff look for players who have at least one clear strength.
  • Players who run the club. The club gives players responsibility for roles such as match analysis, medical support and media, and older players take part in preparing for opponents. The aim is to produce independent people as well as footballers.
  • A pathway to university and the professional game. Omi's first professionals went via university: TAKEMURA Shunji via Rissho University and YAMAUCHI Shusei via Kwansei Gakuin University. In 2026 FUJITA Junya went straight from school to FC Gifu. Several members of the 2023–24 runners-up team are now playing university football in the Kansai Student League.
  • Regional rivals. In Prince League Kansai Division 1 Omi plays against the academies of Cerezo Osaka and Kyoto Sanga as well as strong school sides from across Kansai, so its players face high-level opposition every week, not just at the two national tournaments.

Former players

  • FUJITA Junya (defender, FC Gifu) — a centre-back from Shiga who joined J3 club FC Gifu directly from Omi for the 2026 season. He has started matches for Gifu in the J.LEAGUE YBC Levain Cup in his first season.
  • TAKEMURA Shunji (midfielder, FC Nobeoka AGATA) — from the first year group recruited after the 2015 rebuild. He went on to Rissho University and joined Kamatamare Sanuki in J3 in 2023, making his J.League debut in March that year. He was loaned to FC Nobeoka AGATA in Miyazaki, and after his contract with Sanuki ended he is now listed in Nobeoka's squad.
  • YAMAUCHI Shusei (defender, SHIBUYA CITY FC) — also one of the first recruits of the rebuilt club. After Kwansei Gakuin University he joined FC Ryukyu in 2023, moved to Iwate Grulla Morioka on loan in August 2024 and on a permanent deal at the end of that year, and joined Tokyo club SHIBUYA CITY FC in January 2026.
