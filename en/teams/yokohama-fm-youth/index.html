Yokohama F. Marinos Youth

Yokohama F. Marinos Youth is the under-18 team of Yokohama F. Marinos, one of the original J.League clubs, and one of the most decorated club academies in Japan. It has won the Japan Club Youth (U-18) Championship six times, the old national youth championship once — 7-1 against Jubilo Iwata Youth in the 2009 final — and the J Youth Cup twice. In 2026 it is playing a level below where it usually expects to be, in Prince League Kanto Division 2 after relegation from Division 1 in 2025, and it is top of the table, unbeaten after 11 matches (eight wins, three draws, 27 points). The team is coached by TOGASHI Koichi, who led Japan at the 2023 FIFA U-20 World Cup, and its squad includes MIIDERA Shin, who in April 2026 became the youngest player ever to sign a professional contract with the club.

2026 season

Currently 1st in the Prince League Kanto Division 2 with 28 points from 12 matches (8W 4D 0L, 27-6). Updated daily.

Playing style

The whole Marinos academy plays the same way as the first team, under the label the club itself uses: "Attacking Football". The club's own description is that the attack starts in its own half, moves the ball to build up territorial control in the opponent's half, and creates as many actions as possible to score. In practice that means a possession-based team that plays out from the back, pushes its full-backs high and wants the ball in the final third. Most of the squad have learned this since primary-school age: of the players listed on the club's 2026 youth roster, nearly all came up from the club's own junior-youth teams in Yokohama and Oppama (Yokosuka). TOGASHI Koichi took over the team in 2024, after his spell in charge of Japan's under-20s, and the team trains at the football ground of Yokohama National University.

Some context for readers outside Japan. The top of the under-18 pyramid is the national Premier League, split into EAST and WEST; below it sit nine regional Prince Leagues (Kanto has two divisions), and below those the prefectural leagues. School teams and club academies play in the same leagues. Club academies such as this one do not enter the two school tournaments — the summer Inter-High and the winter High School Championship — so their own national cups are the Club Youth Championship, traditionally played in summer, and the J Youth Cup for J.League academies. The All Japan Youth Championship, which the team won in 2009, was the national competition for schools and clubs together before the Premier League replaced it in 2011.

History

  • 1986 — The club, then the Nissan Motor company team, sets up its youth development organisation, before the J.League exists.
  • 1987 — A first Japan Club Youth Championship.
  • 1994–1995 — Two Club Youth titles in a row, as the club becomes Yokohama Marinos in the new J.League.
  • 2000 — A fourth Club Youth title.
  • 2005–2006 — Two Prince League Kanto titles in a row.
  • 2009 — The All Japan Youth (U-18) Championship, won 7-1 against Jubilo Iwata Youth in the final on 12 October.
  • 2010 — A first J Youth Cup.
  • 2013, 2015 — Club Youth champions twice more. In 2015 the team also wins the Prince League Kanto and is promoted to the Premier League.
  • 2017 — Tenth in the Premier League EAST and relegated.
  • 2018 — The J Youth Cup again, 2-1 against Shimizu S-Pulse Youth in the final in Kitakyushu.
  • 2019 — Second in the Prince League Kanto and promoted back to the Premier League.
  • 2021–2023 — Third in the EAST in 2021 and second in 2022, but eleventh and relegated in 2023.
  • 2024 — TOGASHI Koichi becomes head coach. Second in Prince League Kanto Division 1.
  • 2025 — Eighth in Division 1, and relegated to Division 2.
  • 2026 — MIIDERA Shin signs a professional contract on his 16th birthday. The team leads Division 2 unbeaten after 11 matches. In the J Youth Cup it beat SC Sagamihara U-18 8-0, then went out in the second round to Shonan Bellmare U-18, the eventual runners-up.

Development pathway

  • One way of playing from age six to eighteen. The academy runs a primary (U-12) team, two junior-youth (U-15) teams in Yokohama and Oppama, and the youth team, all coached to the same Attacking Football model. Players who come through the whole pathway, such as the first-team captain KIDA Takuya, play the first team's game long before they reach it.
  • A short step to the first team. In the 2026 season three current youth players hold first-team numbers: MIIDERA Shin (47) on a professional contract, and FUJII Shota (54) and HIRANO Kanata (55) under the J.League's second-category registration, which lets an academy player appear in official first-team matches while still in the under-18 team. According to the club, in 2026 nine academy graduates are in the first-team squad and more than sixty are playing across the J.League.
  • The university route back. Not everyone turns professional at eighteen. AMANO Jun (Juntendo University), KIMURA Takuto (Meiji University) and SUWAMA Kosei (University of Tsukuba) all left the youth team for university and were later signed by Marinos.
  • The ones who went elsewhere. The club's most famous product never played for this team. NAKAMURA Shunsuke was in the Marinos junior-youth but was not taken into the youth team; he went to Toko Gakuen High School instead and joined Marinos from there in 1997. HAYAKAWA Tomoki, now a Japan international goalkeeper, also left after the junior-youth for Toin Gakuen High School and Meiji University. Neither is a youth-team graduate, but both show how much talent passes through the academy at younger ages.

Former players

All of the following played for the under-18 team itself (clubs as of late September 2026).

  • KIDA Takuya (midfielder, Yokohama F. Marinos) — came through the primary, junior-youth and youth teams, and is the first-team captain for the 2026-27 season.
  • YAMANE Riku (midfielder, Yokohama F. Marinos) — promoted from the youth team and named the New Hero (best young player) of the 2024 J.League YBC Levain Cup.
  • AMANO Jun (midfielder, Yokohama F. Marinos) — a youth-team graduate who went to Juntendo University before returning to the club.
  • IIKURA Hiroki (goalkeeper), KIMURA Takuto (midfielder) and SUWAMA Kosei (defender) — more academy graduates in the current first-team squad.
  • ENDO Keita (midfielder, Kashiwa Reysol) — promoted from the youth team, played for Union Berlin and Eintracht Braunschweig in Germany, then FC Tokyo, and joined Kashiwa in June 2026.
  • YURUKI Koya (midfielder, Kashiwa Reysol) — went straight from the youth team to Montedio Yamagata, then played for Urawa Reds and Vissel Kobe.
  • SAITO Manabu (forward) — promoted from the youth team, and named in Japan's squad for the 2014 World Cup in Brazil while a Marinos player.

Players to watch in 2026

Players worth knowing in the 2026 season:

  • MIIDERA Shin (forward/midfielder, first year) — signed a professional contract on 2 April 2026, his 16th birthday, making him the youngest professional in the club's history; first-team no. 47. A Japan under-16 international, he scored twice in a 4-1 win over Argentina at the U-16 International Dream Cup at J-Village in June 2026.
  • FUJII Shota (defender, third year, no. 4) — a Japan youth international at every level from under-15, including the FIFA U-17 World Cup in Qatar in 2025; registered with the first team (no. 54) in August 2026 and has made his first-team debut in the Levain Cup.
  • HIRANO Kanata (defender, third year, no. 2) — a Japan under-18 international in 2026, registered with the first team (no. 55) in July 2026.
  • NAKAYAMA Shuto (defender, first year, no. 33) — a 183 cm centre-back, in the Japan under-16 squad at the 2026 Dream Cup.
