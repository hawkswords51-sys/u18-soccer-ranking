Shizuoka Gakuen High School

Shizuoka Gakuen High School, known in Japan simply as 静学 (Shizugaku), is a private school in Shizuoka City. Shizuoka prefecture has long called itself the "football kingdom" of Japan, and Shizuoka Gakuen has stood apart there for fifty years by doing one thing differently: it builds its teams on individual technique, dribbling and short passing, the style Japanese football writers call "Latin" or Brazilian. It has won the winter championship, the country's biggest high-school competition, twice, in 1995 and 2019. In August 2026 it won the summer Inter-High for the first time. The team plays in Prince League Tokai in 2026 after four seasons in the national U-18 Premier League.

Shizuoka · founded 1967 · head coach KAWAGUCHI Osamu

2026 season

Currently 1st in the Prince League Tokai with 33 points from 13 matches (10W 3D 0L, 44-12). Updated daily.

Playing style

The ball is kept on the ground and in the feet. Shizuoka Gakuen players are expected to receive under pressure, beat a man in a tight space and keep the ball rather than play long. The team's own stated philosophy is to draw out each player's individuality to the maximum and to be aggressive both with and without the ball. The 2026 Inter-High team was also resilient. It went through a penalty shoot-out in the third round and won the final with the last play of the match.

History

  • 1967 — The football club is founded, a few years after the school itself.
  • Early 1970s — A new head coach, a former banker who had played at Keio University, decides that the only way to beat Shizuoka's established schools is to play differently. He adopts a South American style built on individual skill, and it becomes the school's identity.
  • 1976 — The winter championship final, lost to Urawa Minami. A technical, dribbling school side reaching the final is a shock in an era dominated by running and physical strength.
  • 1982 — MIURA Kazuyoshi, later the most famous Japanese footballer of his generation, enrols at the school. He leaves after eight months to go to Brazil at 15.
  • 1995 — The 74th winter championship ends with Shizuoka Gakuen and Kagoshima Jitsugyo sharing the title, the school's first national championship.
  • 2009 — KAWAGUCHI Osamu, a former player of the school who later studied football in Brazil, succeeds his old coach as head coach.
  • 2019 — The 98th winter championship final at Saitama Stadium, in front of more than 50,000 people. Aomori Yamada lead 2-0. NAKATANI Soshin scores just before half-time, KANO Haru equalises and NAKATANI scores the winner five minutes from the end.
  • 2021 — Prince League Tokai champions. The promotion play-off in December takes them back to the Premier League for the first time since 2013.
  • 2022–2025 — Four seasons in the Premier League WEST, with third place in 2023. They are relegated at the end of 2025.
  • 2026 — Shizuoka champions again in June, then Inter-High winners on 1 August at J-Village in Fukushima. They beat Oita 8-0, Kashima Gakuen on penalties after a 2-2 draw, Asahikawa Jitsugyo 3-2, Ohzu 1-0 and Kindai University High School 2-1 in the final. They are also top of Prince League Tokai going into the autumn, still unbeaten.

Development pathway

Three things explain the record.

  • Technique first, for decades. The school decided in the 1970s that skill on the ball would come before everything else, and it has not changed course since. That continuity is rare in Japanese school football, where teams often rebuild around whatever a new coach or a new generation prefers. Coaching has also stayed in the family: the current head coach played under his predecessor.
  • A junior high school feeding the senior team. Shizuoka Gakuen runs a junior high school team, so players can learn the same style from 12. OSHIMA Ryota and HASEGAWA Tatsuya both came through it. So did nine of the 28 players registered for the 2026 Inter-High, including the first-choice goalkeeper.
  • A national draw. The style attracts players from across Japan. The 2026 Inter-High squad included players from Osaka, Kanagawa, Aichi, Hiroshima and Nara, as well as former members of the Shimizu S-Pulse, FC Tokyo, Sanfrecce Hiroshima and Shonan Bellmare junior teams. Graduates have gone straight into the J.League, through university first, and abroad.

Former players

  • HATATE Reo (midfielder, Burnley) — a graduate of this school and Juntendo University who joined Kawasaki Frontale and then Celtic, where he won league titles. He is a Japan international, and he moved to Burnley in the English Championship in September 2026.
  • OSHIMA Ryota (midfielder, Kawasaki Frontale) — came through Shizuoka Gakuen's junior high and high school teams. He has spent his whole professional career at Kawasaki, wears the number 10 and has been capped by Japan.
  • SEKINE Hiroki (defender, Holstein Kiel, on loan from Stade de Reims) — went from this school to Takushoku University and Kashiwa Reysol, and then to France in 2025. He is a Japan international and joined the German second-division club on loan in 2026.
  • MATSUMURA Yuta (midfielder, Kashima Antlers) — joined Kashima straight from school in 2020 and returned in 2025 after a loan at Tokyo Verdy.
  • HASEGAWA Tatsuya (midfielder, Hokkaido Consadole Sapporo) — also came through the junior high and high school teams, then Juntendo University and Kawasaki Frontale.
  • KANDA Soma (forward, Tokyo Verdy, on loan from Kawasaki Frontale) — one of the more recent graduates to go straight into the J.League.
  • MIURA Kazuyoshi (forward, Fukushima United, on loan from Yokohama FC) — he spent only eight months at the school before leaving for Brazil in 1982. He is still playing in his fifties, and the school's link with Brazilian football began with players like him.
