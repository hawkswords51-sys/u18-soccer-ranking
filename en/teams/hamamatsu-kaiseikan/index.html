Hamamatsu Kaiseikan High School

Hamamatsu Kaiseikan High School (浜松開誠館) is a private school in Hamamatsu, in the west of Shizuoka Prefecture, with an attached junior high school. Shizuoka is often called Japan's "football kingdom", and for decades its school football was dominated by famous sides from the centre and east of the prefecture, such as Shizuoka Gakuen High School, Fujieda Higashi and Shimizu Higashi. Kaiseikan is the newcomer that broke into that group. Its football club started with a junior-high team in 2002 and added a high-school team in 2005. The high-school side reached the national winter championship for the first time in 2018, won the Prince League Tokai in 2022, and in 2025 won both of Shizuoka's qualifying tournaments, for the summer Inter-High and for the winter championship. In 2026 the team plays in the Prince League Tokai and was third after 13 matches (six wins, five draws, two defeats, 23 points). It did not reach the 2026 Inter-High, losing the Shizuoka final to Shizuoka Gakuen.

Playing style

The club built its reputation on hard work and intensity. Its motto translates roughly as "fight, run, hold on", meaning physical commitment, work rate and refusing to give up as much as individual skill. The 2026 results fit that description: the team is difficult to beat, and its league defeats have been narrow ones. In June it held Shizuoka Gakuen 1-1 in the league. The same month it reached two finals against the same opponent: it lost the Shizuoka Inter-High final 1-2, and lost the regional Tokai Championship final on penalties (1-1, 3-4) after beating Toyokawa 4-2 and Yokkaichi Chuo Kogyo 6-1. Shizuoka Gakuen went on to win the national Inter-High, so those were close contests against the eventual champions. The season brought a big change off the pitch as well. On 12 June 2026 the club announced that the founding head coach was leaving after 25 years, to become a first-team coach at Shonan Bellmare in J2. The coach of the junior-high team took over the high-school side. Since then Kaiseikan has kept its place near the top of the league table, finishing September with a 4-1 win away to Aikodai Meiden. Neither coach has an official English spelling of his name, so neither is named on this page.

History

  • 2002 — The school forms a junior-high football team. It becomes the base of the club, and many players later stay for six years, through junior high and high school.
  • 2004 — The junior-high team wins the national junior-high championship, only its third year in existence.
  • 2005 — The high-school team is founded.
  • 2009 — Midfielder TAKEUCHI Ryo joins Shimizu S-Pulse straight from the school.
  • 2015 — Left-back MATSUBARA Ko also joins Shimizu S-Pulse directly from the school.
  • 2018–19 — The club's first national winter championship. In the Shizuoka final, Kaiseikan beats Shizuoka Gakuen 2-1. According to Gekisaka, it is the first team from western Shizuoka to qualify in 41 years. At the national finals it goes out 0-1 in its opening match, in the second round, against Nagasaki Institute of Applied Science High School. A first-year forward, KUMATORIYA Issei, is in that team.
  • 2022 — The breakthrough year. Kaiseikan wins the Prince League Tokai for the first time. In the play-off for promotion to the national Premier League it beats Sapporo Otani 2-1 but loses the deciding match 0-2 to Shohei. It wins the Shizuoka winter qualifier, beating Fujieda Higashi 2-0 in the final, and at the national finals it goes out to Ohzu in the second round, on penalties (1-1, 3-4). The junior-high team wins the national title again, 18 years after the first.
  • 2025 — Shizuoka double. Kaiseikan wins the Shizuoka Inter-High qualifier for the first time, beating Fujieda Higashi 1-0 in the final. At its first Inter-High it beats Ritsumeikan Moriyama 7-0 and then loses 0-2 to Yamanashi Gakuin High School in the third round. It finishes third in the Prince League Tokai. In the autumn it also wins the winter qualifier, beating Fujieda Higashi on penalties (0-0, 5-4). At the national finals it beats Kyushu Bunka Gakuen 2-0, then goes out to Kokoku on penalties after a 1-1 draw.
  • 2026 — Runners-up to Shizuoka Gakuen in both the Shizuoka Inter-High qualifier and the Tokai Championship. The founding head coach leaves in June to join Shonan Bellmare's staff. In September, goalkeeper MATSUURA Jin Biera's move to Shimizu S-Pulse for 2027 is announced.

Development pathway

For readers new to Japanese youth football: high-school teams and J.League academies play in the same national league pyramid. At the top are the U-18 Premier League (EAST and WEST), then nine regional Prince Leagues, then the prefectural leagues. The Prince League Tokai covers Shizuoka, Aichi, Gifu and Mie. Two high-school tournaments carry even more prestige than the league: the summer Inter-High and the winter championship, which is televised nationally and ends in January. Shizuoka normally sends only one team to each, so every qualifier is a knockout fight between some of the strongest schools in the country. Japanese school years run from April to March, so a winter championship that starts in late December 2025 belongs to the 2025 school year.

  • A six-year programme. The junior-high team came first, and the club is built around players staying from age 12 to 18. Several alumni now in the J.League came through the school's junior-high team, among them TAKEUCHI Ryo, KUMATORIYA Issei, YOSHIDA Manato and SUGANUMA Kazuaki. The junior-high team's two national titles (2004 and 2022) show how strong that base is.
  • A home for players from J.League academies. Shizuoka is home to the academies of Júbilo Iwata and Shimizu S-Pulse, among others. Kaiseikan has given academy players a different route. MATSUBARA Ko came from Júbilo Iwata's junior-youth team and HAKAMATA Yutaro from Júbilo Iwata U-15. Both became professionals, and MATSUBARA is now back at Iwata.
  • Straight to the pros, or via university. Some players have joined J.League clubs directly from the school (TAKEUCHI Ryo, MATSUBARA Ko, and MATSUURA Jin Biera for 2027). More have gone through university football first. Meiji University is the best-trodden path (HAKAMATA Yutaro, SUGAI Hidehiro, KUMATORIYA Issei), and others went to Kokushikan, Fukuoka University and the National Institute of Fitness and Sports in Kanoya.
  • Defenders and wide players. Several of the best-known alumni are full-backs, which fits the club's emphasis on running and intensity.

Former players

  • MATSUBARA Ko (defender, Júbilo Iwata) — a left-back who joined Shimizu S-Pulse straight from the school in 2015. He moved to Sint-Truiden in Belgium and has since come home to Júbilo Iwata, whose junior-youth team he played for before Kaiseikan.
  • SUGAI Hidehiro (defender, Shimizu S-Pulse) — went to Meiji University after the school, then played for Ventforet Kofu, Kashima Antlers and Kyoto Sanga before joining Shimizu.
  • HAKAMATA Yutaro (defender, Shonan Bellmare) — another Meiji University graduate. He played for Yokohama FC, Júbilo Iwata, Omiya Ardija, Roasso Kumamoto and Tokyo Verdy before moving to Shonan.
  • TAKEUCHI Ryo (midfielder, Fagiano Okayama) — came through the school's junior-high team and joined Shimizu S-Pulse from the high school in 2009. He spent most of his career at Shimizu, apart from a loan spell at Giravanz Kitakyushu, before moving to Okayama.
  • YUBA Kenshin (midfielder, Kashiwa Reysol) — a member of the 2018–19 winter championship team. He went to Kokushikan University, then played for Honda FC, FC Imabari and Sagan Tosu before joining Kashiwa.
  • YOSHIDA Manato (Sagan Tosu) — came through the school's junior-high team and went on to the National Institute of Fitness and Sports in Kanoya. While still a student he was named in Japan's U-22 squad for the 2022 Asian Games, held in Hangzhou in 2023. He then joined Yokohama F. Marinos and had several loan spells at Oita Trinita before moving to Sagan Tosu.
  • KUMATORIYA Issei (Tokyo Verdy) — played at the 2018–19 winter championship as a first-year and later went to Meiji University. He has played for Tokyo Verdy since 2025.
  • SUGANUMA Kazuaki (goalkeeper, FC Gifu) — came through the school's junior-high team, went to Fukuoka University and played for Avispa Fukuoka before joining FC Gifu.
  • KATO Taisei (Iwaki FC) — joined the school from a junior club in Nara, went to the National Institute of Fitness and Sports in Kanoya, and now plays for Iwaki FC in J2.

Players to watch in 2026

  • MATSUURA Jin Biera (goalkeeper, third year) — on 10 September 2026 Shimizu S-Pulse announced that he will join the club in January 2027. Born in Iwata, he said he was "incredibly happy" to begin his professional career in his home prefecture.
