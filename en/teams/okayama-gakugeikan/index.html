Okayama Gakugeikan High School

Okayama Gakugeikan High School (岡山学芸館, usually shortened to "Gakugeikan") is a private school in Higashi Ward, Okayama City, in western Japan. For most of its history the football club lived in the shadow of better-known neighbours, but in January 2023 it became the first team from Okayama Prefecture ever to win the All Japan High School Soccer Tournament, the televised winter championship that is the biggest event in Japanese school football. It beat Higashiyama 3-1 in the final at the National Stadium in Tokyo. Since then Gakugeikan has been the dominant school side in its prefecture, winning the Okayama qualifying tournament for the winter championship five years in a row. In 2026 it plays in the Prince League Chugoku, the regional league for the five prefectures of western Honshu, and after 14 matches it was top of the table with 11 wins, two draws and one defeat (35 points). It also won the Okayama Inter-High qualifiers for a second year running, but lost its opening match at the national finals.

Playing style

The club describes its football in terms of speed and coordination. In its official introduction for the 2024 Premier League play-off it spoke of "speedy pass football that exploits the gaps in the opponent's defence", combined with "coordinated pressing to win the ball back". Two years earlier its stated aim was to break the opponent's press and play a possession game that moves the ball forward quickly. In practice, Gakugeikan teams are known for being hard to beat in close matches: four of its six matches in the 2022–23 championship run were won by a single goal or on penalties.

The head coach, a former player who was part of the early years of Fagiano Okayama, the city's professional club, has led the team since 2008. He is not named here because no official English spelling of his name has been published.

History

  • 2008 — The current head coach takes charge after retiring as a player.
  • 2018 — The club reaches the last 16 of the winter championship.
  • 2019 — Champions of the Prince League Chugoku for the first time, and a first appearance in the play-off for promotion to the Premier League.
  • 2021 — Quarter-finalists at the summer Inter-High.
  • 2022 — A second consecutive Inter-High quarter-final, then the best season in the club's history. The team finishes second in the Prince League Chugoku and goes to the Premier League play-off again.
  • 2022–23 — The 101st winter championship. Gakugeikan beats Teikyo Kani 1-0 and Kashima Gakuen High School 3-2, then draws 0-0 with Kokugakuin Kugayama and wins the shoot-out 5-3 to reach the quarter-finals for the first time. It beats Sano Nichidai 4-0, and in the semi-final at the National Stadium it draws 3-3 with Kamimura Gakuen before winning on penalties. In the final on 9 January 2023 it falls behind to Higashiyama, but an own goal and two goals from its midfielder in the second half (52nd and 85th minutes) give a 3-1 win and the title.
  • 2023 — Runners-up in the Prince League Chugoku, and the last 16 at both the Inter-High and the winter championship.
  • 2024 — Champions of the Prince League Chugoku for the second time. In the Premier League play-off in December, Gakugeikan wins its first match, the only high-school team to do so that day, but loses 2-3 to Avispa Fukuoka U-18 to a goal in stoppage time and stays in the Prince League. At the 103rd winter championship it loses 1-2 to Yaita Chuo High School in the first round, its goal coming in stoppage time.
  • 2025 — Okayama Inter-High champions after a final against Tamano Konan, and last 16 at the national Inter-High. Its fifth winter championship in a row ends in its first match, a 0-2 defeat by Nihon University Fujisawa in the second round.
  • 2026 — Gakugeikan wins the Okayama Inter-High qualifiers again, beating Sakuyo Gakuen 1-0 in the final on 13 June with a goal from a centre-back in the 52nd minute. At the national finals in Fukushima it plays in the opening round on 25 July and loses 2-3 to Toin Gakuen High School. In the league it leads the Prince League Chugoku after 14 rounds, with its eyes on a third play-off for the Premier League.

Development pathway

For readers new to Japanese youth football: high-school teams and J.League academies play in the same national league pyramid. At the top is the U-18 Premier League (EAST and WEST). Below it are nine regional Prince Leagues, and below those the prefectural leagues. The champions of each Prince League do not go up automatically: they enter a play-off in December with the other strongest regional teams, and only four places are available. Alongside the league, two school tournaments carry even more prestige. The summer Inter-High is held in a different region each year. The winter championship begins at the end of December and finishes in January at the National Stadium, which is why a title is dated across two years (2022–23). Japanese school years run from April to March.

  • A title won outside the traditional strongholds. Okayama has never been one of the heartlands of Japanese school football, and before 2023 no team from the prefecture had won the winter championship. Gakugeikan's title showed that a school from a smaller football region can beat the best in the country, and its five straight winter qualifications since then show it was not a one-off.
  • A rivalry for the prefecture. Gakugeikan's main opponent in Okayama is Sakuyo Gakuen (formerly Sakuyo High School), historically the prefecture's best-known football school. The 2026 Inter-High final between them was settled by a single goal.
  • Regional league football every week. Unlike many school teams, which spend most of the year in prefectural leagues, Gakugeikan has been a Prince League Chugoku club since at least 2019 and has finished first or second four times. This gives its players a full season of matches against strong school sides and J.League academy teams.

Former players

Several members of the 2022–23 championship team, including the two-goal scorer in the final, went on to university football in the Kanto region. As of late September 2026 none of them has been announced as joining a J.League club, and no official English spelling of their names has been published, so they are not named here. No football-club graduate of the school with an officially published English name is currently playing in the J.League.

Note for readers checking other lists: several professional players who are sometimes described as Gakugeikan old boys (for example, forwards who came through the Fagiano Okayama U-18 academy) played their youth football for the professional club's academy rather than for the school team, and are not included on this page.

Current players are not named on this page, because none of them has an English spelling published by the JFA, the J.League or a club.
