Senshu University Kitakami Senior High School

Senshu University Kitakami Senior High School (専修大学北上高校, usually shortened to "Senshu Kitakami" or, in Japanese, "Sendai Kitakami") is a private school in the city of Kitakami, in the southern half of Iwate Prefecture in Japan's north-east. It is affiliated with Senshu University in Tokyo. The football club was founded in 1964. For most of its history it sat behind Iwate's two traditional powers, the public schools Morioka Shogyo and Tono, but in 2019 it broke through, winning the prefecture's summer and winter qualifying tournaments and appearing at both national high-school tournaments for the first time. Since then it has become a regular national qualifier: four winter championships (2019–20, 2021–22, 2024–25 and 2025–26) and three Inter-Highs (2019, 2021 and 2026). In 2026 the team plays in Prince League Tohoku, the regional tier just below the national Premier League. At the 2026 Inter-High it beat Yokkaichi Chuo Kogyo 2-0 before losing 1-2 to Toko Gakuen High School in the round of 32.

Playing style

The club's motto is "There is no shortcut to glory" (栄光に近道なし). It describes a programme that values daily discipline as much as talent: many players live in the football club's own dormitory, and the squad is large, with around 120 players across the three school years organised into several teams that train most days of the week. On the pitch, Senshu Kitakami is known in Tohoku for trying to keep the ball and build attacks patiently rather than relying on long balls, and its national-tournament matches have tended to be close. Both of its exits in 2019 came on penalties after drawn matches.

The 2026 Inter-High, held at J-Village in Fukushima, was a good example of the current team. Against Yokkaichi Chuo Kogyo, a famous name from Mie and a former national champion, the first half finished goalless; a substitute forward then scored twice early in the second half to settle the match 2-0. It was the club's first win at the Inter-High since 2019. The next day it met Toko Gakuen High School, the 2019 Inter-High champions, and lost 1-2. Toko went on to reach the quarter-finals.

History

  • 1964 — The football club is founded. For decades it is a mid-ranking side in Iwate, where Morioka Shogyo and Tono, both public schools with national pedigree, dominate the prefectural tournaments.
  • 2019 — The breakthrough year. Senshu Kitakami wins the Iwate qualifying tournaments for both the summer Inter-High and the winter championship, as well as the prefectural freshmen's tournament. At the Inter-High it beats Taisha 3-2 before losing to Kyoto Tachibana on penalties. In its first winter championship it beats Ryukoku 3-1 and then holds Kokugakuin Kugayama, one of the tournament favourites, to 0-0 before losing a long penalty shoot-out 5-6.
  • 2021 — The club wins the Iwate qualifying tournaments for both national competitions again. It loses in the first round at the Inter-High, and 2-4 to Nara Ikuei in the first round of the 100th winter championship.
  • 2023 — The club is in Prince League Tohoku, finishing eighth.
  • 2024 — Fifth in Prince League Tohoku. In September, a new artificial-turf ground opens at the school, ending years of training on a dirt pitch. In November, Senshu Kitakami beats Tono on penalties (1-1, 5-4) in the Iwate final to reach the winter championship for the third time, and loses 0-2 to Kochi in the first round.
  • 2025 — Seventh in Prince League Tohoku. The club retains the Iwate winter title, coming from two goals down to beat Morioka Seio after extra time in the final, the first time it has won the prefecture's winter qualifying in consecutive years. At the 104th winter championship its captain gives the players' oath at the opening ceremony at the National Stadium in Tokyo, a speech that drew national attention for its reference to the Iwate-born baseball star now playing in the major leagues. In the first round the team loses 1-2 to Hiroshima Minami.
  • 2026 — Senshu Kitakami beats Tono 2-0 in the Iwate final to reach the Inter-High for the first time in five editions. At J-Village it beats Yokkaichi Chuo Kogyo 2-0 and loses 1-2 to Toko Gakuen High School. In the league, after 15 of 18 rounds of Prince League Tohoku, it is seventh of ten, with a 4-1 win over the leaders, Shoshi, among its results.

Development pathway

For readers new to Japanese youth football: high-school teams and J.League academies play in the same national league pyramid. At the top is the U-18 Premier League (EAST and WEST), then nine regional Prince Leagues, then the prefectural leagues. Senshu Kitakami plays in the Tohoku region's Prince League, alongside school sides such as Shoshi and Aomori Yamada's second team and academies such as those of Montedio Yamagata and Blaublitz Akita. Two school tournaments carry even more prestige than the league: the summer Inter-High, and the winter All Japan High School Soccer Tournament, which is televised nationally and ends at the National Stadium in January. Each prefecture sends its own qualifier to both, so a club's record is often counted in "appearances". Because the Japanese school year begins in April, a winter tournament is usually dated by its starting year (2025–26 is the "2025" edition).

  • Breaking a two-team monopoly. Iwate is a prefecture where two public schools long took almost every national place. Senshu Kitakami's rise since 2019 has turned that into a three-way contest; the 2024 and 2026 prefectural finals were both against Tono.
  • A boarding programme. The football club has its own dormitory, and players come both from Iwate and from further afield, including junior clubs in the Kanto region around Tokyo.
  • New facilities. The school's artificial-turf ground opened in September 2024, and the club also trains at the artificial pitch of the Kitakami municipal sports park.
  • A route to university football. The club lists graduates who have gone on to play at Senshu University, Waseda University and other university programmes around Japan.
  • A school with two strong football teams. Senshu Kitakami's girls' team is also one of the strongest in Iwate and regularly represents the prefecture at the national women's high-school championship. This page covers the boys' team.

Former players

The boys' football club has not yet produced a J.League player, and no former player of the club currently appears on a J.League squad. Its graduates have mostly continued at university level. Because none of them has an English spelling published by the JFA, the J.League or a club, no alumni are named on this page.

Current players are not named either. None of the 2026 squad has been called up to a Japan youth national team or announced as a J.League signing, so no official English spellings of their names exist.
