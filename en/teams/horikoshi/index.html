Horikoshi High School

Horikoshi High School (堀越高校) is a private school in Nakano, a ward of central Tokyo. Its football team trains at the school's sports ground in Hachioji, in the western suburbs of Tokyo. The club was founded in 1982 and is best known for a way of working that is rare in Japanese school football: since 2012, the players themselves decide the training, the starting line-up, the formation and tactics, and the substitutions. Horikoshi has qualified eight times for the winter All Japan High School Soccer Tournament, counting the 2026–27 edition, and four times for the summer Inter-High. Its best result is a place in the semi-finals of the winter championship in 2023–24. In 2026 the team plays in Tokyo's top prefectural league, the T1 League. On 15 November 2026 it beat Komazawa University High School 1-0 in the final of the Tokyo A Block qualifying tournament, winning the Tokyo title for the fourth year running. No Tokyo school had done that since Teikyo High School 36 years earlier.

Playing style

The club calls its method "bottom-up". It began in spring 2012, when the coaching staff handed the running of the team to the captain of the day. The idea came from Hiroshima Kannon High School, which had won the national Inter-High with a team run by its players. At Horikoshi the players plan the daily training, pick the team, choose the formation and how to play, and make the substitutions. The aim is for players to think for themselves, so that they keep improving after they leave school. In 2026 there has been one change: the coach, a former Horikoshi goalkeeper who has led the team since 2014, has said that he now makes the substitutions himself.

On the pitch the team has won its biggest matches with organised defending as much as with attacking football. In the 2026 Tokyo qualifiers it kept clean sheets in the quarter-final and the final.

History

  • 1982 — The club is founded in April 1982 by thirteen first-year students.
  • 1989–90 and 1991–92 — Horikoshi reaches the winter championship for the first time in 1989–90 and wins one match. It returns in 1991–92 and reaches the last 16.
  • 2012 — The players take over the running of the team, starting the "bottom-up" method.
  • 2014 and 2015 — Horikoshi reaches the Tokyo winter final in both years and loses both, to Mitaka and then to Komazawa University High School.
  • 2017 — The ground in Hachioji is resurfaced with artificial turf.
  • 2020–21 — Back at the winter championship for the first time in 29 years. Horikoshi beats Taisha High School on penalties after a 1-1 draw and Maruoka High School 2-0, then loses 0-4 to Aomori Yamada High School in the quarter-final, the school's first national quarter-final. The captain that year is HINO Shota, and defender INOUE Taisei is named in the team of the tournament.
  • 2021–22 — Horikoshi beats Kochi High School 2-1, then loses 0-1 to Nagasaki Institute of Applied Science High School.
  • 2023–24 — The best run in the club's history. Horikoshi beats Hatsushiba Hashimoto High School 8-7 on penalties after a 0-0 draw, Meioh High School 1-0 and Saga Higashi High School 2-1, then loses 1-3 to Omi High School in the semi-final at the National Stadium in Tokyo.
  • 2024–25 — Horikoshi beats Tsu Kogyo 2-0 and Matsuyama Kita 6-1, then loses 0-1 in the quarter-final to Maebashi Ikuei High School, who go on to win the tournament. A second-year forward scores five goals and finishes as the tournament's top scorer. In December the team plays in the play-offs for the Prince League Kanto Division 2, beating Tokiwa 3-0 before losing 1-4 to Nippon Sport Science University Kashiwa High School.
  • 2025–26 — Horikoshi wins the Tokyo qualifiers for the Kanto High School Tournament. In the winter qualifiers it beats Teikyo High School in the semi-final, its first win over Teikyo in the winter qualifiers, and Kokugakuin University Kugayama High School 7-2 in the final, becoming Tokyo champions for the third year running for the first time. At the national finals it beats Ujiyamada Shogyo 9-0 and then loses 1-4 to Kashima Gakuen High School in the third round.
  • 2026 — In the Inter-High qualifiers Horikoshi loses 0-1 to Tokai University Takanawadai in the second round of the final stage. In the winter qualifiers it beats Soka 4-1 after conceding early, Seiritsu Gakuen High School 2-0, the Tokyo Inter-High representatives, and Tokyo Korean High School 6-1. In the final on 15 November it beats Komazawa University High School 1-0 with a second-half goal, qualifying for the national finals (from 28 December 2026) for the fourth year running and the eighth time in all.

Development pathway

For readers new to Japanese youth football: high-school teams and J.League academies play in the same national league pyramid. At the top is the U-18 Premier League (EAST and WEST), then nine regional Prince Leagues, then the prefectural leagues; in Tokyo the top prefectural division is called the T1 League. Two high-school tournaments carry even more prestige than the league: the summer Inter-High and the winter championship, which is televised nationally and ends at the National Stadium in Tokyo in January. Tokyo is so large that it sends two teams to the winter championship, the winners of an A Block and a B Block. Japanese seasons follow the school year, so a winter championship such as "2023–24" starts in late December and finishes in January.

  • Players in charge. In most Japanese school teams the coach decides everything. At Horikoshi the players decide most things themselves, including who plays. The club believes this develops players who can think for themselves, which helps them later at university and in the professional game.
  • A ground in the suburbs. The school's main building is in central Tokyo, but students on the sports course take their lessons at the school's centre in Hachioji and train on the ground next door. The artificial pitch is 105 m by 68 m, with a shock-absorbing pad under the turf and a 400 m running track around it. The ground opened in 1985 and was resurfaced with artificial turf in 2017.
  • Through university to the J.League. Horikoshi's best-known recent graduates turned professional after university. INOUE Taisei went via Juntendo University, HINO Shota via Takushoku University and HIGAKURE Nash Taishi via SANNO University.

Former players

  • INOUE Taisei (defender, Yokohama F. Marinos) — at the school from 2018 to 2020 and named in the team of the tournament at the 2020–21 winter championship. After Juntendo University he played for Sagan Tosu and joined Yokohama F. Marinos in December 2025.
  • HINO Shota (midfielder, Ehime FC) — captain of the 2020–21 team that reached the national quarter-finals. While at Takushoku University he played for Japan's under-22 team at the 2023 Asian Games. He played for Sagan Tosu and moved to Ehime FC in December 2025.
  • HIGAKURE Nash Taishi (midfielder, SANNO University) — at the school from 2020 to 2022. He will join RB Omiya Ardija in 2027 and is registered with the club as a special designated player in 2026, which allows a university student to play J.League matches.

Several other graduates have played professionally, including a goalkeeper who was named the most valuable player of the 2005 J.League Cup final. Their names are not romanised here because no English spelling has been published by the JFA, the J.League or a club. No Horikoshi graduate was in Japan's squad for the 2026 World Cup.
