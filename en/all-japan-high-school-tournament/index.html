All Japan High School Soccer Tournament 2026–27

The All Japan High School Soccer Tournament is the biggest event in Japanese youth football. Known at home simply as Senshuken ("the championship"), it brings 48 schools — one from each prefecture and two from Tokyo — to a knockout tournament around Tokyo over the New Year, with the final at the National Stadium. Qualifying runs through the autumn in all 47 prefectures, and this page follows every one of them, updated daily. As of 7 October 2026, results are in from 30 of the 47 prefectures and none of the 48 places has been decided yet.

28 December 2026 – 11 January 2027 · Japan National Stadium (MUFG Stadium) and other stadiums in the Tokyo area · 48 schools, knockout

What the tournament is

First played in 1918, the tournament is older than the J.League by three-quarters of a century. It has been organised by the Japan Football Association together with 43 commercial TV broadcasters, so every prefecture's qualifier has its own local coverage and the later rounds are shown nationwide.

Only school teams can enter. The U-18 academies of J.League clubs — which play against schools every week in the Premier and Prince Leagues — are not eligible; they have a separate Club Youth Championship. That makes this the one national stage where school football is on its own.

For third-year students it is the last tournament of their school career. Many of them have already agreed to join a J.League club or a university team in April.

The 2026–27 tournament

Key facts
Dates28 December 2026 – 11 January 2027
VenuesJapan National Stadium (MUFG Stadium) and other stadiums in the Tokyo area
Teams48 — one per prefecture, two from Tokyo
FormatSingle-elimination knockout

In the 2025–26 edition, matches up to the quarter-finals lasted 80 minutes and the semi-finals and final 90 minutes. Only the final had extra time; every other drawn match went straight to a penalty shoot-out. The draw and the venue for each round are announced by the organisers later in the autumn.

Storylines

  • The holders: Kamimura Gakuen (Kagoshima) beat Kashima Gakuen 3-0 in the 2025–26 final to win the tournament for the first time, completing a summer–winter double with the Inter-High.
  • The summer champions: Shizuoka Gakuen won the 2026 Inter-High. They have won this tournament twice, in 1995–96 (shared) and 2019–20.
  • The most successful school of the last decade: Aomori Yamada, with four titles since 2016–17.

Qualifying in all 47 prefectures

Each prefecture runs its own knockout qualifier, mostly between August and mid-November. Only the winner goes through — in Tokyo, the winners of two separate blocks. Large prefectures such as Tokyo, Kanagawa and Chiba play more than 100 qualifying matches, starting with a preliminary stage, while the strongest teams are seeded into the later rounds.

Even the schools in the national Premier League have to win their prefecture first, and every year some of them are knocked out by local rivals.
